Running the Game

Traits

Gear, spells, and creatures can have Traits - special properties that give unique capabilities or limitations. Think of them as Roles or Maneuvers for non-player stuff, mechanical shortcuts that keep your game flowing without demanding lengthy explanations.

Conceits

Conceits are unrated Traits - narrative truths that can't be measured but are absolutely true within the game world. They won't have numbers attached, same as for player characters. A demon might be Immune to Fire (unrated) rather than Resistant +3 (rated).

Compatible Traits stack normally. Resistant +2 and Protected +1 both apply to the same defensive situation (if at different points) because they expand the range of things that cannot affect the target.

Many creatures and some items will get a "Role" that is just whatever they are — "Bear", for example, or "Sylph" for a type of air elemental. They can then get Maneuvers relevant to that. Special items might get dedicated Maneuver-like bonuses in specific situations, such as Sharp for a particularly dangerous edge, or Flaming that ignites fuel and lights fires in its combat power.

This will cover a lot, but some things don't really fit well in that framework, and we will use Traits to round out the edges. In a lot of cases, Traits are just a quick and concise descriptions that communicate the relevant narrative for things that happen pretty often so the GM can create quick and concise descriptions that communicate the relevant narrative — for a ghost that is Stationary it means it's haunting a spot and won't chase the characters outside that area.

Note that a list of "Traits" don't require actual writeups; they are just descriptive labels. Stationary is perfectly ordinary. There's nothing special about it. We're not even going to include it in the list. It just means the thing is... well, stationary.

Some ideas are worth repeatable labels, though. When the GM scribbles "inexorable" on a sticky note, players immediately know they are in trouble.

Behavioral Traits

Some creatures or objects act in predictable patterns that affect how players can interact with them.

Reactive - Only acts when triggered. Obviously this applies to a classic trap or magical ward that lies dormant until someone steps on the wrong stone, but it could also be a ghost that just won't notice you at all until you do that one thing... A herd of megafauna that have never seen humans may not fear you enough to herd one enough for it to call out, the rest may not fear you enough for it to call out, the rest ALL respond.

Ablative - Degrades with use rather than having discrete charges. A magic wand might hold 10 points of energy to be spent in any combination, losing one potency with each roll regardless of the result. Ablative things usually have a protection rating too.

Combat Traits

These modify how attacks and defenses work in ways that can dramatically alter tactical situations.

Inexorable - Cannot make Resistance rolls! This is extremely dangerous and should be commensurately rare and moderated. You should absolutely have a themed domain in which it applies, and you should absolutely use it when it's relevant and appropriate, but there's nothing special about it. A student casting a psychic ward might be inexorable to the average wizard but moderated to the Mage. Creatures that are inexorable can be countered some other way; a patient casting a psychic might be inexorable to the average Mage but a Healer might be able to use an antidote.

Insidious - Independent, only resisted. The attack bypasses active defense entirely. You can't use a sword to fight a poison already in your system, though a Healer might be able to use an antidote.

Subtle - effect is not obvious to anyone unable to perceive the Borderlands or the Spirit Realm.

Contagious - Spawns a copy of itself on any victim of a successful attack. Yes, this is as terrifying as it sounds. While this obviously applies to diseases, it could also be used for fire, especially if the characters have oil spilled on them...

Constrained - Limited to specific dice when making rolls. This is commonly used to indicate a lack of finesse. A forest ogre might be terrifyingly strong, but never roll anything but a d12.

Area Effect Traits

Note: Geometric shapes like cones or bursts are handled through narrative description. The mechanical distinction is about targeting control, not drawing geometry.

Area Effect: Targetable - Caster can use splitting to divide up the EL among different targets. (This is the default.)

Area Effect: Blanket - One roll applies equally to everyone and everything in the affected area. No splitting allowed.

Area Effect: Selective - Affects only targets meeting specific criteria: those wearing metal, weapons, whatever the effect specifies. The method of selection should be specified in the spell — "only enemies" isn't something a fire could determine.

Defensive and Vulnerability Traits

Some things are tougher than expected - others have glaring weaknesses.

Immune - Completely unaffected by the specified attack type. Please allow alternative approaches — invulnerable enemies are boring enemies. How it applies is fully up to the description.

Hardened - Affected by things that wouldn't normally matter: cold iron against fae, salt circles against spirits, sunlight against vampires. How it applies is fully up to the description.

Susceptible - Provides EL for environmental exposure to the specified type, or adds to active attacks using that type. The GM determines appropriate Traits (Insidious, Inexorable, etc.) based on context.

Protected - Rating adds to Resisted effect after a successful Resistance roll.

Worsened - Rating always adds directly to incoming effect of the specified type.

Resistant - Rating adds to EL of resistance rolls against the specified effect type.

Sensitive - Rating adds to the EL of the relevant type targeting this entity.

Spell-Specific Traits

Magic effects in many ways that need mechanical precision.

Difficult - Requires a Difficulty Level roll to cast successfully. If casting succeeds, the DL usually becomes points of Inexorable effect that bypass Normal Resistance. If a spell has DL 0 it can be cast without a roll for "nominal" use, but "Difficult" spells or any actual effect always requires a roll to resist. Inexorable points are taken from the first points of rolled effect, and resistance is applied to any that remain after effect; so that if all remaining points of Inexorable potential is lost.

Immutable - the spell erases itself from the caster's memory when bound, and cannot be cast any other way. Old school and unforgiving. Some settings automatically apply this to ALL spells; the group may decide for themselves whether or not it still counts as DL reduction. Commonly used with Immutable, but not required (unless, again, ALL spells are both.)

Persistent - Trivial Effect continues beyond the initial casting without requiring concentration. For example - Light cast for simple reading or navigation, rather than as an attack. Persistent spells need no special effort to maintain, and can stay active while casting other spells. They cannot hold an effect against someone else's roll — they might not hold the spell active so that the caster can use it for new rolls.

Guidable - Caster can continue directing the effect after casting as they maintain line of sight.

Status - NOT accumulating. When cast again, replace the previous value rather than stacking.

Creating New Traits

Players and GMs should create new Traits when needed, but resist the urge to make everything a Trait. Many effects that might seem like they need special mechanics are better handled through simple description.

A creature that can't move is just described as "Stationary". Flying, burrowing, blindness, alternate senses — all of these work perfectly fine as narrative/descriptive elements without needing formal mechanical shortcuts.
