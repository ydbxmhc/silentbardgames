#PROPOSAL: "Tricks" as a default part of character creation (L1)

**Status:PROPOSED - draft for author review.** Not yet placed in the rulebook.
Draftedfrom the western demo-character discussion (Joe Hardesty, Wes "The Deacon"
Crane),where every character's "Boosts & Tricks" turned out to be the same kind of
thingand clarified the character sharply without adding any new crunch.

---

##The insight

Acrossthe demo sheets, the "Tricks" we wrote for each character were never new
powers.They were **personality traits that state how a character habitually uses
theexisting rules to gain an advantage in a specific, recurring situation.** They
costnothing because they invent nothing - they only describe *how* this particular
persontends to apply the Boosts, Wagers, splits, and free actions they already have.

Namingthat habit does real work: it makes the character clearer in play, gives the
GMa handle, and turns "what can I do here?" into "here's the thing I always do."
It'sthe same design payoff as a Hook, on the method side instead of the motive side.

##Proposed rule text (rulebook voice - edit freely)

>### Tricks - Your Signature Moves
>
>A **Hook** says what your character cares about. A **Trick** says what they
>habitually *do about it*. Where a Hook is a motive, a Trick is a method: a named,
>agreed way you lean on the rules you already have, in a situation you keep finding
>yourself in.
>
>A Trick is not a new power and costs no Karma. It invents nothing - it only
>declares, in advance, how you tend to apply the tools already on your sheet: a
>Boost you always set up, a Wager you habitually make, a cross-Role combination that
>fits your style, a free action your manner makes possible. The Deacon doesn't get a
>new ability from *No Fear*; he's just the kind of man who holds your eyes and
>smiles while the guns come out, and that steadiness is a Boost he can take for free
>because it costs him no effort to be himself.
>
>Because a Trick only reorganizes what you can already do, the table can hand them
>out freely at character creation - none are required, and two or three to start is
>typical - and more can surface in play as a character reveals who they are. Like
>Hooks, they are subject to the GM's approval: a good Trick is *specific to a
>circumstance* and uses *only abilities the character actually has*. If a "Trick"
>would let you do something your Roles, Level, and Maneuvers can't already justify,
>it isn't a Trick - it's a power, and it costs Karma or it doesn't exist.
>
>**Tricks do not rank, and carry no number of their own.** A Trick is just the habit
>the GM uses to justify letting a bonus apply in a fitting moment; the size of that
>bonus always comes from the character's *own* roll and owned abilities - their EL, a
>Boost they set up, a Wager they make. The instant a Trick would want a rank of its
>own, it has become a Maneuver, and is bought and capped like one.
>
>A strong Trick has three marks:
>- **It comes from who they are.** It reads as a personality trait, not a build choice.
>- **It names the exact mechanic and trigger.** State the rule it invokes - a specific
>  Boost, a Wager (and what the points are dedicated to), a split, a free action, a
>  cross-Role combination - and the precise situation that switches it on. "A habit" is
>  never enough on its own; even a pure habit has to say exactly how it cashes out in
>  the rules and when. The advantage is always opportunity-specific, and always paid
>  for out of the character's own EL and abilities.
>- **It's situational.** It applies when the moment fits, not always and everywhere.

##Worked examples (from the demos)

-**No Fear** (Boost, free action). Wes holds a duelist's eyes with a smirk; his
 Intimidate rides along as a free-action Boost, because the composure and reputation take no setup. 
-**Showboat** (cross-Role combination). Given room for a display, Wes stacks Sniper +
 Performer + his Pistol Maneuver into one showmanship roll; brandishing can fold the
 Pistol Maneuver into an Intimidate roll (at the risk of getting shot for drawing).
-**The Two-Step** (habitual free-action Wager for a Boost). Wes keeps within two
 steps of cover on principle; barring total surprise, he Wagers a free Cover Boost
 at the start of an ambush.
-**Get the Drop** (cross-Role setup). Joe's successful Sneak feeds her opening Sniper
 shot - the unseen first round.
-**Sized Up** (one Boost, several Roles). Joe banks a read for a later roll, drawing
 on whichever Role fits what she's reading (Naturalist on ground, Sneak on threats,
 Sniper on the field).

##Placement recommendation

-New short page **Tricks - Your Signature Moves** in the **Characters** section,
 immediately after the Hooks pages (Hooks -> How They Work -> Crafting -> **Tricks**).
-One line in **chargen.html** naming Tricks alongside Hooks as a standard, no-cost
 part of building a character.
-The demo sheets already carry a "Boosts & Tricks" block, so the vocabulary is
 consistent with existing material - this just formalizes it.

##Resolved (author, this pass)

1.**Name:** "Tricks" for now. Individual settings or characters may rename them to
  fit flavor (a gunslinger's "habits," a duelist's "forms," etc.) - same mechanic.
2.**Starting count:** none required; two or three to start is typical. GM's call.
3.**No ranking.** Tricks carry no number of their own. They are habits the GM uses
  to justify a bonus in a fitting situation, but the bonus is always based on the
  PC's own rolls and owned abilities. The moment a Trick would carry a rank, it's a
  Maneuver instead.
