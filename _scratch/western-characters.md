# Western Demo Characters (scratchpad)

Working file for Old West / mid-1800s Level One RPG demo characters.
Not site content. Mechanics verified against the L1 rules pages.

Economy reminder:
- Level: L1 free, then +Level to advance (L2=2, L3=3...). Cumulative: L2=2, L3=5, L4=9.
- Role to rank N cumulative: r1=1, r2=2, r3=4, r4=7 (step cost = current rank; entry=1).
- Each Role rank grants ONE free Maneuver rank to allocate.
- Caps: Role <= Level. Maneuver <= its Role. Generally #Maneuvers <= Role rank.
- Hooks are granted, not bought. EL = Level + Role rank + applicable Maneuver rank.
- Sniper Role rank does NOT apply in melee; Maneuver ranks that make sense still do.
- Down at Level + 1 Harm. Luck Tokens = base Level.

## Josephine "Joe" Hardesty - Bounty Hunter
*"Brings 'em back tied, not draped."*

**Level 3 | 10 Karma | Luck 3 | Down: 4 Harm (Level 3 + 1)**
Difficulty: Moderate (a focused kit; rewards range discipline and Hook timing)

### Karma ledger (10)
| Buy | Cost | Free Maneuvers |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 -> 3 | 5 | - |
| Sniper 2 | 2 | Rifle 1, Knives 1 |
| Naturalist 2 | 2 | Tracker 2 |
| Sneak 1 | 1 | Badlands Terrain 1 |
| **Total** | **10** | |

Caps: Roles (2,2,1) <= Level 3; every Maneuver <= its Role. Legal.

### Effective Levels
- **Rifle** - 3+2+1 = **EL 6**
- **Knife, thrown** - 3+2+1 = **EL 6** (ranged; Sniper governs)
- **Knife, in the clinch** - 3+0+1 = **EL 4** (melee: no Sniper Role rank, Maneuver still counts)
- **Pistol, at range** - Role rank only, 3+2 = **EL 5**; grappling distance = EL 3
- **Tracker** - 3+2+2 = **EL 7**
- **Badlands Terrain (Sneak)** - 3+1+1 = **EL 5**
- **Fists / riding / base** - **EL 3** (everyone rides; she's no brawler)
- **Hook** - +2 once/scene when it fits

### Hook - "No Place for a Woman" (Rank 2)
*"They keep telling me what I can't do. I keep collecting the bounty anyway."*
Invoke +2 when a man's underestimating or dismissing her turns to her advantage
(strong Sneak synergy - nobody watches the woman they've decided doesn't matter).
Earns Karma whenever refusing to play the meek expected part costs her: doors shut,
lawmen balk at paying a woman her due, warrants disputed, someone decides to make it
a problem. She could defuse it by performing the part. She won't.

### Tricks
- **The Bead** (Wagered Aim Persistent Boost) - within talking distance she steps out,
  rifle up, and Wagers points off her rifle EL as a persistent Aim Boost. Once
  established it adds to both the roll to make him stand down and the shot if talk fails.
  Her social base is just Level (no Performer), but the leveled rifle is the bonus - "I've
  got you, and you can see it." One rifle, threat and trigger at once. When possible, she
  will use Sneak to boost the Wagered Aim roll first.
- **Mercy Shot** (Dedicated Effect Wager as Hindrance) - she wagers up to her Sniper
  rank (2 points) as dedicated Hindrance rather than Harm: arm or knee, to stop him
  drawing or running, pending medical care. Points come off her EL first; if the shot
  lands without them they take effect as a Hindrance. But a bullet's a poor mercy tool -
  shock and bleeding can still kill. It's a way to take a man alive...probably.
- **Read the Ground** - Joe will stake out an ambush when she can, to take a man
  unawares at a place and time that works to her advantage. A Tracking roll lets her set
  up a Persistent Wagered Boost that can be applied to her Sneak rolls as well as Cover
  for a defensive bonus, hopefully in a place that catches her target at a disadvantage,
  such as watering his horse by the river, which might allow a free action boost because
  his movement is hindered. She's setting up her Boost in advance. That's the whole point.

### Modus operandi - the quandary (leave it)
She's built to kill from concealment, and in her world dead is safer, easier, and pays
enough. "Tied not draped" is a principle she pays for, not a discount she gets. Her
Tricks are exactly the habits that let her *attempt* alive when she chooses -
out-position for a surrender, or take the costlier wounding shot - while the lethal
option is always the easier one right beside it. Live bounties pay more, but the moment
they know she's there she loses the drop, so every capture is a real decision. The
tension is the character; don't design it away. It's a good argument for the table.

### Gear
Long rifle, personally sighted - revolver, worn but true - a good horse (ridden, not
trained) - throwing/belt knife - manacles and a coil of rope - warrant book and a
deputy's tin star, more often pocketed than pinned - bedroll, canteen, field rations -
a folded wanted poster gone soft at the creases; not her current name, kept anyway.

### Play style
Kills at distance, tracks like a bloodhound, disappears in open country, answers a grab
with a blade instead of fists. Fragile at Down 4 - built never to be in reach when it
matters. Don't get hit.

## Wesley "The Deacon" Crane - Gunfighter (hypertuned)
*"Fastest draw in the territory, and he knows it."*

**Level 3 | 10 Karma | Luck 3 | Down: 4 Harm (Level 3 + 1)**
Difficulty: Easy to play, hard to keep alive. One number, sliced well.

### Karma ledger (10)
| Buy | Cost | Free Maneuvers |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 -> 3 | 5 | - |
| Sniper 3 | 4 | Pistol 3 |
| Performer 1 | 1 | Intimidate 1 |
| **Total** | **10** | |

Caps: Sniper 3 <= Level 3; Pistol 3 <= Sniper 3; Performer 1 <= Level; Intimidate 1 <= Performer 1. Legal.

### Effective Levels
- **Pistol, at gunfighting range** (beyond melee - across a room or street) - 3+3+3 = **EL 9**
- **Pistol, point-blank / grappled** - Sniper Role rank drops, Pistol Maneuver still counts - 3+3 = **EL 6**
- **Intimidate (Performer)** - 3+1+1 = **EL 5**
- **Performer, other** (bluff, a few campfire chords on a borrowed guitar) - 3+1 = **EL 4**
- **Everything else** - base **EL 3**
- **Hook** - +2 once/scene

### Why Intimidate over Fast-Draw
Fast-Draw would only stack in the draw itself. Intimidate is load-bearing across
far more: it sets up less-lethal takes, powers Boosts, and defends against someone
trying it on him. Broader leverage from the same single point.

### How EL 9 actually plays
- **The duel:** opposed draw at EL 9 beats essentially anyone straight up.
- **The gang:** split 9 across several men (three at EL 3, or two at 4/5).
- **Called shot:** wager up to Sniper rank (3) as dedicated effect - disarm, wing, wound.
- **Stay standing:** hold points as defense (free, reusable) - EL 5 attack + EL 4 dodge.

### Hook - "Reputation Precedes Him" (Rank 2)
*"Every town's got a boy who wants to be the man who outdrew the Deacon."*
+2 when his name does the work (backing down a room, rattling a challenger). Earns
Karma constantly because he cannot walk away - challengers seek him out, declining
costs him the edge that keeps him alive. The reputation that protects him will
eventually get him killed.

### Signature Tricks
- **No Fear** - holds his opponents' eyes with a smirk as they try to kill each
  other. His Intimidate (EL 5) rides along as a free-action Boost in a duel - it
  takes no setup, it's just his manner.
- **Showboat** - given room to put on a show (spinning the pistols), he can stack
  **Sniper + Performer ranks + the Pistol Maneuver** into one display roll. When it
  makes sense he can also fold his Pistol Maneuver ranks into an Intimidate roll by
  brandishing - with the standing caveat that drawing on someone in a tense standoff
  is a fine way to get shot.
- **The Two-Step** - he assumes someone will always try to ambush the man who killed
  The Deacon, so he stays within "two steps" of cover by habit. Unless completely
  surprised, at the start of an ambush he rolls a free-action Wager for a **Cover**
  Boost, getting something between him and the guns.

### Weaknesses (by design)
Down 4 - a lucky shot ends him like anyone. No breadth: he can't track, hide, or
talk past a problem, only shoot. And he is chained to his own legend.

### Contrast with Joe
Joe spread 10 Karma to survive by never being in reach. Wes stacked 10 Karma into a
single number and dominates the exchange he can't avoid. Same budget, opposite
philosophies - a good demo pair.

## Dr. Elias Warrick - Physician & Medicine-Show Drummer
*"Professor Warrick's Traveling Dispensary & Painless Dentistry."*
Physician, surgeon-barber & dentist. Modern remedies, honestly sold - loudly.

**Level 3 | 10 Karma | Luck 3 | Down: 4 Harm (Level 3 + 1)**
Difficulty: Easy. The party's medic, banker, and non-violent problem-solver.

### Karma ledger (10)
| Buy | Cost | Free Maneuvers |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 -> 3 | 5 | - |
| Healer 2 | 2 | Diagnosis 1, Trauma Handling 1 |
| Tinker 2 | 2 | Chemist 2 (the "Alchemy" chemical-arts maneuver) |
| Performer 1 | 1 | Salesman 1 |
| **Total** | **10** | |

Caps: Roles (2,2,1) <= Level 3; every Maneuver <= its Role. Legal.
*Author's proposed alt: Healer 2 -> Apothecary 2 instead of Diagnosis/Trauma, if a
pure druggist is wanted. Chemist already covers compounding, so the split above
broadens him to diagnosis + surgery + dentistry with no redundancy.*

### Effective Levels
- **Diagnosis (Healer)** - stethoscope, pulse, reading symptoms - 3+2+1 = **EL 6**
- **Trauma Handling (Healer)** - surgery, the barber-dentist's chair, wounds - 3+2+1 = **EL 6**
- **Healer, general** - Role rank 2: treats 2 patients at once before EL penalty, and
  grants bonuses to post-conflict recovery rolls (Complication vs Injury). Do not
  underestimate this - it is the single most valuable thing he brings to a party.
- **Chemist (Tinker)** - antiseptics (carbolic), anesthesia (ether/nitrous), distilled
  tinctures, coca stimulant, chloroform - 3+2+2 = **EL 7** (needs tools, materials, time)
- **Salesman (Performer)** - the pitch, the sale - 3+1+1 = **EL 5**
- **Performer, general** (read a room, bluff) - 3+1 = **EL 4**
- **Fighting anything** - base **EL 3**. He is not a fighter.
- **Hook** - +2 once/scene

### Hook - "First, Do No Harm" (Rank 2)
*"I took an oath. It did not include a means test, and it did not ask whose side you're on."*
Invoke +2 when the oath drives a genuine medical effort. Earns Karma whenever treating
someone costs him: patching a wounded outlaw who then robs him, staying with the sick
while danger closes, refusing to brew a poison though it would solve everything, giving
away medicine he meant to sell. He cannot leave the hurt untreated, and everyone who
knows him knows it.
*(Alt Hook if you'd rather lead with the era-tension: "Ahead of His Time" - he preaches
germ theory, antisepsis, and anesthesia to a world that calls it quackery or deviltry;
+2 when modern knowledge wins the moment, Karma when it brands him a fraud or a heretic.)*

### Tricks
- **The Examination** (Healer / Diagnosis Boost) - stethoscope, pulse, the right
  questions. A Boost that reads the true trouble and adds to his next treatment roll;
  on a person rather than a patient it doubles as a read of what really ails them.
- **The Medicine Show** (Performer / Salesman, Wagered Persistent Boost; may stack a
  Tinker demonstration) - the pitch and the flash: colored smoke, a whiff of nitrous
  laughter, a planted "miracle." He Wagers Performer EL into a persistent "the crowd's
  with me" Boost for the scene - priming a town to trust him, buy his tonics, and fund
  the practice. A chemical demonstration (Tinker) can be folded in when he has the
  setup. The show is the cover that lets him practice real medicine at all.
- **The Dispensary** (Tinker / Chemist, prepared in advance) - given time and his kit he
  has already compounded what the day needs, banked as a Persistent Wagered Boost applied
  when the right remedy becomes relevant: carbolic antiseptic (turning a mortal wound
  survivable on the recovery roll), ether or nitrous to put a man under for the knife,
  laudanum or willow-bark for pain, coca to keep a failing patient on his feet - or a
  chloroform rag to take a man down without firing a shot. His "combat" is a prepared
  soporific, not a gun. Setting the Boost up early is the whole point.

### Gear
Stethoscope - medicine chest of labeled tinctures (laudanum, willow-bark, cannabis,
coca, carbolic acid) - a nitrous/ether apparatus and mask - barber's razor and dental
forceps - the show wagon, banners, and a folding stage - a real diploma, and a second,
grander one that is mostly calligraphy.

### Play style
The indispensable non-combatant: keeps the party alive (recovery bonuses), keeps them
funded (the show), and solves problems with chemistry and a silver tongue instead of a
gun. Fragile (Down 4) and no fighter - but a chloroformed sentry never raised the alarm.
His Hook is an adventure engine: he will always stop to help, and that will always cost.
