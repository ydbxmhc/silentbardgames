# L1 vs. the Field
### A mechanical and philosophical comparison -- internal working note

*Just-for-you orientation, not canon and not site copy. Analysis only -- treat as `@@*`
until ratified. **v3:** blackjack (not roll-under); a success-pool that scales by width;
Extra Effort makes it *unconstrained* blackjack; folded in Shadowrun, fuller WoD + Exalted,
and Amber Diceless; and realism is a **table dial**, which settles the placement.*

---

## 1. L1's design DNA

Strip L1 to its load-bearing choices:

1. **Blackjack resolution in a success-counting pool.** Each die wants to land *under* your
   Effective Level but *as high as possible* -- bust if you go over, and in opposition the higher
   non-bust roll wins. That's blackjack, not plain roll-under: maximizing under a ceiling, not
   clearing a floor. The default **Simple Pool** rolls several such dice and counts successes. EL
   (base Level + relevant Role + Maneuver + Boosts) is your *per-die* competence -- the ceiling;
   the *pool* is your capacity. And the ceiling isn't fixed: **Extra Effort turns it into
   *unconstrained* blackjack** -- push past EL by deciding, *before the dice fall*, how much extra
   you're taking on, bust risk and all. You call your "hit me" blind and up front (and it can't
   buy you a *natural* extreme -- those must be rolled clean).
2. **You scale, and act, by widening the pool.** More dice = more successes = more capability, and
   **splitting** the pool across actions is the L1 answer to "extra attacks" -- spread thin for many
   weak actions or concentrate for one strong one. Speed is a resource-allocation choice, not a stat.
3. **One grammar for everything.** Roles and Maneuvers describe PCs, NPCs, monsters, and forces of
   nature alike -- a bear takes the Role *Bear*; a ghost takes *Shade*. Subsystem work becomes PC work.
4. **Depth and breadth, not builds.** Deepen a Role (depth of EL) or cover weak seams (breadth of
   pool) -- never assemble a combo that wins. Stated as a *value*.
5. **Hooks: drives that cut both ways.** A tappable liability. Add its rank when it helps; anyone who
   knows it adds its rank *against* you. Fate's Aspect / Cortex's Distinction, load-bearing.
6. **Advancement IS characterization.** Karma is *earned by leaning into your Hooks and complications.*
   Playing your drives, and paying for them, is how you level. (Karma on a Hook's first trigger per
   session, Tokens after -- an anti-farming cap.)
7. **Harm is a menu.** The attacker chooses the *kind*: Complication, Injury, Hindrance, Hook, or Down.
8. **Improvised labels, fiction-first, tone-agnostic.** "Name the thing that matters, give it weight."
   The engine hosts whatever genre and realism the table enforces (see 5, below) -- it doesn't impose one.

> **Is #6 a weakness?** (You asked.) A *real tension*, not a clean win, shared by the whole
> narrative-reward family (Fate compels, PbtA flags, oWoD Nature, Exalted stunts, Burning Wheel Artha):
> it can reward drama-for-drama's-sake and Hook-farming; it advantages the player who leans into brutal
> Hooks over the cautious one (your *Pellan Atavism* note); it gently coerces a play style. You already
> built the main guardrail -- **Karma only on a Hook's first trigger per session** caps the farm. What
> remains is uneven advancement and play-style pressure: manageable, maybe the point, but worth a
> session Karma ceiling or spotlight guidance.

## 2. The resolution question -- blackjack in a pool, and where that sits

The die mechanic is **blackjack** -- roll high *without busting EL* -- inside a **success-counting
pool**. Three consequences:

- **L1 is a pool system, squarely** -- same broad family as Cortex, World of Darkness, Exalted,
  Shadowrun, Year Zero, Blades. It **scales by width**, like they do; EL "depth" is just each die's
  ceiling. (My v1 "depth not width" was backwards.)
- **Blackjack is the uncommon part.** Games roll under a target (CoC/BRP, GURPS, 2d20, old B/X), and
  games roll pools and count successes (above) -- but *rolling as high as you dare under a ceiling, in
  an opposed pool* is rare; I can't name a major-market game that centers it. Nearest kin is Cortex's
  opposed "higher total wins," with the ceiling added. Plausibly **easier to digest than Cortex**: no
  keep-two, no effect die, no hitches -- just "each die, high as you dare under your number; count what
  lands." And **Extra Effort removes the ceiling for a price**, which no roll-under game does.
- **On the Fortune / Karma / Drama axis, L1 is Fortune tilted toward Karma.** (The old resolution
  taxonomy: chance decides = *Fortune*; fixed character ranking decides = *Karma*; story-logic decides
  = *Drama*.) L1 rolls dice (Fortune) but the deep-EL blackjack means competence usually tells, and
  characterization feeds the currency -- Karma and Drama leaning on the dice. The pure-Karma pole of
  this whole map is **Amber Diceless** (below): no randomizer at all.

The takeaway for the reader page: **teach it as blackjack in the first sentence.** The ceiling is what
earns the "counterintuitive" rap, and "high as you can without busting" is a model every player owns.

## 3. The landscape, family by family

**The d20 mainstream -- D&D 5e, Pathfinder 2e.** *L1's opposite pole.* Roll-high single die vs a pool;
build-optimization vs anti-build; power on the sheet vs power in play; monster-as-different-object vs one
grammar; near-zero metacurrency vs Karma/Tokens; HP attrition vs a harm menu.

**OSR -- B/X, OSE, Shadowdark.** *Philosophically close, mechanically distant.* Shares anti-build,
rulings, GM trust, lethality-as-honesty; but no story-reward economy, and single-die not pool.

**Trad-unified engines -- GURPS, BRP.** *Cousins on "one mechanic for everything," opposite temperament.*
GURPS unifies like L1 but is the ultimate build game and story-neutral. BRP is roll-under skill par
excellence, story-agnostic. Both single-roll, not pools.

**The narrative family -- PbtA, Fate, Blades.** *L1's philosophical home.*
- **PbtA**: fiction-first; failure is interesting; advance by leaning into flags/bonds/drives
  (= Karma-from-Hooks); GM "makes moves" (= triggering your Hooks). Same soul, 2d6-tiered math.
- **Fate**: Aspects *are* Hooks; compels pay you to complicate; invoking is tapping your Hook. And
  **Fate Core's Quick Character Creation is the direct precedent for the Recovery Trope** -- build in
  play; L1 does it in Karma.
- **Blades / FitD**: consequences-as-a-menu = attacker-chooses-harm; flashbacks = the *action* form of
  declare-in-play (not unique to Blades -- Cortex and others do a version -- but cleanly formalized).

**Pool-and-metacurrency games -- Cortex, Shadowrun, World of Darkness / Exalted, Savage Worlds, Year
Zero.** *This is L1's mechanical neighborhood.* All pair a success-counting-ish pool with a metacurrency.
- **Cortex** -- closest mechanical relative. Trait-dice for everything, Distinctions = Hooks, Plot Points
  ~ Karma, opposed "higher total wins." L1 is Cortex-shaped with a blackjack ceiling and a lighter load.
- **Shadowrun** -- a *fat* d6 success-pool (5-6 = hit) with **Edge** as a luck/push metacurrency (~ Luck
  Tokens) and glitches on 1s. Same engine family as L1; *opposite* build philosophy -- it's famously
  gear-and-cyberware optimization. A clean illustration that "success-pool + luck token" doesn't dictate
  L1's anti-build stance; that's a separate, deliberate choice.
- **White Wolf -- Storyteller/Storytelling (Vampire, Werewolf, Mage) and Exalted.** d10 pools counting
  successes, **Willpower** as metacurrency, morality/nature tracks. Two real parallels: old WoD's
  **Nature archetype regains Willpower when you act in character** -- characterization paying a resource,
  a genuine ancestor of Karma-from-Hooks -- and **Exalted's stunts**, which hand bonus dice and resource
  regen for describing an action with flair. That is "the vicarious characterization IS the power-play,"
  already on the table since the '90s. L1's move is to make it the *spine*, not a bonus.
- **Savage Worlds** -- Hindrances are dual-natured liabilities-for-points, genuine Hook cousins; Bennies
  the metacurrency; pulp-tuned, roll-high exploding dice.
- **Year Zero** -- count-6s pool with a *push* (risk-for-reward after a fail); stress/panic. Roll-high.

**Diceless -- Amber Diceless (Wujcik).** *The far pole of L1's own conviction.* No dice at all:
ranked attributes (Warfare, Psyche, Strength, Endurance, bought by auction) plus circumstances, tactics,
and *good roleplaying* decide, with GM adjudication; "Good Stuff / Bad Stuff" is a karma balance.
It is the purest statement of "characterization and standing decide outcomes" -- L1 shares the conviction
but keeps the dice, using deep-EL blackjack to let competence usually tell *without* going fully
deterministic. Amber is where L1's philosophy goes if you delete the randomizer.

**Dice-as-story -- Genesys/FFG, Daggerheart.** *Shared instinct, different device.* Narrative dice
generate story texture beyond pass/fail -- L1's "the number determines the fiction, now narrate why."

**Story-crunch -- Burning Wheel.** *Philosophically closest, tonally opposite.* Advance through use and
by playing Beliefs/Instincts for Artha -- the purest characterization-as-power loop, heavy where L1 is light.

## 4. Your three buckets

**Genuinely similar -- L1 is in good company.**
- A success-counting pool that scales by width (Cortex, Shadowrun, WoD, Year Zero, Blades).
- A metacurrency economy (Fate points, Bennies, Willpower, Edge, Plot Points, Artha -> Karma/Tokens).
- Dual-natured descriptors that cut both ways (Aspects, Distinctions, Hindrances, Natures -> Hooks).
- Fiction-first, failure-is-interesting, GM-complicates-via-your-drives.
- One unified grammar for PCs and world (GURPS, Cortex, Fate).
- Build-in-play (Fate Core Quick Character Creation -> the Recovery Trope, in Karma).
- Rewarding characterization *at all* (oWoD Nature, Exalted stunts, Fate compels, BW Artha).

**A little different -- a familiar idea with an L1 twist.**
- **Blackjack per die, made *unconstrained* by Extra Effort** -- set your own ceiling before the roll.
- **Pool-splitting as the action economy** -- extra actions by spreading the pool thinner, not a feature.
- **Hooks that pay *advancement*, both sides tapping** -- routed to Karma, not just a spend or XP.
- **Harm as an explicit typed menu**, attacker's choice.

**Genuinely divergent -- where few or none stack up.**
- **Characterization-as-advancement is the *core loop*, not a garnish.** Everyone from WoD to Exalted to
  Fate to Burning Wheel rewards characterization, but as a layer on a conventional engine; L1 makes it the
  spine.
- **A universal engine where the *build isn't the win button*.** GURPS, Cortex, Shadowrun unify and
  reward gear/optimization; L1 unifies but makes the combo a dead end for *power* -- you can specialize
  (deeply, if the GM allows), it just costs escalating breadth, and real advancement comes from
  characterization. Not "builds forbidden" -- builds just aren't how you win.
- **The specific fusion + the blackjack engine.** No popular game stacks all of: unconstrained-blackjack
  success-pool + one grammar for every entity + advancement-bought-with-characterization +
  attacker-chooses-harm menu + explicit anti-build creed + table-dialed realism. Each piece has precedent;
  the assembly is a fingerprint.

## 5. L1's actual position (`@@*` -- proposed)

**"Trad" was wrong (v1), and your realism-dial point settles why.** The taxonomy is a rough trichotomy --
**Trad** (GM runs a simulated world; fixed engine; 5e, GURPS, WoD, Savage Worlds), **OSR** (rulings,
lethal, minimal build; B/X, Shadowdark), **Storygame** (shared authority, fiction-first, metacurrency,
characterization-rewarded; PbtA, Fate, Blades, Burning Wheel). By reward loop, harm-menu, Hook economy,
and fiction-first ethos, **L1 is a storygame.**

I'd worried the "simulationist streak" (roll-under competence, EL depth) pulled it back toward trad. Your
answer dissolves that: **realism is a dial the table sets** -- GURPS-hard SF, *Ninja Scroll* anime, or
*Pokemon*, same engine, tuned by convention and GM enforcement. So the simulationism isn't a commitment;
it's *tunability*. That makes L1 a **universal / genre-neutral engine** (the GURPS / Cortex / Fate /
Savage Worlds camp) whose *heart* is storygame -- a narrower peer group than "universal" alone, because
most universal systems are trad-simulationist (GURPS, BRP) or pulp (Savage Worlds). The universal engines
that are *also* storygame-hearted are **Cortex and Fate** -- exactly L1's closest relatives.

**The one-liner (recorded 2026-09-14).**

> **L1 is a universal, genre-neutral engine with a storygame's heart -- realism dialed by the table,
> advancement bought with characterization, and a dice-pool you play like blackjack: hit me, but
> don't bust.**

*Fuller version, for the map (keeps the peer placement):* a universal, genre-neutral engine with a
storygame's heart -- realism dialed by the table (GURPS-hard to Pokemon), advancement bought with
characterization, resolved through a clean, unconstrained-blackjack success-pool that sits nearest Cortex.

Peer group, sharpened: **Cortex and Fate** are the universal-storygame cousins; **Amber Diceless** is the
philosophical far pole (delete the dice and L1's convictions become Amber); **PbtA and Burning Wheel** are
the reward-loop kin. L1's own corner of that room is the blackjack engine + the anti-build creed +
characterization-as-*core*-advancement -- no one of the cousins holds all three.

That's the honest map. The wobble's gone; if anything still reads off, it'll be a matter of emphasis, not
category.
