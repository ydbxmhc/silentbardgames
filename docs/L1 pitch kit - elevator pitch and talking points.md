# L1 -- Pitch Kit
### Elevator pitch, talking points, and read-the-room pivots

*Companion to "L1 vs. the Field." This is outward-facing pitch language, not canon and not
site copy. Draft -- `@@*` until you've said it aloud a few times and kept what lands.*

---

## 0. The hook (one line)

> **L1 is a universal, genre-neutral engine with a storygame's heart -- realism dialed by the
> table, advancement bought with characterization, and a dice-pool you play like blackjack:
> hit me, but don't bust.**

If they blink or lean in, you've got the ride.

---

## 1. The elevator ride (three beats, ~30-45 seconds)

Three punches, ordered by what lands fastest. You rarely need all three -- one usually earns the question.

- **"You play your character, not a build."** No feat trees, no combo you have to look up, no arms
  race. The reward for playing your character *well* is that you get to be awesome when it's your
  moment. Lean into who they are, and the game pays you for it.
- **"One set of rules runs everything."** A hero, a dragon, a plague, a god of storms -- same words,
  same math. Learn it once and you can stat anything at the table without flipping to a different chapter.
- **"Your flaws are your engine."** The things that get your character into trouble -- the fear, the
  greed, the oath -- are literally how you level up. Play them, let them cost you, get stronger.
  Drama isn't a tax; it's the fuel.

Land any one and they'll usually ask a question. That's the door.

---

## 2. Outside the elevator (they stopped to chat, ~2-5 minutes)

Now you can open the hood. Reach for what fits the conversation, not all of it.

- **The blackjack reframe -- disarm the dice question early.** *"It isn't roll-under weirdness -- it's
  blackjack. Roll high without going over your number. You already know how to play it; and if you
  want the good stuff up top, you stack on effort to *reach* it -- the ceiling doesn't move, you just
  build a taller footstool, and lean too far and you bust: hit me, but don't bust."* Turns the one
  genuinely unfamiliar thing into the most familiar thing at the table.
- **Call your shot -- damage can be a story, not a number.** A normal hit is a passing setback that
  shrugs off with a breather. But you can *call your shot* and push for something specific: a lasting
  wound, a disarm, even a compulsion that rewrites what they want. Fights bend the story when you aim
  them to, instead of just draining a health bar.
- **Any genre, any grit.** The same engine runs hard sci-fi, wuxia anime, or something as light as
  Pokemon. Realism is a dial the table sets, not a wall the rules build -- one system for any campaign
  you'll ever want.
- **Where it sits (for people who know systems).** *"The tidiness of Cortex, the heart of Fate, and a
  house rule against min-maxing."* For the theory crowd: a universal, genre-neutral engine with a
  storygame's soul -- Fortune tilted toward Karma, if they speak that dialect.

---

## 3. Read the room -- pick the door for who you're talking to

Lead with the beat that scratches *their* itch:

- **5e / Pathfinder player** -> *"Power comes from playing your character, not stacking feats -- and one
  stat block runs every monster."* (Anti-build + ease.)
- **Fate / PbtA / story-gamer** -> *"The characterization reward you love, except it's the spine of the
  game, not a bolt-on -- running on one tight universal engine."* (Story-heart-as-core.)
- **OSR player** -> *"Rulings-friendly, as lethal as you want, no build bloat -- but with a real reason
  to lean into your character."* (Anti-build + tunable grit.)
- **GURPS / Cortex / universal fan** -> *"One grammar for everything, the way you like it -- but here
  the min-max isn't the cheat code: specialize as deep as you want, it just costs you breadth, and the
  power comes from playing your character."* (Universal; the build isn't the point.)
- **Total newcomer** -> *"You already know blackjack; you make up a character; the dice tell you what
  happens. Nothing to build."* (Pure accessibility.)

---

## 4. The two objections you'll actually hear

- **"Roll-under is weird."** -> It's blackjack (see §2, first bullet). This is exactly why the *word*
  matters -- lead with it and the objection never forms.
- **"Why another universal system?"** -> *"Most generic engines are build-games or story-neutral;
  story-games run loose. This is a clean universal ruleset where playing in character is HOW you power
  up."* Cortex-tidy, Fate-hearted -- the corner no one else is standing in. *(Post-ready; this became
  the socials draft.)*
