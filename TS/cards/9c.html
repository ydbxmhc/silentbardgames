Nine of Clubs

Censor's Torch

No, and - Conflict

Keywords - Upright: knowledge destroyed, the seeker marked  ·  Inverted: the burning as confession, ash that testifies

Upright

The knowledge existed, you found it, and now it's ash and you're known for having looked. Everything got worse the moment the torch fell.

You found where the answer lived, and someone burned the building. The record is gone - not lost: unmade, on purpose, recently - and your name is on the visitor rolls that survive. Two facts now travel together: the thing you sought cannot be read, and the people who unmade it know somebody tried. The first fact ends a search. The second one begins a different kind.

Inverted

Destruction is a statement with a return address. What was burned, how fast, and who could order it - the fire answers questions the documents never would have. Someone spent power on this and left prints doing it; the absence itself now testifies. You cannot read the page. You can read the burning - so read it aloud, to the right listener, and watch what moves.

In Play

Upright. Wynn reaches the registry a day behind the fire - the deed-book is ash, and the clerk who admits her writes her name into the fresh visitors' ledger with a steady hand.

Inverted. One book burned, in a room of hundreds, within a day of her first inquiry - which tells Wynn the deed was real, current, and defended by someone close enough to hear of her quickly.
