Queen of Clubs

Witch's Curse

No, and - Conflict

Keywords - Upright: targeted malice, an enemy declared  ·  Inverted: the curse as signature, malice mapped

Upright

Not merely opposition - deliberate, targeted, personal. Someone with real power decided you specifically are the problem. The conflict that follows has weight and intention behind it.

This is not weather; it is aim. Someone with real power has considered you - specifically, by name - and spent effort on your harm. The bad luck has architecture, the obstacles have authorship, and the campaign against you is a decision being renewed daily, somewhere. Frightening, yes. But mark what it proves: you matter enough to curse. Powers do not spend malice on the irrelevant.

Inverted

Malice this personal is a document. The curse's shape - what it strikes, what it spares, what it knows of your habits - is a portrait of its maker: their reach, their sources, their fear. They wrote their motive into the working. Read it back: who profits, who watches, who knew that about you? A curse names its caster to anyone who studies it. Study it before you answer it.

In Play

Upright. Vell's luck goes wrong all at once and all in one direction - every door in the river trade closing in the same week, with the same words.

Inverted. The same words every time: whoever set this knows the harbormasters by name and fears Vell on the water specifically. That is a short list, and half of it dined at her table last spring.
