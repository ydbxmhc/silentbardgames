The Deck

Ace of Spades

Duellist's Evasion

No, but - Labor

Keywords - Upright: the attack turned, ground given wisely, a read gained  ·  Inverted: the read ready to spend, retreat as setup

Upright

The blade arm failed but the footwork saved you. A skilled retreat, not a rout. You know more about your opponent now.

The attack failed - parried, sidestepped, answered - and the failing was managed. You gave ground on your own terms and paid attention while you did it: how the counter came, where the weight sat, what the defense costs its owner. No blood, no breakthrough, but the map of your opponent is a page fuller. That page is the consolation. It is not yet the win.

Inverted

The lesson is ripe. What the failed exchange taught is specific and current, and there is a narrow window in which it stays true - guards adjust, and opponents notice being studied. Spending the read now, on the very next exchange, is the draw. Or read it the other way: the ground you gave was never lost at all, and the retreat pulled them exactly where you wanted them standing.

In Play

Upright. Aldric's lunge is turned and he falls back a pace - noting, as he goes, how the man's guard drops whenever he counters high.

Inverted. The dropped-guard tell holds; the next exchange is Aldric's to take, if he takes it before the man's second shouts a warning.
