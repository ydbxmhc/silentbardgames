King of Clubs

Inquisitor's Writ

No, and - Conflict

Keywords - Upright: authority in full, process as weapon  ·  Inverted: procedure binding the wielder, the writ's own leash

Upright

The full weight of organized authority, properly documented, coming down. This isn't personal - it's institutional. Which makes it worse. The Inquisitor has paperwork.

The machine has your name in it now. Not an enemy - a process: warrants sealed, jurisdictions cleared, men assigned who have done this before and will do it again after. Institutional weight has no temper to lose and no mercy to appeal to; it simply proceeds. The writ is not personal. That is precisely what makes it heavy.

Inverted

Everything that makes the machine strong makes it rigid. The writ binds its bearer too: forms that must be correct, orders that must be shown, boundaries drawn by the very authority being wielded. Procedure is a cage with two occupants - and the one who knows its bars better wins. Read the writ more closely than its bearer ever expected anyone to.

In Play

Upright. The inquisitor lays the sealed writ on Tamsin's bench, and the room's business continues around them in a wide, careful circle.

Inverted. Reading it through twice, Tamsin finds the writ names her workshop but not her person, and this county but not the next - and the border coach leaves within the hour.
