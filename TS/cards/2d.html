Two of Diamonds

Marksman's Reload

Yes, but - Commercial

Keywords - Upright: the shot placed, tempo spent  ·  Inverted: the gap exploited, caught between shots

Upright

The shot landed. Now there's a pause, a cost, a moment of vulnerability while the mechanism resets.

The shot does its work - placed, not sprayed - and now the mechanism wants its due. Every precise instrument buys its precision with time: the span, the wind check, the fresh bolt seated. The success is banked. The pause that follows is the price, and everyone on the field can watch you paying it.

Inverted

The reload is where the field turns. Whatever the shot achieved, the silence after it is an invitation, and something has accepted: closing the distance, changing cover, using the seconds you cannot shoot to rewrite the problem. The next draw is not about aim. It is about what reaches you before the string is taut again.

In Play

Upright. Sera's first bolt takes the raiders' leader off his horse - and while she spans the crossbow, the rest scatter into the treeline with their plunder.

Inverted. The bolt lands, and the two nearest riders mark where it came from - they are halfway up her ridge before the next quarrel seats.
