# Three of Diamonds

![Three of Diamonds](https://pub-e0f96c01318c4755b491bf481c530eb1.r2.dev/cards/3d.png)

## Mentor's Critique

Yes, but - Commercial

**Keywords** - *Upright:* success with notes, a teacher's real attention ·
*Inverted:* approval as leash, standards that climb

### Upright

*The performance worked. Now here are the notes, the expectations, the
required improvements. Success with an invoice of obligations attached.*

It worked - the audience, the teacher, the room all say so - and the praise
comes stapled to a syllabus. Genuine attention from someone who knows the
craft is worth more than applause, and costs more too: expectations,
corrections, the assumption that you now intend to be excellent. The
compliment is the assignment.

### Inverted

The mentor's approval has quietly become the performance's purpose, and that
is a trade you never agreed to. The notes never end; the bar climbs with every
clearance; the repertoire narrows to what pleases one chair in the room.
Whether to play for the room or for the chair - and what refusing the chair
will cost - is a question worth its own card.

### In Play

*Upright.* The maestro applauds Pell's recital, then hands her three pages of
corrections and the date of her next examination.

*Inverted.* Pell's tavern bookings begin to vanish - the maestro has let it be
known that his students do not sing in common rooms.
