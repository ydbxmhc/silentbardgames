Social Roles

Performer

— The world is my stage, and I know exactly how to play it.

A Performer is an emotional architect who understands that all human interaction is fundamentally theater. They manage perceptions with chosen words, gestures, or artistic expression, and reshape how an audience feels and thinks. A Performer doesn't just "entertain" — they manipulate the currents of emotion and logic, delivering exactly the right message at the right moment, and correctly understanding the difference between what people want to hear and what they need to hear.

They don't have to be flashy entertainers — a Performer might be a quiet counselor who never raises their voice, a gruff sergeant who knows exactly how to motivate his troops, a street hustler who three-card montes their way through life, or virtually every merchant who has ever lived. What matters is understanding human nature and knowing how to use it.

The Role applies to any situation where understanding and influencing human emotion and behavior matters: persuasion, deception, intimidation, entertainment, negotiation, or social maneuvering. Performers are especially good at leveraging Boosts — a little preparation goes a long way.

Common Performer Maneuvers

Artistic Expression

  • Lutes — Plucked stringed instruments with a neck and frets
  • Rhetoric — Oration, debate, rapier wit and response to challenges
  • Physical Theater — Body language, dance, mime, and physical performance techniques
  • Sleight of Hand — Misdirection, stage magic, and manual dexterity for entertainment

Specific Instrument Groups

  • Vocals — Voice as instrument

Social Manipulation

  • Etiquette — Cultivated properly in high society
  • Empathy — Reading and responding to hidden emotions and concerns
  • Social Chameleon — Blending in with mannerisms, dress, and customs
  • Acting — The art of believable representation; also useful for deception
  • Court Politics — Navigating formal hierarchies, protocol, and political intrigue
  • Subtle Influence — Long-term social manipulation through seemingly casual interaction

Advanced Applications

Performers are especially good at leveraging Boosts. Before they enter a room they can talk to the local gossip to set up a Persistent Boost by knowing things that would get the crowd's attention. A moment to tap into local customs and mood (a free action Boost) adds an extra die.

Looking to be invited for their supper at the local tavern? Before they place they can talk to the local gossip and set up a Persistent Boost by knowing the crowd's dynamic. Add the fact they often have the opportunity for free action Boosts — a Performer roll against Drunk Crowd and Popular Song, add the fact they often have the opportunity for free action Boosts, together with an introduction before playing a song, and adding Maneuvers for both singing and playing, and playing, easily rolling with a Level 2 minstrel rolling with more than EL10.
