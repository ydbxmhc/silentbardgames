Social Roles

Performer

- The world is my stage, and I know exactly how to play it.

A Performer is an emotional architect who understands that all human interaction is fundamentally theater. They read crowds using chosen words, gestures, or artistic expression, and fear that they reshape how an audience feels, and how they think and act. A Performer doesn't just "entertain" — they manipulate the foundations of social reality.

The Role applies to any situation where understanding and influencing others matters: persuasion, deception, inspiration, intimidation, seduction, or anything else that must read and adjust social dynamics. Performers excel at gauging crowd mood and commanding attention in any setting, delivering exactly the right message at the correct moment, and correctly understanding the difference between what people want to hear and what they need to hear.

They don't have to be flashy entertainers — a Performer might be a subtle courtier who never raises their voice but always gets their way, or a gruff sergeant who knows exactly how to motivate his troops. What matters is understanding human nature and knowing how to use it; a very distant second, and that every interaction is a chance to guide that response.

Common Performer Maneuvers

The classic range for Performers is on a literal stage, but politicians are Performers too, as are "good cop/bad" watchmen, loan shark enforcers intimidating debtors, street hucksters scamming with 3-card monte, priests debating theological points, and virtually every merchant who has ever lived.

The most effective Performers understand that their greatest performances happen when the audience doesn't realize they're watching a show. True mastery lies not in obvious manipulation, but in making people feel understood, inspired, and acted to their own conclusions — whether it's placing a bet, confessing a crime, or coming to their feet for the resounding applause of a nice long standing ovation.

Artistic Expression

  • Stagecraft — Creating emotional resonance with artistic preparation and presentation
  • Rhetoric — Oration, debate, rapier wit and misdirection to move hearts and minds
  • Physical Theater — Body language, dance, mime, and visual performance techniques
  • Sleight of Hand — Misdirection, stage magic, and manual dexterity for entertainment

Specific Instrument Groups

  • Lutes — Plucked stringed instruments with a neck and frets
  • Pipes — Wind instruments such as flutes
  • Vocals — Voice as instrument

Social Manipulation

  • Etiquette — Cultivated properly in high society
  • Empathy — Reading the room, detecting enthusiasm and discontent, or facts and lies
  • Social Chameleon — Blending in with mannerisms, sub-cultures, and colloquialisms
  • Acting — The art of believable presentation; also quite useful for deception

Advanced Applications

Performers are especially good at leveraging Boosts.

Looking to sing for their supper at the local tavern? Before they place they can talk around for the local gossip to set up a Persistent Boost by knowing things that get the crowd's attention. Take a moment to tap pre-existing free action Boosts — roll against Drunk Crowd and Popular Song. Add the fact they often have the opportunity to talk and set up an immediate Boost, guiding the various patrons together with an introduction before playing a song, and then adding Maneuvers for both singing and playing, and you have a Level 2 minstrel rolling with better than EL10.
