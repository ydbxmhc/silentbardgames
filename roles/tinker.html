Knowledge and Craft Roles

Tinker

— These don't grow on trees, you know.

Tinkers transform raw materials into solutions, welding hammer and pliers with plans that assume understanding how things are put together. They assume that given proper tools, good materials, and time, they can craft anything within their expertise, restore damaged equipment, or create totally new devices from available materials.

This encompasses everything from village blacksmiths to alchemical researchers, from locksmiths to siege engineers. They see the world as a collection of puzzles waiting to be unlocked — and they carry the tools to unlock them.

Tinkers don't rely on brute force — they apply leverage, whether mechanical, chemical, or conceptual. A good Tinker might spend weeks perfecting a lock pick that would take a lesser thief an hour to bypass and years to master.

Creation and Manipulation

Crafting and Repair: Tinkers excel at creating, fixing, and modifying manufactured items. Given proper tools, good materials, and time, they can craft items within their expertise, restore damaged equipment, or adapt existing tools for new purposes.

Problem-Solving Applications: Beyond basic craftsmanship, Tinkers apply their technical knowledge to overcome obstacles — picking locks, disarming traps, jury-rigging repairs from available materials, or sabotaging enemy equipment effectively.

Specialized Knowledge: Each area of expertise requires specific Maneuver investments. The blacksmith's general metalworking skill won't help design a complex clockwork mechanism; that requires Clockwork specifically.

Common Tinker Maneuvers

Mechanical Arts

  • Locksmithing — Creating, defeating, and understanding mechanical security systems
  • Clockwork — Precision mechanical systems and energy storage mechanisms
  • Engineering — Structural design, mechanical advantage, and large-scale construction
  • Weaponsmithing — Forging, tempering, and perfecting instruments of combat

Chemical Arts

  • Alchemy — Distillation, purification, and understanding of plant essences and their extraction
  • Herbalism — Understanding plant properties and their extraction
  • Metallurgy — Alloy creation, metal treatment, and specialized smelting applications
  • Explosives — Controlled combustion for mining, demolition, or tactical applications

Practical Applications

  • Trap Making — Concealed mechanisms for tactical or security advantage
  • Handyman — Emergency repairs and improvisational construction
  • Tool Making — Creating specialized implements for specific tasks
  • Sabotage — Understanding systems well enough to disable them effectively

Advanced Applications

Synergy with Other Roles: Tinkers often complement other abilities — a Spellweaver might use Tinker Maneuvers to create scrolls, potions, and magical implements. A Warrior might craft superior weapons through dedicated smithing expertise.

Resource Management: Tinker work often depends as much on preparation, materials, and appropriate workspace as on technical skill.

Creative Problem-Solving: The best Tinkers see connections between different fields, applying principles from one area to solve problems in another.
