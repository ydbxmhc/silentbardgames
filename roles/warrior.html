Combat Roles

Warrior

— I am brave, strong, and ready to fight.

The warrior role is about physical conflict, dealing out harmful force (or the threat of it) with precision and using their training to survive the ire of others. They can effectively use any common or reasonable improvised weapon effectively; a warrior can make a beer stem hurt you in ways you will remember for a lifetime. The Role also applies to feats of intimidation, courage, tactical evaluation of enemies and strategies, and throwing anything handy at short range.

This doesn't mean they instinctively know the specialized techniques for exotic weapons like a khopesh or kusarigama — that requires specific training, expressed as Maneuvers. At its base, the Warrior Role is a brawler; it represents someone who understands the fundamentals of violence: timing, distance, leverage, and the will to act decisively without being squeamish when it matters.

Common Warrior Maneuvers

Warriors will specialize their combat expertise in different ways. Remember, these are examples — each represents a specific expertise that transfers between related situations. A warrior with Polearms can find dishonourable advantages in any combat, from tavern brawls to formal duels. These are just a few examples.

Weapon Categories

  • Brawling — special training for unarmed fights
  • Swords — Edge work, binding, measure, and tempo with any bladed weapon
  • Shields — Cover angles, bash techniques, and weapon integration with any shield
  • Polearms — Reach management, leverage, and formation fighting

Combat Techniques

  • Battlefield Awareness — Read combat flow, assess threats and opportunities
  • Combat Reflexes — Improved reaction to sudden attacks and resistance to surprise attacks
  • Dirty Fighting — Exploit any dishonourable openings — eye gouges, groin shots, thrown sand
  • Armed Combat — ways to leverage advantages against more experienced combatants for more advantage
