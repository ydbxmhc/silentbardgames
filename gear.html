Core Mechanics

Gear

Gear is any relevant equipment your character carries — weapons, armor, tools, spells, followers, spirits. Keep inventory simple. Characters are competent people who keep inventory of their expected challenges. It's reasonable for them to have what they need for their expected challenges. If you prefer OSR-style play that counts every caltrops and spike, write them down, make a roll. We aren't here to waste your time, so let's get to how you can use your gear to do it.

Gear is transient — it's just stuff. Swords break, torches catch fire, packs wash downstream. If an item matters to a character concept, represent it as a Hook or Maneuver. Add a Concert, or just make it a House Rule that the hero never loses their hat.

What Gear Does

Creates Narrative Permission — This is gear's primary function. Even without mechanical bonuses, your archer needs a bow. No weapon means unarmed combat defaults to Complications that won't fade after the fight. An archer without a bow can throw rocks. Using weapons means some Harm might be Injury. Armed combat represents the normal post-conflict recovery system: the usual post-conflict recovery (see Harm & Recovery).

Provides Mechanical Benefits — Gear can boost existing skills, above and beyond the flat bonus. A knife doesn't just add flat bonus — training with it adds Maneuver ranks to making every cut count and apply to active defense Focus effects as well. A heavy weapon will slow a PC in a chase, but "dealing with it in the narrative" is simple — drop it or set it down first.

Creates More Narrative! — Gear often comes with built-in drawbacks or "Snags" that generate story complications. Like Plot Hooks, these limitations work in your favor — they don't provide Karma but they make your character more interesting and more memorable.

Walking into town armed and armored marks you as potential trouble until locals know you better — using weapons means some Harm might be Injury depending on the narrative. Using weapons means unarmed combat doesn't apply until you lose your weapon. One doesn't stroll into the marketplace with a strung bow to buy sausages. Using weapons means some attention from authorities, thieves, and people with problems they hope you can solve.

Gear can also apply Hindrances to specific activities — mostly movement and agility. Don't swim in chainmail or attempt calligraphy in plate gauntlets. Heavy gear slows movement and creates noise, significantly hampers Sneak rolls, and heavy armor draws unwanted attention from authorities, thieves, and people with problems they hope you can solve.

Other Gear

Many other tools can follow this model, but for a lot of things the better approach is to use circumstantial Boosts. If you have Athlete with Climbing and are getting out pitons probably counts as a Persistent Boost that then applies to the whole climb. If you're already in the water, you can probably just let them cross unless it's already dangerous, and at that point it's probably going to mean an Extreme roll to lose the whole mule.

Not every item should trigger a dice-rolling frenzy. Characters are competent. If a character has an axe, you can probably let them just cross unless it's already dangerous, and at that point it's probably going to mean an Extreme to lose the entire mule.

Be wary of letting players use this rule on an item that has Maneuver ranks of its own; ranks in Swords represent real training and actual skill, not just a situational Boost. If they want to do that sort of stacking they need to explain why this particular use of the weapon would always add that roll when most attacks do not.

Armor

Armor creates narrative permission to resist harm effectively. Narratively, the more inconvenient the armor (the due cost of social penalties, movement restrictions, and the like), the more it protects you. Characters without Armor Maneuvers can still cite simpler approaches, but they don't get to add armor to the simplest approaches, but they aren't getting any roll bonuses on splits or use it in action economy tricks.

Characters with those Maneuvers can use those extra points of resistance EL on splits or use Focus effects as well. A Warrior trained in wearing armor is going to be a serious threat to nearly any other opponent.

It also provides Plot Armor: spend a Luck Token and instead of taking harm, have your armor absorb ALL the damage. Unless a Karma invocation is used — if the damage exceeds the armor's rating, it is destroyed and you receive some the Harm that was absorbed into your character. After combat, if the GM grants a recovery roll for Complications (and they usually should), armor's rating stays reduced until repaired.

After combat, if the GM grants a recovery roll for Complications (and they usually should), the second one converts some remaining Injury back to Complications that persist until the next successful physical recovery.

Weapons

Without weapons, characters' combat defaults to Complications (bruised and winded); it's nothing that won't fade after the fight. Characters can still try to cause Injury deliberately by using Focus (the "called shots" Wager rule), even when unarmed — narrate the justification, such as trapping a punch and overextending the elbow to break it.

Weapons make conflicts more dangerous by changing the normal default to Complications. Some Harm, especially with cutting or piercing weapons, will naturally become Injury. You can use Focus to inflict Injury deliberately; you can use the called shot rule to cause Injury even when unarmed — narrate the justification.

Maneuvers in weapon training add to attack rolls and can apply to active defense Focus effects. Note that most sword-sized weapons count against shorter weapons like knives and most polearms count against swords.

Significantly longer reach weapons allow their user to gain an Inside Reach boost and negate the reach advantage; held out of range, opponents can't make melee attacks against them without first overcoming that reach — they have to close the distance first. Note that most sword-sized weapons count for a reach advantage against shorter weapons like knives and most polearms count against most swords.
