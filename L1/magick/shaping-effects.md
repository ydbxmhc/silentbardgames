# Shaping Effects: Adding Traits

[Traits](../traits.md) like *Area*, *Insidious*, or *Inexorable* make an
effect bigger, sneakier, or harder to escape. A caster doesn't get that for
free. Adding a Trait adds *difficulty* - and each kind of caster pays for it
in the currency that fits what they *are*. The principle is shared; only the
coin differs.

## Spellweavers Pay in Control

A Spellweaver manipulates external formulae, so the danger is that a more
complex working slips its leash. Adding a Trait makes a spell *Difficult*:
each Trait that makes it more useful or dangerous raises the casting
difficulty by one (limiting Traits subtract). The caster makes a free-action
roll to hold the altered spell together; succeed and it casts normally, with
the Traits in effect. Fail and the spell **destabilizes and is lost** until
re-bound - which is never possible mid-fight. This is the existing
[Difficult-spell](spells.md) rule; it is the Spellweaver's whole relationship
with Traits. The risk is front-loaded and binary, but it is paid back in
flexibility of power.

## Adepts Pay in Their Own Flesh

An Adept *is* the magic, so pushing it harder strains the vessel. Every Trait
added lowers the Adept's [Power Threshold](../roles/adept.md) - the safe
ceiling of what they can channel. The effect still happens; but the further
the result clears that lowered Threshold, the more
[Overchanneling](../action/adept.md) Harm potentially arcs back through them,
resisted by Endurance. An Adept can always choose to hold the result down to a
safer level if they prefer, or just roll the Overchanneling and take the
gamble. Because each Trait lowers the ceiling, the gambit is self-limiting:
the more you shape the effect, the smaller the slice you can throw without
risk of burning. The cost is paid as you go, in a currency that recovery might
reduce at the end of the scene - or kill you, if you push a low-Level Adept
too far.

## Conjurors Pay in the Relationship

A Conjuror's power is neither internal nor formulaic - it lives in the spirit,
the bargain, and [the Accords](conjuring.md) , and the strength of their own
will and soul. A Conjuror pays for Traits in the relationship, or in scars on
his psyche rather than body or merely his time and opportunity. There are two
cases, because Conjurors do two different kinds of work.

### Direct Conflict

When the Conjuror builds Traits into a Working of their own - a
[Ward](../roles/conjuror.md) or [Rebuke](../roles/conjuror.md) - then like the
Spellweaver they must resist the difficulty they have accumulated; like the
Adept they risk Harm, but from *Soulburn* as they pit their aura directly
against the power of an otherworldly will. Unresisted exposure of this sort is
taken as a *Soulburn* [Hindrance](../types-of-harm.md) which weakens the
Conjuror's interactions with any spirit until *resolved*. At recovery time
after the conflict, the Conjuror and the GM can negotiate converting the
Soulburn into an [Imposed Hook](../hooks.md) , which can then be dealt with
like any Imposed Hook.

Note that useful [Traits](../traits.md) for Conjuror actions might not be what
you expect, though they can usually be treated like their more typical
physical counterparts. *Area* Wards force spirits to honor physical distance
and locality if applied to spatial concepts, but the *concepts* are what
matter - *Guidable* isn't applicable. *Selective* often does not apply, as a
Ward which "only affects spirits" is the norm, but it also doesn't *have* to
take a Trait for "affects insubstantial spirits," so that's a wash; narrow it
to "only demons" for a difficulty reduction. *Inexorable* (the sealed
salt-circle) and *Persistent* (a standing wall that needs no concentration,
without a [Wager](../wagered-actions.md) ) might be useful, but a spirit who
chooses to breach an Inexorable Ward must simply accept that portion of the
effect as Harm, since it cannot be resisted. *Area* Rebuke might affect
several spirits (or Halflings!) and *Inexorable* could apply, but *Guidable*
and *Persistent* do not; a Rebuke is pure will, not something that is
constructed and then directed about.

### Borrowed Power

When a spirit produces an effect on the Conjuror's behalf, its own nature
governs how the effect is shaped. The Conjuror's cost for demanding more is
always a negotiation. A bigger ask is a steeper bargain, paid in offerings,
[Karma](../karma.md) , or [Luck Tokens](../luck.md) spent as bargaining chips.
This can be negotiated in advance for appropriately increased cost, or on the
fly at a *much* higher price. Pay it and the spirit obliges; refuse and it
declines or immediately renegotiates, which is an action spent. If you try to
compel the work, the steeper demand gives the spirit a stronger chance to
pervert or slip the terms, scaling with the Traits you force on it. The
failure here is unmistakably a Conjuror's own: not self-harm, not a lost
spell, but a recalcitrant, angry, literal-minded, or loosed spirit.

> **The Shared Shape**
>
> Three currencies, one bargain with reality: the Spellweaver risks *losing the working*, the Adept risks *burning herself*, the Conjuror risks *the spirit's price or its treachery*. In every case, a more powerful effect costs more difficulty, and a roll stands between the caster and the consequence. Pick the Trait you want and decide, in your own coin, whether you can afford it.
