# Tricks - Your Signature Moves

Where a [Hook](hooks.md) says what your character cares about, a Trick is what
they do about it. It's a named, regular *thing* they fall back on. That's it.

Tricks don't generally change the rules, though they might bend them a little.
When you define a Trick, it gives the GM an excuse for why they might
interpret those rules just a little differently in certain situations, but
generally it's just an example of how to apply rules-as-written that you reach
for when it fits.

A gambler might always read the table before the cards turn. A gunfighter
maybe always drifts to within two steps of cover. A doctor tries to always
have the right compound already mixed. None of that is a new power. It's just
who they are, written down so it runs smoothly at the table.

A Trick costs no [Karma](karma.md), because it grants nothing you don't
already own. It only declares, in advance, *how* you tend to apply the tools
already on your sheet: a free-action
[Boost](actions-and-timing.md#boost-actions) you always get for a known habit
in a certain situation, a [Wager](wagered-actions.md) you habitually make, or
maybe a common circumstance where two [Roles](roles.md) would routinely
combine. Naming that habit makes the character clearer in your own mind, gives
the GM a handle to reward, and turns "what can I do here?" into "here's the
thing I always do."

## What a Trick Is - and Isn't

A Trick reorganizes what you can already do; it rarely adds to it. The
advantage is almost always *opportunity-specific*, and is just your own EL -
your roll, with a [Boost](actions-and-timing.md#boost-actions) you actually
set up, points you actually [Wagered](wagered-actions.md), etc. The Trick is
just a quick reference, a recorded excuse for the GM to allow a minor
advantage in a fitting moment.

> **A Trick Is Not a New Power**
>
> Like [Hooks](hooks.md), Tricks are subject to the GM's approval. A good one is
> *specific to a circumstance* and uses *only abilities the character actually
> has*. If it would let you do something your [Roles](roles.md) and
> [Maneuvers](maneuvers.md) can't justify, it isn't a Trick - it's a power, and
> a power costs [Karma](karma.md).

## Tricks Don't Rank

A Trick carries no number of its own. Any bonus always comes from the
character's own rolls. If it wants a rank of its own, it's a
[Maneuver](maneuvers.md).

## The Three Marks of a Good Trick

- **It comes from who you are.** It reads as a personality trait, not a build choice. If you can't say it as a statement of *behavior* it isn't a Trick.
- **It's situational.** There's a clear, declared trigger. It applies when the moment fits, not always and everywhere. A constant behavior is a [Hook](hooks.md) . A Trick the Warrior uses in every fight becomes a reputation someone could also use to set him up for a Boost of their own...
- **It names the mechanism.** State the rule it invokes - a specific [Boost](actions-and-timing.md#boost-actions) , a [Wager](wagered-actions.md) , a [split](multiple-actions.md) , a free action, a cross-Role combination, etc. Good tricks often exist just to justify a GM call that bends the rules a *little* , explaining why *this* case warrants Synergy or a free-action Boost.

## How Many, and What to Call Them

None are required. Two or three to start is typical, and more will surface in
play as a character reveals who they are - the GM can allow them freely,
because they cost nothing and break nothing. "Tricks" is only the default
name; a setting or a single character is welcome to rename them to fit - a
gunslinger's *habits*, a duelist's *forms*, a courtier's *graces* - the
mechanic is the same either way.

## Examples

### Combat Tricks

**Feint**  
Simple Boost - Success creates an opening; each point of success adds directly
to EL on the next combat action against that opponent.

**Power Attack**  
Attack made Contingent on an Athlete (Strength) Boost. If the Boost fails, the
attack is forfeit.

**Grapple**  
Wagered Persistent Boost - Set aside N points of EL; if remaining EL succeeds
on the exchange, opponent is *Grappled*. The wagered dice count as a Boost
that can be applied in whole or in part to subsequent attack and defense rolls
until released, or the victim wins a roll to get free. (Note that a skilled
Grappler can *reverse* such holds...)

**Lock**  
Follows a successful Grapple - an attack that applies a Hindrance limiting the
opponent's movement. With a Lock held, victim cannot escape until they win a
roll specifically to get free - not just any success.

**The Breaker**  
Wagered Injury to joints. Usable as a response to an opponent's attack, or
when the PC already has a Lock.

**The Pavement Was His Enemy**  
Brawling Wager that applies Injury on success. Doesn't require a Lock or an
active attacker, but does require a wall, paving stones, or a suitable
substitute to be present - environment as weapon.

### General Examples

**Test the Blade**  
Spend the first exchange of a fight reading the opposition's guard. Study
their style as a Boost to the next attack. Caution worn as habit; better to
learn a man than trade blows with him blind. This does no Harm, but does count
as opposition. On a win, Wager the margin on the next roll for a Persistent
Boost for the rest of the fight.

**The Opener** (Spellweaver - Release into a fast follow-up)  
On the first round of a fight, Maerin Releases her bound Light as a free
action: no improvisation, just the guaranteed 2, taken as a Complication or a
*Dazzled* Hindrance. The Release consumes the spell and frees its Spellweaver
rank, so the strike that follows lands at a higher EL. She casts Stone Shape
on the spot, drawing a blade from any nearby stone and driving it home. The
target may resist the Light for free; if the Dazzle sticks, it drags down
their defense against what's coming. With luck there's no second exchange -
and time afterward to re-bind the Light.  
(On the deck of a ship, she might flip them just to boost the Light as her
best option...)

**I Recognize This!**  
Faced with something uncanny, Iris's first action is to spend an exchange to
name it: this is a basic knowledge/deduction roll that, on success, allows PCs
with relevant skills a free action roll for a Boost of their own against the
thing, because now they know its vulnerability - salt, iron, whatever she can
tell them. Truly obscure, alien, and/or unnameable horrors, or even those
intelligent enough to know they should dissemble, can roll against her. An
intelligent opposition might create a Boost of its own...

### Genre Examples

**Western: The Bounty Hunter**

*The Bead* - within talking distance, she steps out with her rifle up and
[Wagers](wagered-actions.md) points of her EL as a persistent *Aim* Boost.
Once established it adds to *both* the roll to make the target stand down
*and* the shot itself if talk fails. When she has the chance, she'll use
[Sneak](roles/sneak.md) to Boost the Aim roll first.

*Mercy Shot* - to take a man alive, she [Wagers](wagered-actions.md) up to her
[Sniper](roles/sniper.md) rank as dedicated [Hindrance](types-of-harm.md)
rather than possible Injury: a round through the arm or knee to stop him
drawing or running. The points come off her EL first, so choosing "alive"
genuinely costs her the surer shot - and a bullet is a poor tool for mercy, so
it's never "safe."

**Western: The Gunslinger**

*No Fear* - a gunslinger holds his opponent's eyes with a smirk while the guns
come out. His [Intimidate](roles/performer.md) rides along as a [free-action
Boost](actions-and-timing.md#boost-actions) in the exchange, because the
composure costs him no setup; it's simply his manner.

*Showboat* - the gunslinger stacks his [Sniper](roles/sniper.md),
[Performer](roles/performer.md), and Pistol Maneuver into one show of spinning
irons; a display like this can even fold his Pistol Maneuver into an
[Intimidate](roles/performer.md) roll (at the risk of getting shot for
drawing.)

*The Two-Step* - always hoping to stay two steps ahead of an ambush, the
gunfighter keeps within two steps of *some* kind of cover by habit. Unless
completely surprised, he may roll a free-action [Wager](wagered-actions.md)
for a *Cover* Boost at the start of an ambush, getting something solid between
himself and the guns unless there simply isn't anything available.

**Sci-Fi: The Engineer**

*Cyber-HUD* - On his own ship, every system has been custom-interfaced with
known iconography, specific locations in his field of view, and macros for
fast response on any problem he's had before. During any shipwide emergency,
Taro can roll a free-action Boost on a response, even if he's not at a
console. Note that it's not the equipment giving him the bonus, it's his work
*customizing*. Bonuses from high-tech [Gear](gear.md) with the [assist
rule](assist-rule.md) would be in addition.
