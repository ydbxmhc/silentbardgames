# The Kamikaze Blitz

An optional rule for the desperate, the dedicated, and the doomed.

We've said that [opposition](actions-and-timing.md#direct-opposition) is a
*compression* - a single contest standing in for what is really a separate
attack and a separate defense, rolled the long way. Most of the time the
compression is exactly what you want: it's fast, and it quietly assumes both
fighters are trying not to die. But sometimes a character *isn't*. The zealot,
the cornered guardian, the warrior who has chosen an honorable death - for
them, the Blitz takes the system at its word and opts *out* of the
compression.

## Opting Out of Opposition

Any combatant may declare that an engagement resolves as **pure
attack-and-defense** instead of opposition. The actor declaring it forces this
for that pairing - you cannot make someone's offense protect them against your
abandon. When opted out:

- **Your attack dice no longer protect you.** Only points you actually allocated to *defense* can soak a hit. In normal opposition your offense does double duty; opt out and that free protection is gone.
- Each side's attack is rolled against the other's defense, separately. **Both fighters can land full effect in the same beat** - the mutual kill, the *ai-uchi* , that ordinary opposition can never produce.

The result is more swing, more lethality, more dependence on luck. This is
*not* a free statistical equivalent of opposition; opposition is genuinely
*safer*, because offense pulls double duty there. Opting out trades that
safety for the chance to trade blood for blood. The lethality is the point.

## The Blitz

The Blitz is the all-or-nothing version: **allocate zero defense and pour your
entire budget into one attack.** Offering no defense at all is so far outside
what any sane opponent plans for that it works like a kind of ambush - nobody
expects a foe to simply *let* themselves be killed. That shock earns you an
instantly available, no-preparation **free-action Boost**:

**Roll a Boost right now, as a free action, and add its successes as bonus
dice to your EL for the attack.** This genuinely raises your ceiling - an EL6
strike with a 4-success Boost is rolled, and kept, at EL10. Its value scales
with your own skill, so a dangerous fighter's death-strike is genuinely
dangerous, while a mook's flailing sacrifice mostly just gets him killed.
([Extra Effort](dice.md) is a separate, stackable choice with its own
overshoot risk; you may layer it on the Boost or the attack, but it is not the
same thing.)

Against a foe who guards, the Blitz is largely soaked - but a pure defender
deals little back, since defense dice are dice not spent attacking. Push the
defense knob up and the whole engagement gets *less* lethal for everyone; two
Blitzers, on the other hand, slaughter each other. That is the gambit's honest
math: you make yourself impossible to ignore, and you accept that you will
probably not survive being right.

> **Worked Example - Ai-uchi at the Daimyo's Door**
>
> **Kazuo** (Level 2 / Warrior 2 / Swords 2 → EL6), no armor, his wakizashi
> broken and his luck nearly spent, will not let the assassin reach his lord.
> **Komi** (Level 3 / Warrior 3 / Katana 3 → EL9) is armored and known by
> reputation. Kazuo is outclassed and knows it, so after two careful exchanges
> he chooses an honorable *ai-uchi* over certain defeat.
>
> He rolls his Kamikaze Boost with 2 [Extra Effort](dice.md) dice for a **4**,
> adds it to his EL, and attacks at **EL10** (with 4 more Extra Effort dice) for
> an **8**. Komi, expecting a man to fight for his life rather than throw it
> away, has split normally: **4 attack, 5 defense**. Because Kazuo opted out,
> it's pure attack-and-defense:
>
> Komi's blade goes into Kazuo's shoulder - Kazuo doesn't even try to deflect
> it. Komi's 4 attack rolls a **2**; with no defense, Kazuo takes 2 - his Down
> threshold is 3, so that leaves him standing, barely. Kazuo's **8** lands;
> Komi's 5 defense rolls only **3**, leaving **5** on his one-point men-yoroi
> mask - **4** gets through, past his Down-3. Unless Komi spends a [Luck
> Token](luck.md) for [Plot Armor!](gear.md), the younger samurai has a story to
> tell about the night he saved his daimyo.

**For the GM:** when a player character pulls this off against a worthy foe,
think hard before spending an NPC's [Luck Token](luck.md) to cheat the ending.
The Blitz is meant to earn its win. If no one ever dares it, it isn't doing
its job.
