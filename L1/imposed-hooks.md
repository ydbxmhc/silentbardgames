# Imposed Hooks

Most [Hooks](hooks.md) are yours from the first scene - things you chose,
personal drives you built the character around - but the world pushes back,
and now and then it pushes a Hook *into* you. A curse, a slow poison, a
spell of terror or fascination, a spirit's patient whisper... while most
[Harm](harm.md) that takes your *agency* rather than your health would
usually land as a Hindrance, some, especially compulsions, will more likely
be an **Imposed Hook**: a forced condition that behaves like a Hook, because
it *is* one... just not one you chose.

## What It Is

An Imposed Hook is a real [Hook](hooks.md) in every way but one. It has a
rank, it matters in some situations and not others, and it counts against
you. Others may [exploit](hooks.md) it. The frightened soldier is easier to
rout, the charmed courtier easier to fool, the addict easier to bait. Someone has added a lever on you and the handle points the wrong way.

The single difference from an ordinary Hook: **an Imposed Hook earns you no
[Karma](karma.md).** It is not yet yours; it was done *to* you, and the game
does not pay you for being a victim. 

Not yet. Karma is earned.

## Imposing Is Not Commanding

Winning the roll that imposes a Hook is not the same as making you act on
it. When a mage's *"Drop Your Sword!"* overwhelms your attack, the
magic has thwarted that one swing - the psychic wave washed over you and the
blow never landed, but the sword is still in your hand. Prying it loose is a
separate action, and you still have the *option* to
[resist or oppose](resistance-and-opposition.md) it like any other. The mage
has laid the glamour on you, but now he must invoke it, and that means the
rolling of dice, ratings against ratings; he just gets to add the Hook's
rank when it applies. 

Would it make more sense to have imposed the Harm as a Hindrance to
resisting his commands? Well, yeah, sure, but sometimes a Hook *is* the
right tool. When, you ask? *It depends.* We'll get back to that. For now, this example is simple, and fun.

## Adopting the Baby

*Here is the door, and it only opens from your side.*

Imposed Hooks provide no Karma (or Luck Tokens, but I'll skip that for now so we don't have to keep saying it.) Resisting *any* Hook provides no Karma.

The moment you stop merely *suffering* an Imposed Hook and *use* it, leaning
into it because it is, now, a thing your character would do, you *can* get
that awesome supply of sweet Karma (and yes, ok, Luck Tokens) *on demand* by [self-hooking](self-hooking-and-table-courtesy.md) on it like any normal Hook. The instant you do, it's *yours*. Adopted. Promoted to a full Hook:
[tap its bonus](hooks-how-they-work.md), rank it up, grind it down. It's
now honestly a part of who you are. Take the Karma, and you have adopted it.

Full stop.

Feign compliance with a charm to get close to the enchanter, tell yourself
it was a ruse, but you *fed* it to do so and took the Karma. The compulsion
was steering, if only a little. An enchanter who trusts a mind he beguiled
this way is a fool, but when you turn on him that Hook sits on *his* side of the roll, dragging at the hand, slowing the strike meant to end him. 

Kill him anyway? Good job! The Hook does not simply vanish.

## Now It's Yours

An adopted Hook is a living thing, and it's yours now. It may still be
[renamed](hooks-and-growth.md) as it grows or diminishes. Maybe it isn't
*Charmed* any longer, but **Grief and Remorse**: the same Hook,
internalized, reminding you what you did. You can refuse the Karma and try
to grind it away, or keep it and earn from carrying the weight, even feed
it and let it deepen. Your Hooks are a record of what the world has done to
you and how you made it your own, and adopted Hooks are exactly where those
meet.

## Getting Rid of It

You are never *required* to adopt anything. An Imposed Hook you refuse - decline the Karma, resist it when it rises, act against it wherever you can
- behaves like [any unfed Hook](hooks-and-growth.md): it lets you degrade it
by a rank each session it was triggered and resisted, and may be renamed as
it fades. *Must Keep It Secret* dwindles to *Habit of Silence*, to *Socially
Distant*, to nothing at all.

This is convalescence, not a cure-all. It costs real sessions of refusing what is easy, but the door out is always open.

This is also how a Conjuror sheds a [Soulburn](magick/shaping-effects.md)
that has set into an Imposed Hook, and how many lasting curses, addictions,
and fears can be overcome.

## A Worked Example - "Drop Your Sword"

The mage's glamour lands and you take *Drop Your Sword* as an Imposed Hook. His win thwarts your attack - the compulsion drowns it - but the blade stays in your grip.

Next turn he repeats the command, and this time he adds the Hook's rank to
his roll: the imposed compulsion activated. You could resist again, and if
you succeed the sword is yours for the round... but you've seen his mistake.
You *choose* to open your hand, call it self-hooking, and pocket the "free" [Karma](karma.md). Before the blade rings on the stones you step in and
put your gauntlet in his teeth.

The Karma was a gift you gave yourself. You walk away owning a brand-new,
faintly ridiculous, entirely genuine Hook: *Drop Your Sword*. Now every time
you find yourself in need of a long blade the GM grins and picks up a Karma
token to tempt you, and you are becoming known for the dramatic self-disarm
and the sucker punch that follows. The world handed you a compulsion; you
turned it into a *bit*.

That is the whole engine in one exchange: the world compels you, you seize the wheel, and the compulsion becomes character.
