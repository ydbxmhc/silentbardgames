# Hooks on Credit

## Starting Hooks Above Rank 1

Normally, [Hooks](hooks.html) start at rank 1 — free, no [Karma](karma.html) cost. They grow through play as they’re triggered and you choose to rank them up. Sometimes, though, a character concept demands a [Hook](hooks.html) that’s already deeply embedded in who they are before the story begins.

A knight whose entire identity is built around a sacred oath. A cursed heir who has lived with the curse for years. A soldier whose conditioning runs so deep it defines every choice they make. These aren’t rank 1 complications — they’re rank 3 or higher, central to the character from the first scene.

With GM approval, you can start a [Hook](hooks.html) at a rank higher than 1. For each rank above 1, you receive **one additional starting [Karma](karma.html)** to spend on [Levels](chargen.html), [Roles](roles.html), and [Maneuvers](maneuvers.html). A rank 3 [Hook](hooks.html) grants 2 extra [Karma](karma.html). A rank 4 [Hook](hooks.html) grants 3.

This is **[Karma](karma.html) on credit**. You’re borrowing against your future growth to be more capable now, in exchange for a [Hook](hooks.html) the GM will trigger *hard* and *often* from session one. The higher the rank, the bigger the lever the GM has to pull on you — and the bigger the bonus you get when the [Hook](hooks.html) works in your favor.

## The Catch

First, [Hooks](hooks.html) are still limited by [Level](core-mechanics.html). A higher starting rank [Hook](hooks.html) means you *have* to invest starting [Karma](karma.html) in Level to support it. With only 5 starting [Karma](karma.html), there’s not very far you can go.

Second, if you want to reduce a credited [Hook](hooks.html) — because the character is growing past it, fighting free of it, or simply tired of the complications — you must **pay back the [Karma](karma.html)**. Each rank you reduce costs 1 [Karma](karma.html), just as if you were [spending it on advancement](spending-karma.html).

You cannot decline a [Hook](hooks.html) event with a [Luck Token](luck.html) if you still owe [Karma](karma.html) on it. The credit must be repaid first. If you want to decline the [Hook](hooks.html), you have to pay the [Karma](karma.html) on the spot, or accept the trigger.

This means a character who takes a rank 3 [Hook](hooks.html) for the 2 extra starting [Karma](karma.html) and then immediately tries to shed it will spend their first 2 earned [Karma](karma.html) paying off the debt before they can reduce the [Hook](hooks.html) normally. During that time, the [Hook](hooks.html) fires at full rank, generating story, creating complications, and — yes — earning them the [Karma](karma.html) they need to pay it off. The system feeds itself.

## Why This Works

The player who embraces the [Hook](hooks.html) gets the best deal. The complications fire, they play into them, they earn [Karma](karma.html), and they become more capable in both directions — more skilled AND more defined by their story. The [Hook](hooks.html) is an engine that drives their narrative and funds their growth simultaneously.

The player who wants to break free of the [Hook](hooks.html) can do it — but it costs real progression. Every [Karma](karma.html) spent on reducing the [Hook](hooks.html) is [Karma](karma.html) not spent on Levels and [Roles](roles.html). They’ll be mechanically behind their peers for a while, and that’s the price of freedom. That’s a valid and sometimes powerful character arc.

## Guidelines

Not every [Hook](hooks.html) qualifies for credit. The GM and table should agree that the [Hook](hooks.html) will create **real, frequent, meaningful complications**. A [Hook](hooks.html) that’s mostly advantageous — “Devastatingly Attractive” in a social campaign — doesn’t earn credit unless the GM commits to making it a genuine problem at least as often as it helps.

**Examples:**

*A young prodigy* takes “Too Much to Prove” at rank 2 for 1 extra [Karma](karma.html). She’s talented beyond her years, but she takes unnecessary risks, can’t back down from challenges, and alienates allies with her arrogance. The [Hook](hooks.html) drives her into trouble and earns her [Karma](karma.html) to grow — which eventually lets her mature past it, if she chooses to pay the cost.

*A bound spirit* takes “Chained to the Amulet” at rank 3 for 2 extra [Karma](karma.html). It’s powerful — far more capable than a starting character normally would be — but it cannot act freely, cannot refuse the amulet’s bearer, and suffers when the bearer’s goals conflict with its nature. The GM has an enormous lever. The spirit’s player has signed up for a story about servitude and the price of power.
