# Resistance and Opposition

Of course NPCs fight back, but even inanimate objects can present obstacles. A
simple, well-oiled lock has low EL; a complex or rusted mechanism is harder.
Everything pushes back - when you try something hard, roll your EL against the
task's EL.

If you roll a successful 7 and the task rolls a 4, you get effect 3. It's the
same fighting a goblin, picking a lock, or sweet-talking a guard, with one
very important difference: some things just resist, but others hit back. (You
*can* roll over EL and fail outright; we'll explain soon.)

![Resistance](Resist-cropped.jpg)

Resistance is passive. It never uses your turn, but also doesn't generate
effect. If a locked door isn't trapped it's not likely to hurt you, though it
still has a job to do. When Resistance wins a roll all effect is canceled out;
this is the same whether something is resisting the actions of your character,
or your character is the one resisting. Locks don't open, rocks won't budge,
or poison just doesn't work. It might be boring or frustrating, but it's
simple.

Opposition means whatever you are trying to affect is trying to affect you
back and it's a contest. The difference becomes the winner's effect. Sometimes
the door is trapped. If your opposition rolls higher, they get effect against
you!

This absolutely means that on "your turn" the goblin can hurt you, but the
important distinction is that Resistance doesn't use a turn - it's virtually
always a free action. If the goblin hits you on your turn it's because they
spent their turn opposing you, and it takes skill and planning to get another
turn. (You can get multiple turns if you're good enough - that's called
Splitting, we'll get to that in a bit.)
