Roles

Roles — What You Do Well

If Hooks define what drives your character, Roles define what they're capable of. They're broad thematic competencies — not job descriptions, but skillsets, but character has developed. We've put these off long enough, but by now you should understand the roles well enough to understand seeing them in action.

The Nature of Roles

Roles are intentionally flexible. "Warrior" doesn't mean "sword-wielding knight" — it means "someone who solves problems through skilled violence." A warrior can be a scarred tavern brawler, a foppish but deadly duelist, or someone raking naked berserker with an axe. The Role provides the mechanical framework; you provide the specific expression.

This flexibility serves both narrative and mechanical purposes. Narratively, it lets you build exactly the character concept you want without being forced into preset packages. Mechanically, it means Roles can combine in unexpected ways to create unique or standard archetypes.

They are also intentionally limited. Roles are not labels that hand you a lot of different abilities; a Role does one thing. Warriors brawl. Snipers shoot. Sneaks… sneak. There's a lot going on there, though: a warrior can usefully pick up any standard weapon and use it effectively — or a chair, for that matter. It doesn't make them an expert with any particular weapon, but keep reading!

Mechanical Function

Every Role provides two things: Narrative Permission, and Improved Effectiveness.

Some things need a Role to even attempt. A Loremaster might recall obscure historical details; others are limited to common knowledge. Only a Spellweaver can learn and cast spells.

When your Role applies to a situation, add its rank to your EL. This scales you significantly better at the things your character does. Anyone can attempt a lot of things, but a Warrior adds all their Role ranks, and has a few other tricks as well.

Within a Role you can learn relevant skills to improve your use of Role-specific behaviors. Warriors can learn to use swords effectively; everyone else is just faking it.

Class-ic Tropes?

Yes, put-intended, but the point remains. Most any standard character types from another system can be created here using the standard Roles and a little creative presentation.

Maneuvers — Specialization Within Roles

Each rank in any Role comes with a relevant Maneuver — a specific trick that improves the function of the Role when applicable. Every Role has some typical examples a character might want to use, but players are welcome to invent new ones and negotiate the details with their group.

The Sweet Spot: Not Too Broad, Not Too Narrow

The key to good Maneuvers is conceptual coherence — they should represent a meaningful area of expertise with sensible boundaries.

Blades is too broad — it straight razors and halberds both have blades, but addresses nothing common in how they are used. Broadsword is too narrow — it excludes sabers, cutlasses, and battlefield rapiers for no good reason. Swords is just about right — it leverages the shared principles of edge work: distance, timing, and technique, and though it won't teach the character uses of katana, epee, or khopesh, it could reasonably be used to make any of those effective.

Maneuvers must be:

  • Specific enough to create clear situations where they apply
  • Broad enough to be useful regularly throughout play
  • Thematically consistent with your Role and character concept

Maneuvers should be:

  • Helpful — don't waste Maneuver ranks on rare options
  • Interesting — they should support character development
  • Clear — make sure player and GM have the same idea

Maneuvers can be:

  • Simple — most Maneuvers just add their rank to EL when applicable
  • Tactical — it's fine for a Maneuver to have a cost for an extra benefit
  • Creative — who says Warriors can't have Flaming Rage?

How They Work

When your Maneuver applies to a situation, add its rank to your EL per rank in the Maneuver. You absorb more than one Maneuver if more than once to rack it up!

You can buy additional ranks with Karma, exactly like Roles, though you never have to. Maneuvers can never exceed their associated Role rank, and in general a character shouldn't have more Maneuvers than their rank in the associated Role.

Examples in Practice

A Warrior with Swords 2 gains +2 EL when fighting with any sword — broadsword, rapier, even an unfamiliar katana (though they might use it differently than intended.) The Maneuver represents understanding of blade work that transfers between weapons in the category.

A Performer with Stage Presence 1 gains +1 EL when commanding attention in social situations — whether entertaining a crowd, intimidating bandits, or rallying troops before battle.

Maneuvers are how you customize a Role to match your character concept. Want your knight to specialize in sword-and-board combat? Take ranks in Swords and Shields. Prefer a scholarly warrior-philosopher? Try Battlefield Tactics and Unshakeable Morale.

Role Synergy

Armor and a sword alone do not a knight make! Planning to joust? Obviously you'll want ranks with a lance… and some ranks of Athlete(Horseman). Your opponent is probably adding his skill at riding to his roll, and it might give him the win even if you're better with your lance.

The Role explanations below list a few synergy examples, but players and GMs should negotiate any that reasonably apply. If it makes sense, do it!

Combat Roles

Lots of characters fight, but if they do it well they are probably using one of these.

  • Combat: Warrior, Sniper
  • Social: Performer, Sneak
  • Outdoorsman: Naturalist, Beast Handler, Athlete
  • Knowledge & Craft: Healer, Loremaster, Tinker
  • Supernatural: Adept, Spellweaver, Conjuror
