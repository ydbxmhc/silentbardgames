Roles

Roles — What You Do Well

If Hooks define what your character cares about, Roles define what they're good at. They're broad thematic competencies — not job descriptions, not rigid class features, but flexible frameworks for the ways your character interacts with the world.

The Nature of Roles

Roles are intentionally flexible. "Warrior" doesn't mean "sword-wielding knight" — it means a brawler, a fencer, a tavern tough who's survived more fights than you've had hot meals, or a formally trained soldier who knows positioning and formations. The Role applies to any situation where understanding violence: timing, distance, leverage, and the will to survive them — actually matters.

This flexibility serves both narrative and mechanical purposes. Narratively, it lets you build exactly the character concept you want without being forced into preset packages. Mechanically, it means Roles can combine in unexpected ways to create unique character expressions.

They are also intentionally limited. Roles aren't labels that hand you infinite ability — they're specific areas of excellence. A Warrior can't substitute for a Loremaster when translating an ancient language just because they've traveled widely. A Performer can't pick a lock just because they've got nimble fingers — that's Tinker territory, or a Sneak Maneuver.

Class-ic Tropes?

Yes, it's intentional, and the point remains: most standard character types from other systems can be here using these Roles as needed, with a little creative application and sometimes a tiny bit of creative presentation.

Mechanical Function

Each Role provides two things:

  • Its rank is added directly to your EL whenever that Role's scope clearly applies to what you're doing
  • A list of Maneuvers — specific applications of the Role you've deliberately developed

Some things need a Role to attempt at all. A Loremaster might recall historical details; others can only add as much base Level as their rank in the relevant Role. More competent characters can attempt more things, but only with training in the right areas.

When your Role applies to a situation, add its rank to your EL. When it doesn't, you're working with just base Level (or not at all, if the skill requires Role training).

Maneuvers — Specialization Within Roles

Each rank of a Role comes with a free Maneuver — a named specific application that grants +1 EL when that particular approach is used.

The Sweet Spot: Not Too Broad, Not Too Narrow

Blades is too broad — it redundantly halves both have blades, but addresses something common in how they are used. Swords is just about narrow enough — it leverages the shared principles of edge weapons: timing, technique, and though it won't teach the character katana or khopesh, it could reasonably be used to make all of those effective. Broadsword is too narrow — it excludes sabers, cutlasses, and battlefield rapiers for no good reason.

Maneuvers must be:

  • Specific enough to create clear situations where they apply
  • Broad enough to be useful regularly throughout play
  • Thematically consistent with your Role and character concept

Maneuvers should be:

  • Helpful — don't waste Maneuver ranks on rare options
  • Interesting — they should support character development
  • Clear — make sure player and GM have the same idea

Maneuvers can be:

  • Simple — most Maneuvers just add their rank to EL when applicable
  • Tactical — it's fine for a Maneuver to have a small tactical cost for an extra benefit
  • Creative — who says Warriors can't have Flaming Rage?

How They Work

When your Maneuver applies to a situation, add its rank to the Role's rank, and the Role rank adds to Level. You may add more than one Maneuver if more than one clearly applies — a warrior using both Swords and Battlefield Tactics against an armored opponent is using both.

Maneuvers can never exceed the associated Role rank, and in general a character shouldn't have more Maneuvers than their Role rank (though this isn't strictly enforced — the GM is the final word).

Role Synergy

Armor and a sword alone don't make a knight! Planning to joust? Obviously you'll want ranks in Athlete (Horsemanship). Your opponent is probably also doing that, and it might give him the win even if you're better with your lance.

The Role explanations below list a few synergy examples, but players and GMs should negotiate any that reasonably apply. If it makes sense, do it!
