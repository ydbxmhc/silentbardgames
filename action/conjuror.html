Supernatural Roles

A Conjuror In Action

Mira sensed the grief before she saw the old man.

It struck her like a physical blow as she walked the cliffrop path above Gull's Rest - raw anguish so profound it surely polluted every spirit for miles around. She paused, and let him come to her with practiced ease, and immediately understood why the dead wife had been trying so desperately to reach her all morning.

Below, an elderly man trudged down the coastal road with grim purpose, clutching a crumpled letter. The locals watched from their windows as he moved along the blue cottages where Rob Henley lived alone since his stroke three years back. The man's spiritual aura blazed with grief and loss, the kind of emotional intensity that could sweep sleeping gods awake and draw hungry shadows.

He's going to kill him, whispered one observer. Look at that face. She could read their expectations from here, but she didn't think they were right.

His march was full of intention, but not belligerence. Mira could see the wife's spirit hovering anxiously beside the widower, translucent hands reaching furtively toward his shoulders as he stopped by the cottage and looked toward the address, then looked toward the letter in his hand - an envelope? He turned and walked away from the cottage without a second glance, stumbling down the sandy path to the beach where he fell heavily to his knees in the wet sand.

He began to weep. His aura crumpled from desperation, so far from any help his heart might stop.

She descended the path quietly. The wife's ghost turned to her with desperate gratitude - forty years of trying to comfort her husband, and finally someone who could serve as bridge between them.

"She wants you to know," Mira said gently, settling beside him in the sand, "that she's sorry."

The old man looked up, startled by this stranger who spoke as if she'd heard his private grief. "What?"

Mediating for spirits was often hard - some drifted so far beyond the daily bodily existence that their needs hardly translated into words anymore. "My name is Mira, and I know that you lost your wife. She -"

But Mira realized the woman's fear and remorse was distracting her. She took another look at this man's aura and saw that there was no anger in it. He was bitter, yes, and hurt, but not even close.

"You knew," she said. His lopsided smile bore no mirth as he nodded.

"Found the letter years ago. 'Twas me own fault for bein' to sea for so long. He'd expect?" "What do ya expect?" He turned his face back to the sea. She waited, and let him have the silence. Eventually he pointed back toward the cottage with a careless thumb. "At's his house. Heard he took it years back." He wiped his nose. "I should pro'ly look in on 'm, make sure he's ok."

This surprised Mira, but she held her silence. He eventually said "I can't remember years back." He sighed hard, and tried to continue. "Said the offshore of his house looked like her eyes."

She didn't know if she needed to say more; he fell to weeping and she waited, and then took his hand away from his face and held it. "Then why are you here?"

It took a long time before he could speak again - it took him halting - he haltingly told her "I jes' wanted to see 'im." He shook his head. "I ain't nearly as ready as I thought." He shook his head. "I ain't ready as me."

But he couldn't continue. Mira looked up at the spirit beside him, and could see the shock and relief and disbelief in it.

Spirit, she whispered, take my hand.

She was known to him by no more than his own love and the fear that he would find this very letter and know of her betrayal, decades past. As she gave herself to the reading, she lent it her strength, so the letter found him with lips and pressed it against his and closed her eyes. Oh, Bailey, she heard it whisper with her lips, you darling sweet man. I love you so.

He could only weep, because he could not deny the lips of his dead wife, whether it made sense or not. He felt the woman's hand touch her cheek, so softly. "I missed you." He leaned his brow onto her shoulder, and sighed, this time a sound of contentment, like all the breath left his body a final time.

Mira held him as the spirit released her to go with her husband into the beyond.
