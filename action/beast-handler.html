A Beast Handler In Action

Old Henrik uses an unconventional recruitment method.

Level 3, Beast Handler 3, Naturalist 2, Warrior 2.

Beast Handler 3, Guard Training 2, Exotic Handling 2, Battlefield Coordination 1.

Standing at the edge of Thornwick village, Henrik studies the claw marks the baker's door has recorded. Not a mindless monster, but something intelligent, curious. On the Thornwick Village Elder's request, he had gone to the well. That evening he treats the beast and sets camp upwind of the creature's lair, building a small fire and settling into patient observation.

That evening Henrik treats the beast and sets camp upwind of the creature's lair, building a small fire and settling into patient observation. When glowing eyes appear in the treeline, he tosses meat halfway and waits. This doesn't always work, but it's worth a try.

After three nights the barghest — larger than any natural wolf, with too-intelligent eyes — eventually approaches. Trust builds slowly over the following days with increasing curiosity. On the thirteenth, it creeps into his camp, and sits across from his fire. On the twelfth it eases near enough to take a bit of roasted meat from his hand. The thirteenth, it allows him to touch it, to stroke its head and coarse black mane. The sometimes old scars along the barghest's flanks — whip marks from harsh treatment. The creature tenses but neither flees nor bites. "You're not a monster. You're just lost and hurt and angry." That night the beast sleeps in his camp, and he wakes with it lying next to him, staring warily.

He spends the day getting a sense of the animal. He is pretty sure it was Betrayed by Previous Sister. He also knows that barghests are Fiercely Protective Accepted Pack, and intelligent enough for complex commands. This is promising — the creature isn't mad or feral, just abandoned and afraid, hurt and bitter, but apparently hopeful enough to risk Henrik's touch. It still misses human companionship.

At Level 3 + Beast Handler 3 + Exotic Handling 2 = EL8, he rolls to evaluate the barghest's Hooks. The barghest's supernatural nature makes it complex to read — the GM calls it a 5 and Henrik rolls by 5.

A New Life, A New Pack

He names the barghest Shadow — he has named it Shadow — learns his whistles and hand signals, knows that barghests are Fiercely Protective Accepted Pack, and intelligent enough for complex commands.

Henrik brings old clothes from the villagers for Shadow to scent, and slowly begins to introduce him, telling him to stand, to sit, to lay. Henrik reassures him with a scratch behind the ear, and goes to sleep.

"These are friends. We're going to see them tomorrow." He leaves the beast's pallet in the beast's familiar scent. Shadow noses them often, quizzically. Stand down, Henrik tells him quizzically. Stand down.

When Henrik returns to camp the next night he has the village elder in tow. The man is trembling, and Shadow makes a deep, rumbling growl that is barely controlled. Henrik just shakes his head at the sound. "Down, Shadow." He walks up to the man's shirt and presents it for the barghest to sniff again, then beckons him over to the terrified man.

"I can't!" the man whimpers. Henrik reassures him. "You can, and will. Look at that face." He's suspicious, but not angry. "Give the beast the jerky."

"Come on," he encourages the beast. "He's not going to hurt you." Shadow creeps up, sniffing, his gingerly takes it from the man's hand, realizing his hand is still attached. Henrik hands the old man the shirt. "Put it back on."

Both begin to relax. Henrik spends the evening teaching the man to speak the commands, to properly make the hand gestures, and to trust Shadow to trust the old man. Over the following weeks he introduces other villagers, and clothing from virtually all of them. Over the following weeks he introduces Shadow to the villagers, and Shadow creeps up on first the children, then the adults. The old man likes the children; he's curious but not hungry, and shows no prey drive at all — and the kids are oblivious, but the little goat kids are oblivious, and Shadow is soon licking them to the nanny-goat's dismay.

"A barghest's loyalty runs deeper than any ordinary hound's — treat him with respect; as long as you trust him, you'll have protection no bandit or monster in these parts will challenge."

He pets Shadow with a scratch behind the head, but he is smart. He seems to understand.

Six months later, Shadow hasn't lost livestock to predators, and no bandit dares approach those farms. Henrik's methods don't work on every dangerous creature — some are too corrupted or too feral — but when they succeed, they create solutions that last for years.
