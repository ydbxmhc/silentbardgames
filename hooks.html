Characters

Hooks

Hooks are your character's defining motivations — the personal drives, obligations, relationships, and flaws that make them more than a collection of stats.

Every character starts with one Hook at Rank 1 for free. Additional Hooks can be purchased with Karma (at the current rank cost, just like any other advancement).

The Nature of Hooks

A Hook is a short statement about who your character is and what they care about. It might be a personal code, a loyalty, an obsession, a fear, a relationship, or a philosophy. What makes it a Hook rather than just backstory is that it actively affects play.

Good Hooks have teeth. They don't just describe — they constrain, compel, and motivate.

Examples of Weak Hooks
  • Loves animals — too passive; what does it force you to do?
  • Was a soldier — that's backstory, not a drive
Examples of Strong Hooks
  • No one fights alone — compels dangerous loyalty; bonus when protecting allies; can't retreat while allies are in danger
  • The King's Debt — owed a debt to the crown; bonuses when serving royal interests; but they will call it in
  • Curiosity Killed the Cat — must investigate mysteries; bonus when researching or exploring; compels dangerous investigation
  • I Never Lie — bonus when the truth matters; creates constant dilemmas about honesty vs. survival

Mechanical Function

Each Hook has a Rank (starting at 1). The rank does two things:

  1. Bonus: Add the Hook's rank to any roll where that drive, obligation, or characteristic clearly applies. The GM may award this bonus even if you don't ask — they know your character.
  2. Karma Source: When a Hook causes a significant complication in a session, you earn 1 Karma. A Hook can be triggered for Karma a number of times per session equal to its rank. After that, further triggers yield Luck Tokens instead.

Designing Your Hook

Ask yourself these questions when designing a Hook:

  • What does my character refuse to walk away from?
  • What gets them in trouble even when they know better?
  • What would they sacrifice other goals for?
  • What are they trying to prove, protect, or become?

The best Hooks feel inevitable — like this character couldn't do anything else in this situation.

Hook Advancement

At the end of any session where a Hook was triggered, you may choose to increase its rank (paying Karma equal to the new rank). When you do, consider whether the Hook's description should also change — a Hook that started as "Revenge for my father" might become "I will not let the powerful prey on the weak" as the character evolves beyond their original motivation.

The GM can also propose changing or retiring a Hook if the character has grown past it or if it's no longer generating interesting stories.

Multiple Hooks

Characters can have more than one Hook. Additional Hooks beyond the first cost 1 Karma to acquire (at Rank 1). However, more Hooks means more complications — the GM has more handles on your character's story, and that's usually a good thing.

A character with three Hooks is rarely bored.
