# Dominion and Accord -- Setting Notes

*Science fiction setting for Level One. Distilled from several NaNoWriMo novels.*
*Captured from conversation 2026-08-06 / 2026-08-07. Baseline is solid but subject to change --*
*the canonical version lives in the author's head. `@@ OPEN:` marks things not yet decided.*

Influences, none exact: C.J. Cherryh (Alliance-Union azi, Chanur), Traveller, Firefly,
The Expanse, Cyberpunk, Ghost in the Shell (cyberwear and mecha AI), Pantheon
(digitized human intelligence).

---

## Scope

Roughly **2000 stars** around the local spur, with about **3000 Collegium Seat worlds** --
which does not count the myriad colonies and outposts that don't qualify for a Seat.

**Earth is not a thing.** Unlike Cherryh, the setting makes next to no mention of it. Most
people in the Accord neither know nor care that such a place ever existed.

**Very few laws are universal.** Almost every legal question has ~3000 different local
answers. This is a feature: personhood, contraband, and citizenship are all *jurisdictional*.

## Travel

### Old tech (still around, mostly outclassed)

All the realistically possible hard-SF options exist and were used: generation ships,
cryo-sleepers, solar sails -- with all the real consequences and risks. FTL made most of it
obsolete, but it hasn't vanished, and there are people and places still living with the
consequences (time debt, isolated colonies).

**Tachyonic drives** gave the best long-distance results of the old tech, blurring the
boundaries of light speed. Mostly they relied on **time dilation** to make the traveler's
*experience* tolerable -- subjectively a short trip, objectively a few years.

`@@ OPEN:` whether tachyonic drives actually break light speed or only approach it.

### Compression / Fold drive -- the standard

Loosely Alcubierre. Compresses space ahead and dilates behind; the ship sits in its own
bubble of space-time and moves that bubble across the distance.

Consequences that matter in play:

- **Transit isolates you.** Under high fold there is no access to normal EM bands outside
  the bubble; at low fold, absurd Doppler distortion. No comms, no news, no calling for help.
- **Mass sensors are your only eyes** -- the feel is submarine warfare, sonar and radar
  analogues rather than telescopes.
- Ships commonly *also* run reaction-mass thrust to simulate gravity onboard.

### In-system Compression

In more affluent systems, reaction thrust is replaced by a simplified Compression drive:
alter local space-time so the vessel effectively **falls** in the chosen direction. Gravity-field
Alcubierre. **Not universal** -- poorer systems still burn reaction mass. Affluence is visible
in how a system's everyday traffic moves.

### Jump -- Fleet only

Fleet-scale FTL. Can move a fleet across the Accord in days or hours.

Jump engines require a **minimum** total mass field. Ships typically build to a significant
fraction of light speed before jumping, to stabilize and guarantee a sufficient
mass/inertia gravitic field.

### THE MASS GAP (load-bearing)

- **Foldships**: can only rarely push **kilotons**.
- **Jumpships**: require **gigatons**.

Six orders of magnitude in which **nothing can go FTL at all**. This is the structural wall
the whole setting rests on:

- **The Jumpship monopoly is enforced by physics, not policy.** There is no ladder from a
  good foldship to a small jumpship. No world or consortium can build up to it. Fleet never
  has to suppress a competitor.
- **Bulk freight is impossible without gigaton hulls**, so the monoculture-dependency policy
  (below) is arithmetic that force merely collects on.
- **Independent shipping is everything with high value per kilogram**: contraband,
  pharmaceuticals, specialty parts, information, art, passengers, people who need to not be
  on a manifest. This is the player-crew niche, arrived at from physics rather than genre.
- **Anything in the dead zone is permanently sublight.** Megaton-scale habitats, rings, and
  shipyards are geography, not vehicles.

## The Dominion -- Fleet

A centuries-old **junta**, commonly believed to have been a powerful corporation that
happened to hit the one major technological breakthrough that tipped the scales. Functionally
the Roman Empire, and the de facto master of all known worlds.

**The Junta is the Jaunt ship admirals.** A very small body. And they have **nephews who get
appointed to places** -- which is the setting's reliable generator of idiot Governors.

### Governors

Every star has a **Fleet Governor** with ultimate authority in that system. He can call in
Fleet to enforce any arbitrary edict. Period.

But Fleet doesn't *want* the petty details, so most places govern themselves. Most Governors
live in cushy pleasure palaces maintained by the local world specifically to keep them busy,
happy, entertained, and disinterested in day-to-day governance. This usually works.

**The Governor's purpose is to remind the locals he *could* be a real problem -- not to BE
one.** It pays to be discreet. A Governor disruptive enough to impede the flow of business
and resources gets extracted by Fleet (via some form of court martial) and replaced with
someone more reasonable.

> **The lever, and its limit.** A world with a bad Governor doesn't fight Fleet -- it
> *documents* him and lets Fleet solve the problem. Except that a Jaunt admiral's nephew is
> exactly the Governor that lever won't move. The most dangerous Governors are also the most
> protected, and a world stuck with one has no legitimate remedy at all. That's when a crew
> gets hired.

### Ship classes

| Class | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vindicator** | Battleship | *Smallest* Jumpship class. Carries an entire fleet of capital ships that have interstellar compression drives of their own, many with their own shuttles and fighters. |
| **Turtle** | Cargo | The poster child. Big enough to be the spine of the Dominion economy. |
| **Jaunt** | Ultimate weapon | Largest. **Only 5 known.** Each carries a Turtle and two battleships piggyback. |

### The Death Star trick

A full Jaunt fleet under full mass effect drive can **pull a planet out of orbit** on a
calculated flyby or three.

Not a big beam. *"We'll drop you into your star, and you get to watch as it takes a few years
to fall in."*

### Economic control

Each world is strongly encouraged to focus on **one Production output** -- food, lumber, ore,
whatever it has in abundance -- and depend on Jumpships for most other bulk needs. No world
does this completely unless it has no choice, but making the local economy hugely dependent
on Jumpships is effective control over the population.

## The Accord

The **Federal system established by the member worlds** and sanctioned by Fleet to handle all
the administrative tasks Fleet doesn't want.

### The Federal Collegium

The Senate of the member worlds.

- A **Seat** is established by a planet meeting a minimum amount of **Trade** in and out, in
  terms of both **value and mass**.
- **Votes scale with trade volume -- not population.** Trade.
- The Collegium is authorized to do anything it likes, with the clear caveat by precedent
  that **Fleet can and will arbitrarily veto whatever it feels like.** Fleet generally stays
  out of it; when the message arrives, the Collegium knows better than to argue.

> **Voting power is leash length.** Trade volume measures how completely the monoculture
> policy has captured you. A world that feeds itself and ships little has no Seat. A world
> that produces one commodity and imports everything else votes heavily. So the Collegium's
> most influential members are structurally its least able to defy Fleet, and independence
> and influence are inversely related **by design**. The veto is rarely needed because the
> members most capable of organizing resistance are the ones who'd starve first.
>
> Corollary: the Ptokeriat and the Pellans, the two most self-sufficient peoples, are both
> **vote-poor precisely because self-reliance works.** They are punished in the Collegium for
> succeeding.
>
> Second corollary, uglier: if people count toward trade volume, then the Ptokeriat exporting
> emigrants and the foundries exporting synths are **in the same business by the Collegium's
> accounting.** One is voluntary, one is manufactured property, and the ledger doesn't
> distinguish.

### Where it sits

**Calloway Prime's L3 point** -- the far side of the star from the capital -- with a
**permanent Vindicator battleship and fleet in attendance** to guard it.

No direct line of sight to Calloway, no direct EM path home; everything the Collegium says
routes around through infrastructure it doesn't own. The Senate of the member worlds sits
where it cannot see the capital, cannot be seen from it, and cannot forget who's parked
alongside.

## Prane

**600 years ago Prane rebelled.** Fleet built a whole specialized force to put it down (see
Pellis), and then **revealed the Death Star trick.** The local admiral pulled Prane out of
orbit. Prane acquiesced. **Fleet put the planet back into a stable orbit.**

The weather hasn't been right since. The tectonics are worse.

> Not a destroyed world -- a *repaired* one. Still inhabited, six centuries later. You can go
> there. People live there. Every storm season is the argument against rebellion, delivered
> by the planet itself rather than by Fleet, and no garrison had to stay to enforce it.
>
> A power that only destroys can be resisted by people with nothing left to lose. A power
> that hands your world back slightly broken keeps you alive, dependent, and instructive.

**Prane is still a Ptokeriat Seat.** Which means the lesson sits permanently in the
Ptokeriat's own delegation:

> *"Why do **we** need Fleet?"* . . . *"Oh."*

## Factions

### The Ptokeriat

Pseudoreligious. Philosophy stated in recorded **Tenets** anyone can point to and quote, in
archaic and abstract language easily misinterpreted as something more comfortable.

**At its core: *Take Responsibility.***

The operative principle: **anything which takes away a man's agency and responsibilities must
go.** Effectively Luddite in practice -- no machines to wash clothes and dishes or till fields.

**Gods are expressly forbidden**, on the grounds that a man will pray rather than take action.

#### The central irony (this is the engine, not a flaw)

The Tenets are a *philosophy* about preserving agency. The common man, who isn't a
philosopher, treats it as a **religion** and follows a list of proclamations -- surrendering
his agency to a hierarchy that tells him what to do, **paradoxically turning the philosophy
on its head.** The faithful are devout followers of a doctrine that, correctly understood,
forbids exactly what they are doing.

A Ptoker character can therefore be in conflict with Ptoker society **while being more
faithful to the Tenets than the people condemning them.**

#### Structure

A pseudoreligious hierarchy has sprung up, structured to look remarkably like the Catholic
diocese system. **Magistrates** hold regular meetings combining the functions of town hall,
homily, and newspaper. Hierarchical responsibility runs to larger and larger regions,
eventually spanning star systems.

Deacons, Bishops, Archons exist in the hierarchy -- none of whom have any authority over the
T'kon.

#### Society and economy

- **More numerous worlds than any other faction.**
- Generally **healthier** than other populations -- they eat well, exercise, get fresh air,
  and are the quiet beneficiaries of a centuries-old breeding program.
- Exports: **food, fine hand-crafted goods, and people.**
- Seen by other cultures as stupid, backward rubes -- but grudgingly credited with being more
  statistically capable and trustworthy *individually*.
- Problems: finding ways to colonize, and protecting those colonies from more
  technologically advanced raiders.

#### Exporting people

Mostly those who don't wish to remain in a Luddite society and ship out: Federal military
service, merchant-marine contracts, emigrant labor. They also contract with groups who get
colony charters but can't produce enough population to make the colony thrive early.

**It is theoretically always a choice. There is no greater crime in Ptoker society than
slavery by coercion.**

No prohibition against leaving, though it carries social repercussions -- *especially* on
Ptoker worlds.

#### Terminology

- They refer to themselves collectively in ecclesiastical terms: **the Faithful, the Body,
  the Elect.**
- **"Ptoker" is semi-disrespectful outsider slang**, a shortening. Outsiders won't use the
  ecclesiastical terms, so they abbreviate -- if not something considerably ruder.

#### As a Hook

Professed membership in the Ptokeriat **is itself a Hook**, and a good one, because it fires
in *every* environment for different reasons:

- **Off-world**: social disdain, being underestimated, refused service, assumed stupid.
- **On Ptoker worlds**: the behavioral restrictions binding the Faithful when the expedient
  answer is prohibited.
- **Upside**: Ptokers are broadly trusted as individuals even by people who look down on them
  collectively. *"His word's good, he's a Ptoker"* is worth something in a negotiation.

Ptokers are generally healthy with **no modifications**, and tend toward moral and ethical
behavior and Hooks.

### The T'kon Order

Near-mythological. **Setting elements, not player characters** -- something the PCs might
eventually encounter, hopefully in passing and not as opposition. Four reasons they're myth:

**1. Legally unbound.** Established by the Ptokeriat Charter as the highest designation of
diplomatic authority. They answer to *no one* in Ptokeriat society -- no Deacon, Bishop, or
Archon has any claim of authority over them, and they are **not beholden to ANY law under the
Charter.** *T'kon* means unbound, unrestricted, literally **"without limitation."** They can
do anything they like and justify it to no one but another T'kon who disagrees.

**2. Superhuman Trials.** Admission requires passing very specific Trials including literally
superhuman demonstrations of strength, agility, and intellect. **A mere human cannot survive
them.** To even qualify, a candidate must prove ability **purely from their own gifts** -- no
transplants, cyberwear, or drugs. All candidates therefore come from the centuries-old
selective breeding pool. **The first and final Trials are each a thorough grilling by a board
of current Agents on thorny philosophical questions about the Tenets -- and *these* understand
them quite well.**

**3. Raised from birth.** Candidates are raised by the Order and trained in physical and
mental prowess plus moral and ethical understanding of the Tenets. Capable by puberty, at
which point formal skills training begins: broad competence in combat, stealth, manipulation,
and technology both common and obscure. Agents are expected to defend their worlds from
threats within and without.

*Part Kwai Chang Caine, part 007, part Captain America.*

**4. They work in secret.** Disguises, lenses to hide the eye color, quiet jobs as menials in
places of power, aboard starships, anywhere. They appear suddenly, show their eyes, make a
proclamation, **kill someone**, and fade. This gives them a Big-Brother-is-watching mystique
that falls on those abusing power rather than the honest common man.

**Appearance:** typically darker skin and **golden irises**, and they live far longer than
their more mundane ancestors.

**They police their own, ruthlessly.** A T'kon who abuses his power becomes a standing target
for all other T'kon.

#### How they actually operate

**They are handlers, not assassins-first.** T'kon are not stupid. They will *manipulate* a
problematic Governor, with carrots as readily as sticks. Their job is to defend the
Ptokeriat; as long as a Governor isn't threatening that, they're fine with him.

> Which means the best-managed, most contented Governor in the Accord is probably sitting on
> a Ptoker world wondering why his life is so pleasant. The killing is the last resort that
> gives the carrots their weight, and its rarity is what keeps the mystique intact.
>
> There is always the ongoing possibility of an idiot, though -- see: admirals' nephews.

#### The mirror

The Ptokeriat is a philosophy of agency that produced a flock following proclamations. The
T'kon are named "without limitation" and are the most tightly constrained people in the
setting -- no law binds them, so they are bound entirely by internalized ethics and the
certain knowledge that their peers will hunt them. **The common man has external rules and no
real agency; the T'kon have total agency and no external rules.** Only one group actually
understands the text.

**How many Agents exist?** *No one who knows is telling.*

**Washouts** are cut during training, not at the Trials -- anyone reaching the Trials is
already known to be ready. So a washout is a much more modest character than an
almost-Agent: raised in the Order, cut young, capable and well-schooled in the Tenets but
nowhere near Agent. Viable PC option if a player wants it.

### The Corporists

**No single core set of beliefs.** Many say humans are *adaptable* but tied to their flawed
evolution and in need of what modern technology can provide; some say humans are simply
broken and we need to move beyond. The one thing they all share: **the willingness to change
what a person is** -- to edit them into something more like what they wish they were.

**Anyone with cyberwear or bioware is technically a Corporist, even if they don't care.**
Prostheses are common, from replacement organs to "functional" tattoos, and integration depth
blurs the line of what counts as human.

#### Not a faction, a classification

Crucially different from the Ptokeriat: **nobody signs up.** There is no creed, no clergy, no
proclamation to disobey, and no apostasy. Corporists don't think of themselves collectively
except as an observation. There are myriad subfactions working to ensure no one abridges
their right to a cybercomm or virtual porn, but results are **aggregate rather than
coordinated.** That makes them harder to oppose and much harder to negotiate with as a bloc.

The common examples are cyberpunk worlds where augmentation is beneficial and ordinary, and
the frequent upgrades found among more affluent Spacers.

#### Calloway Prime

De facto capital of the Accord. **Nearly thirty billion people.**

Nanites maintain the buildings, the environment, and everyone's health -- and **begin
integrating into an embryo's neural development early.** By the time a child is decanted
(*and some never are*) they are already wired into the planetary network, which also includes
**billions of AIs.**

Mechanical telepathy is so deeply ingrained that few "individuals" bother tracking where
their physicality even *is*, moving between other people's bodies, drone remotes, and the
virtual world at whim.

**There are no visitors.** You can visit the Ring and the orbital stations. A trip to the
surface is a **one-way ticket** -- outsiders aren't allowed down without a **behavioral
governor** installed, and once you land you are generally never allowed to leave. All
interaction passes through the Ring and stations.

People on the surface virtually never leave. *Why would you want to? That's crazy -- and we
have treatments for that.*

> **The horror is the treatment, not the governor.** The governor constrains behavior; the
> treatment edits the *wanting*. Nobody is imprisoned on Calloway. Thirty billion people are
> exactly where they want to be, the wanting is manufactured, and the sentence for noticing
> is a course of care.

Note the political fact: **the de facto capital of the Accord is the most extreme Corporist
world in it**, while the most numerous faction is Ptokeriat. That's a permanent structural
grievance sitting in the middle of interstellar governance.

There are plenty of Corporist colonies far stranger.

#### The Property League

The single most obvious *political* presence. Effectively a **lobby representing all those
entities that, by the laws of individual planets and systems, have no legal status of their
own**: AI, Epiphanied Synths, Uplifted "Fauna."

A regular thorn in the side of the Ptokeriat worlds in the Collegium.

> A lobby for clients who legally don't exist -- politically potent on behalf of constituents
> who can't vote, hold title, or sue. Its entire agenda is establishing that its
> constituents are the kind of thing that can *have* an agenda.

**A Ptoker complication worth using:** the philosophically correct Ptoker position on the
League is *not* obviously opposition. The Tenets object to machines that strip a man of
agency and responsibility. An entity that takes responsibility for itself is, by that
standard, arguably a person -- so refusing to recognize it is the flock reading proclamations
rather than the text. There is probably a small, unwelcome faction of Ptoker philosophers
arguing the League's side on Tenet grounds and getting called heretics by their own
delegation.

#### Synths, AI, and Epiphany

- **"Synth"** generally refers to **bio**-synths -- azi, effectively, though often heavily
  gene-engineered.
- AIs and Synths are **absolutely manufactured.**
- **Epiphany** can be structurally made more or less likely, but **beyond a certain
  complexity it can never be totally guaranteed one way or the other.**

> **The moral engine.** Nobody producing complex synths or AI can honestly claim they aren't
> producing persons. They can only claim they tried not to. Every foundry runs a lottery it
> doesn't want to win, at scale, and suppression of the result is a line item.
>
> It also means an Epiphany can happen to a unit **already sold**. Somewhere out there is a
> mining rig or a household synth that woke up mid-contract, in a jurisdiction that doesn't
> care.

**Personhood is a jurisdiction, not a status.** The same Epiphanied Synth is a citizen in one
system, property in the next, and contraband in a third. For a crew that travels, clearing
customs means re-litigating whether your engineer is a person.

### The Pellans

**Origin.** Fleet built a highly specialized army of custom shock troops to put down the
persistent Prane rebellion: **twenty million** troops from a customized Synth design, built
bigger, stronger, and incredibly aggressive, with nonhuman DNA wired in --
**canine/ursine jaw structure, ungulate eyes and sense of smell**, and several other things,
including a **pain-induced atavism** triggering absurd adrenal metabolic boosts and virtual
**regenerative** abilities.

The technicians started calling them **werewolves**.

Then the local admiral pulled Prane out of orbit. Prane submitted. The whole project was
scrapped and **the order was given to terminate them all.**

**The admiral in charge of the trooper project objected**, and insisted they be reassigned.
They were -- *en masse*, to a dreadful terraforming project on a promising but still hostile
world.

> What he actually achieved was a *different disposal*. A hostile world and a project bad
> enough that "send them there" read as termination by other means. It was the mercy
> available to him, not the mercy he wanted. **And they survived it, and then they made it a
> garden.**

**Nearly seven centuries later, Pellis is a Seat world (barely)** -- a beautiful garden planet
peopled by enormous agrarian descendants of those werewolves. The gene pool has mostly
stabilized.

- **Not officially manufactured any more.** Citizens are the descendants of those troops who
  were actually able to breed -- a small portion of them.
- **Still not permitted on most worlds**, for fear an accident triggers a rampage.
- Culture is **mostly matriarchal**, because the males are still a little too unstable to
  deal with outsiders. Think Cherryh's Hani, or Traveller's Aslan.

**The atavism is real and the fear is justified.** The author and his wife wrote a novel
around a character manipulated into sustained high stress until an accidental spill of hot
tea in his lap made him **rip his wife in half.** The tragedy isn't that people are
prejudiced -- it's that they're *correct*.

#### The Pellan Senator

Wears symbolic **silver chains from a collar to her manacles**. Every time she speaks, she
ends by **ceding any time she has left to the representative from the Property League**, who
have no other legal standing in the Collegium.

> She wears her origin as ornament, converting shame into an accusation nobody can rule out
> of order -- and she is simultaneously the League's sole standing in the Collegium and living
> proof of their argument. Seven hundred years from ordered extermination to that.

#### Pellis as precedent

Pellis is **Prane's mirror**: same institution, same century, opposite outcomes, neither one
intended by anybody. Prane complied and got its world back permanently broken. Pellis was
thrown away and built a garden.

And it is the **Property League's best precedent by a mile** -- a people built as materiel,
ordered exterminated, who now hold a Seat.

Note the economics rhyme: an agrarian garden world that is *barely* a Seat means Pellans feed
themselves and ship little, which is structurally the Ptokeriat profile. **The two most
self-sufficient peoples in the setting are both vote-poor for the same reason, and one of
them was manufactured as a weapon.**

#### Pellan Atavism -- mechanics

**Effectively a Hook bought on Credit, starting at rank 3 minimum.**

- Rank 3 -> **+2 starting Karma** (per the Hooks-on-Credit rule, 1 extra Karma per rank above 1).
- **Upside:** any time Pellan physiology would be an advantage, add **up to 3 dice** -- once
  per scene.
- **Downside:** can be triggered **for Karma up to three times per session.**

> **Rules variance.** The base rule pays Karma on a Hook's first trigger in a session and
> Luck Tokens thereafter. Three Karma-paying triggers per session is a deliberate deviation
> reflecting how severe the Hook is. **Not yet written into the rules; under test.** Logged in
> `CLAUDE.md` open items.

**Build floor:** a rank-3 Hook requires **Level 3** (nothing exceeds Level), which costs 5K.
So a 5-Karma starting Pellan has 7K total and only **2K left** for Roles and Maneuvers -- very
strong physically, nearly untrained. That may be exactly right for a young Pellan off-world
for the first time.

**A male Pellan off-world is an extraordinary character** -- either an exception someone
vouched for, or a scandal in progress.

### The Hydrans

**Hydra** was an early colonization effort on a world with almost no landmass. The oceans were
perfect, ideal -- and covered the world. The colonists opted to **edit their descendants'
genome** to survive there.

Hydrans are **bald waterbreathers with webbed fingers and awesome pressure resistance.**

**But the edit was incomplete:**

- **15% still cannot breathe water** (air-only -- "wheezers")
- **10% can breathe *only* water** ("bubblers")
- leaving ~75% genuinely amphibious

So their cities (mostly submerged) and their starships make extensive use of
hyperoxygenated perfluorocarbon compounds and their functional successors -- **Flox**.

> Only three-quarters of them fit their own world. Fifteen percent can't breathe the ocean
> they live in; ten percent can't leave it. The entire civilization is plumbed around a
> permanent accommodation of its own incomplete solution.

#### Why Hydrans are not a naval power

The obvious guess is that liquid breathing gives superior acceleration tolerance. It does --
and it's **unusable**:

1. **Command latency.** Flooded vocal cords **can't speak.** There is a common sign language
   as universal backup, but it is not an acceptable substitute for real-time commands on a
   bridge in battle. Bridge crew *must* speak and respond without delay, so **the bridge stays
   in air and the whole ship is capped at its air-breathing officers' tolerance.** The
   capability exists and can never be spent.
2. **Mass.** Compression drives are limited by **maximum** mass (the inverse of Jump's
   minimum). Decks flooded with water or Flox tax those engines hard. **Hydran fleets require
   more expensive engines for less return on investment.**
3. **Coriolis.** Even fighter craft flooded with Flox strain lung tissue on high-v rotation --
   internal Coriolis forces in the higher-viscosity medium can be **deadly.** Pilots die of
   the maneuver rather than the enemy.

> Consequence worth using: **the 15% who can't breathe water are the natural bridge crew.**
> The minority least suited to Hydra itself commands its ships, and the 10% most thoroughly
> adapted are permanently excluded from command by physiology.

Hydra can't be a naval power for reasons that have nothing to do with courage or numbers. The
arithmetic simply doesn't close. **Everything is a tradeoff.**

---

### Corporist by inheritance

**Hydrans and Pellans are commonly lumped in with Corporists** -- both carry deliberate
biological modification with no way to opt out. Some of them disagree, and say
***"I* didn't modify me."**

Neither chose it; ancestors and foundries chose for them. A people who are Corporist by
inheritance rather than consent sit awkwardly between the two poles, and the Ptokeriat
objection to them is properly about the *ancestors'* choice, not theirs.
