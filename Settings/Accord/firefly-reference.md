*Not setting material. These are the worked examples used to calibrate the 10-Karma standard*
*and to sanity-check the translation method before building Accord pregens. Built 2026-08-07.*

The point of the exercise was to **make the actual characters on a few Karma**, rather than
weak approximations bounded by numerical limits. It's a narrative system; the numbers don't
need to skyrocket.

## Method

Start from the fiction, then choose mechanics that represent it. Roles have **narrow thematic
verbs** even though their narrative expression is flexible:

- **Loremaster knows. Sneak sneaks. Warrior hits. Sniper shoots.**

"Knows the undercity" is Loremaster, *not* Sneak. A Sneak Maneuver may encode undercity
knowledge only insofar as it serves sneaking -- hiding places, sight lines, patrol schedules.
Cross-Role application is the interesting exception, never the baseline.

Sci-fi lanes settled during this exercise:
- **Piloting** -> Athlete Maneuver
- **Navigation** -> Tinker Maneuver
- No new Roles needed.

## Wash -- Hoban Washburne

```
3K   Level 3              3 Luck Tokens
2K   Athlete 2            Pilot 2
1K   Performer 1          Wisecrack 1
1K   Tinker 1             Navigation 1
1K   Loremaster 1         Spacer's Ear 1

Hook: Glib & Gifted Pilot, Otherwise Mundane 1 (free)

Tricks:
     Leaf on the Wind -- In the pilot's seat, a free-action
       Athlete(Pilot) Boost to any shipboard action that depends
       on ship positioning: evasion, pursuit, lining up a shot
       for someone else.

     Dinosaur Wisdom -- Performer(Wisecrack) as a Boost to defuse
       tension or hold morale together. Situational: only when
       he can actually talk freely.
```

The Hook is the whole character. He's extraordinary in the chair and an ordinary man
everywhere else, and it earns Karma every time that gets him captured, outmatched, or
sidelined. *Spacer's Ear* is trade knowledge from years in the black -- what sounds wrong,
which ports don't ask, who's hiring.

Deliberately no Warrior or Sniper. He doesn't fight. That's the point.

## Kaylee -- Kaywinnet Lee Frye

```
3K   Level 3              3 Luck Tokens
2K   Tinker 2             Ship's Engines 2, Jury-Rig 1
2K   Loremaster 2         Engine Girl 2

Hook: Heart on Her Sleeve 1 (free)

Tricks:
     Talk to Me, Girl -- When diagnosing any ship problem, a
       free-action Tinker(Jury-Rig) Boost to her next repair
       action. She doesn't troubleshoot with manuals; she listens.

     Make It Work -- Tinker(Jury-Rig) Wagered. She commits to a
       fix before knowing whether the improvised part will hold.
```

Only two Roles, both focused. *Jury-Rig* is the non-engine improvisation -- life support
parts, catalyzers, making do. Both diagnostic Tricks key off it rather than Ship's Engines,
because listening-to-the-problem applies to everything, not just engines.

Her social warmth is real but lives at **base Level**, not in a Role. Kaylee doesn't
*technique* people; she's just herself. *Heart on Her Sleeve* earns Karma when that openness
costs her.

## Zoe Washburne

```
3K   Level 3              3 Luck Tokens
2K   Sniper 2             Rifles 2
2K   Warrior 2            Tactician 2
1K   Performer 1          Command Presence 1

Hook: Mal's Right Hand 1 (free)

Tricks:
     Covering Fire -- Sniper(Rifles) Wagered as a Persistent
       Boost to allies' movement or positioning.

     No Hesitation -- Warrior(Tactician) as a free-action Boost
       to her first action when things go sideways. Not reflexes:
       she already assessed and planned.
```

Warrior 2 is real -- Wash calls her *warrior-woman* constantly -- but expressed as **Tactician**
rather than a melee Maneuver. She doesn't brawl; she evaluates fast and clean, then adds the
Boost to whatever she needs to do. Base Sniper rank covers a pistol fine; Rifles 2 is for the
Mare's Leg.

*Command Presence* covers the deadpan that shuts Jayne up, the instant "wife" persona in The
Train Job, and the quiet authority that doesn't need volume.

## Jayne Cobb

```
3K   Level 3              3 Luck Tokens
4K   Sniper 3             Rifles 3
1K   Warrior 1            Brawler 1

Hook: Money Talks 1 (free)

Tricks:
     Vera -- Given time to set up with his named rifle, a Wagered
       Sniper(Rifles) Persistent Boost. The setup is the
       commitment; the payoff is a shot nobody else could make.

     Intimidation by Mass -- Warrior(Brawler) free-action Boost to
       making someone back down through physical menace. Not
       charm. Bulk.
```

**Sniper 3 costs 4K** (1+1+2) on top of Level 3's 5K, leaving exactly 1K. That squeeze is the
lesson: depth in one lane means something else gives. He's the best shot on the crew by a
wide margin and a thug everywhere else.

## The calibration test that sold it

**Jayne vs. Zoe, bare hands.**

- Jayne: Level 3 + Warrior 1 + Brawler 1 = **EL 5**, flat, every round. His only option is to
  hit harder.
- Zoe: Level 3 + Warrior 2 = **EL 5** on the exchange -- *plus* Tactician 2 sitting right
  there. She Boosts off the terrain, his overcommitment, the chair behind him, and rolls her
  next action higher. Or spends it defensively and is never where his fist is.

Same base number. She takes him apart, because breadth plus a smart Maneuver beats raw depth
when the raw numbers are equal.

**At range it inverts.** Zoe EL 7 with a rifle; Jayne EL 9. With Vera and time to set up he
is the most dangerous person on the ship, no contest.

That's the system making the right argument, and it's the same thesis as the deferred
*"It's Not About the Build"* page in `CLAUDE.md`: **EL for depth, breadth for versatility,
Hooks for the story that pays you.**
