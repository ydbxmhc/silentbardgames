## Structural

### Tiering
Entries run at three very different depths - four-line sketches (Pell, the Reincarnate), full essays (Agnes), and stubs that defer elsewhere (Vane, Malisondre). Nothing signals which the reader is about to hit. Either declare the tiers in the preamble or normalize.

### Dangling references
- "Full mechanical entry in the Vane artifact" - **the Vane artifact does not exist.** Cut, or replace with an honest `@@`, or with a line that stands alone: *"His Level One block is complete; the rest is a table's business."*
- "That is a matter for the Accords, and another chapter" - not intended to be in this document. Cut.
- "Full entry in the Malisondre artifact" - this one is real. Wants a filename or link rather than a bare mention.

### The four-tier layout (planned)
1. **Player-facing** - folklore. Hedged, sourced, incomplete, occasionally *wrong*.
2. **Description** - the truth. GM's eyes.
3. **Functional block** - cross-system "how do I run this."
4. **System blocks** - the translations.

`@@` The current descriptions are GM-truth written in an omniscient voice, and they hand over solutions: the Hanged Man's description gives the quest outright, Vane's announces that exactly three endings exist, Agnes's names the Anchor and calls her a coward. None of that survives contact with a player-facing tier unedited.

Folklore that is *wrong* is the prize here. The village is certain Agnes's barrette should be buried at a crossroads. It isn't true. It's a great scene.

### Missing-work markers
Only two `@@` markers exist in the whole file. Boundless is absent from five of seven entries and the preamble carries that admission alone. Flag gaps where they occur.

### Cosmetic
- Pell's `### D&D 5e*` has an orphan asterisk with no footnote.
- The `---` rules between system blocks appear for Pell and the Hanged Man, then vanish for Zombie and Agnes.
- Capitalization of *the dead* wanders - lowercase in Pell's prose, capital in his Boundless block, and the document is titled *The Dead*.
- Notation drifts: Hook ranks appear on Agnes and Malisondre but not Pell or Vane. `Constrained 1d[0-3]` is undefined anywhere in the document.

### Entry order
Pell -> Hanged Man -> Zombie -> Agnes -> Vane -> Malisondre -> Reincarnate. Looks like escalation collapsing at the end; reads better as a bookend - a living man the dead speak *through*, closing on a living child who walked back *from* them. If that was deliberate, keep it. If not, it happened anyway and is worth keeping.

Load-bearing setting terminology: a class of entities fully present in both the physical and spirit realms at all times. **Not hobbits.**

`@@` **This document never says so.** A reader arriving from a link has "Averond zombies are Halflings" and their own assumptions, and those assumptions are wrong in an actively misleading direction. One clause on first use, or a link to the setting glossary.

## The Zombie

### Prose
`@@` "the gap between a literal command and a clever **world**" - probably *word*. Verify at desk.

### The persistence question - **RESOLVED**
Not improvisation. Not the body solving. The body **not stopping**, with a hand coming off as byproduct. It doesn't choose to deliver itself; it just doesn't stop trying to do as commanded, and this is the kind of thing that happens as a result.

`@@` L1 resolves this through the Conceit (all damage received is treated as Unarmed). **The other systems don't inherit it.**
- **5e** - Undead Fortitude covers surviving 0 HP, not ignoring self-inflicted structural loss. A DM asked "can it pull its own hand off to slip the rope?" has nothing to point at.
- **Cortex** - closest to working already; mob-as-one-zombie reads lost members as lost bulk, so the hand is a d6 leaving the pool.
- **Fate** - see below.

### 5e
Fold blindsight and Spirit-Touched under one named Halfling trait, so the mechanic and the setting term point at each other.

### Fate - the design - **RESOLVED, needs text**
The ten Condensed stress boxes are a deliberate red herring. Knocking them off feels like progress and accomplishes nothing; the zombie ends up a pulpy mess and no less effective. Using Condensed rather than Core is itself a tell for readers who know the difference. No Mental track because there is no mind that matters.

The Aspect **Immune to Mental and Physical Consequences** is worded correctly and doing its job. It is not a description - it is a **sign reading "Attack is the wrong verb."** Consequence slots are normal and always were.

At least four ways through, none of them a sword:
1. **Overcome the Aspect** - break the animating enchantment.
2. **Magical Consequences** - the immunity names physical and mental; spells likely land. GM call.
3. **Create an Advantage** - he isn't immune to those, or to physical attrition. *Disarmed*, in the literal sense. *Hobbled*. Chop him into pieces and he'll have a hard time stopping you doing whatever you came to do.
4. **Go around** - a torso with opinions and no way to act on them is not an obstacle.

`@@` **None of this is on the page.** The entry offers the Aspect, ten boxes, and *"Call your shots carefully"* - which points at Attack, the one verb that doesn't work. Needs an In Play note that *promises* a solution without naming one. Something like: there are at least four ways to end this thing and none of them are a sword.

`@@` Reconcile the two true readings of the stress track: **cosmetic** (real damage that changes nothing about his willingness) and **attrition** (what makes dismemberment plausible). Both are correct from different chairs. The text has to land it deliberately or a GM picks one and loses the other.

`@@` Consider whether a full stress track needs an explicit non-Taken-Out note, since RAW reads no stress plus no available Consequences as Taken Out.
