### CROSS-SYSTEM PRINCIPLE - "off-turn effect" translation (2026-07-22)
**L1 gives reactive effect for free:** defense/resistance is a *free action* and can *carry effect* (impose a Hook/Hindrance on a winning defend), so an L1 creature works *on the player's turn* without spending its own. **No initiative system hands you that for nothing.** To translate any L1 creature that acts off-turn, bolt the effect onto that system's designated off-turn hook:
- **Fate:** Defend-with-**style** → a *boost* (RAW, but temporary + 3+ shifts only). For a *lasting* aspect on any win → a **Stunt** (e.g. *"on a successful defend vs. revealing/discarding the barrette, place a situation aspect with a free invoke, no style required"*). The stunt IS the license.
- **5e:** a **Reaction**, or **lair/legendary actions**.
- **Cortex:** a reaction roll, or a triggered **SFX**.
Do **not** fake L1's frictionlessness in the initiative systems - the creature *should* feel a hair less effortless there; that's their grain.
**Recurs immediately:** Agnes's reactive imposition, Vane's *Doomglare* on eye contact, the Hanged Man's every-turn wail, Malisondre interrupting a cast. All want the right off-turn hook per system.

### Concession-as-sacrifice / victim-as-inside-man (2026-07-23, Agnes but general)
The victim's yield **inverts meaning depending on whether an ally can reach her**: alone it's a tragic retreat; with a rescuer who can cross the Veil it's a **bait play** - the victim throws the fight, narrates his exit so Agnes is fully committed/exposed taking him, and the ally strikes the **Anchor** in that window. **The Barrette of Agnes - a tiered "ongoing attunement" curse.** Pickup = Charmed (barrette as the
object of the charm; blocks *destroying* it, not *abandoning* it, so early self-rescue is real; -2

back to rank 2; charm & exhaustion remain), sleepwalk (modest DC, exhaustion re-inflates), 1d6
psychic pangs ("her pining, not an attack"; no charm-break).

**Signature mechanics established (reusable across the other six):**
- **Modest base DC + let exhaustion escalate** - never stack a steep base on a mechanic exhaustion
  is already climbing (the trapdoor-avoidance rule).
- **The who-narrates telegraph** - DC5 save each push: success = *player* narrates their restraint,
  fail = *GM* narrates the tell + 1 exhaustion (fail-only, clears on a long rest pre-tier-3,
  foreshadowing the lethal tier-3 version). The roll *telegraphs and prices*, never *determines* -
  the choice stays the player's. It is the "GM must leave clues" mandate rendered as dice, and the
  middle rung of the Hooks escalation ladder.
- **Possession-as-cursed-item climax** - seize at near-death (0 HP or about-to-die-of-exhaustion,
  *attuned only*); wipe exhaustion; animate with her own aura (host HP = min(16, full)); beat the 16
  to 0 = disrupted (reforms tomorrow); overage -> host; nonlethal (melee) finish = host stable at 0,
  safe to revive. Player runs the possessed PC, double XP for evading the party. **This template
  lifts straight onto Malisondre / any Anchored spirit.**
- **"The odds always lie; easy is not sure"** - per-roll odds vs the cumulative clock.
  Lessons-Learned-worthy.

**Creature block = a footnote** (AC 12, HP 16, Ethereal, Anchored). "The item isn't enchanted - she
is." Inert without her; disrupting her form only disperses (reforms 24h); true end = destroy the
anchor (scatter across running water) or bind/bargain (Accords).

### `@@ MUNDIS` - it isn't improper, it's plural

*mundus* is 2nd-declension masculine. The forms:

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| mundus | the world *(nom. sg.)* |
| **mundi** | **of the world** *(gen. sg.)*, or *worlds* (nom. pl.) |
| mundo | to/by the world *(dat./abl. sg.)* |
| mundum | the world *(acc. sg.)* |
| **mundis** | **to / for / by / in the WORLDS** *(dat./abl. pl.)* |

So "of the world" would be **mundi**, not mundis. **Mundis is a perfectly real Latin form - it's just plural.**

> Which is *better* than off-ness. Averond has a material world and a spirit realm, and "nailed to **Mundis**" parses as *nailed to the worlds*, both of them. That is exactly Malisóndrë's condition, and exactly what Halflings are. The word has been quietly correct this whole time.

- [ ] Keep **Mundis**. Consider whether the plural reading is worth making explicit somewhere in-world - a Loremaster's aside would carry it nicely.

### HOOKS CUT BOTH WAYS - rules clarification to draft (2026-07-21)

*Closes an open item already logged in `SilentBardGames/CLAUDE.md` (surfaced 2026-07-14) - but that note only captured the **GM-against-PC** direction. The **PC-against-NPC** half is the more valuable one and was never written down.*

**Author's clarifications:**
- Splitting Level across Hooks was a quick way to get numbers, **not a rule.** Each Hook may be up to Level.
- **No cap on how many Hooks a character has** - but no advantage either: **the first Hook triggered in a session earns Karma; every trigger after that earns a Token.** Wants strong guidance against too many, since they dilute rather than reinforce.
- **Anyone who knows a character's Hook may add its rank to their own roll against them.** Fully symmetric - PCs do it to NPCs exactly as the GM does it to PCs.
- Both sides may tap in the same contest; the party can **balance out or cancel** an NPC's own Hook use.

**The Malisondre worked example (author's):** the party is brought before her; she is enjoying the speech. A Bard or Conjuror, having *learned or surmised* that she likes cat-and-mouse, taps **her** Hook and adds its dice against her to force a parley. It becomes social combat; she may tap her own Hooks to resist. **The moment she Agrees, the Accords bind her for the duration of the Negotiation** - *"an established Agreement precludes the Participants directly harming each other."* The win isn't beating her. It's getting her to say yes.

**RESOLVED 2026-07-21. Approved draft for `hooks.md`:**

> **They Cut Both Ways.** Anyone who knows what drives you can play to it. Add your Hook's rank to *their* roll when they're working that angle deliberately - the same number, pointed the other direction. A drive that could only ever help you wouldn't be a Hook; it'd be a bonus.
>
> It runs the other way just as well. Learn what a foe can't resist and you can use it on them exactly as they'd use it on you. Finding that out is what an evening in the archives is *for*.
>
> Being played this way is a Hook event like any other. Let it land and you're paid as usual. Refuse it and you pay to refuse, as usual. Sometimes the GM will let you *fight* it instead of buying your way out - and if he does, you're on your own dice, with no payment either way.

**The GM's escalation ladder** - this is the framing for the GM-facing text. The offer-a-roll option is a *middle* setting on a dial that already exists, not a new mechanic:

| Pressure | The offer | Cost to refuse |
|---|---|---|
| **Soft** | Suggest a self-Hook | Nothing. Decline freely. |
| **Middle** | Offer the roll - *"take this, or try to resist it"* | Your own dice, no payment either way. You may fail. |
| **Hard** | Hard invocation - *"take this, or pay me one"* | A Luck Token, or a rank off the Hook. |

**Why the free resist doesn't break the Token economy** (author's point, and it's better than the fix I proposed): the Hook is adding its rank to the *opposing* roll, so resisting is uphill by construction. Against stacked odds you'd practically have to spend a Token on the roll anyway - so the Token gets spent either way. The difference is whether you're buying a **certainty** (decline) or a **gamble** (resist). And sometimes even the Token won't be enough. The GM controls the odds, and may set them as pure colour or stack them deliberately.

*Two independent safeguards, then: the GM grants the roll, and bad odds tax it.*

### `@@ NEW PAGE: "Choosing the Right Tool"` - Hook vs Hindrance vs Complication vs Injury
When does it make more sense to impose which? "The attacker chooses the Harm" is the engine - this page teaches the *choice*. Seed of the decision framework (from what we've built this week):
- **Complication** - transient setback that should clear with a breather; the *default* for generic damage. Wiped by any recovery roll. (fatigue, lost nerve, a rattling blow) - the Zombie forces *everything* here.
- **Injury** - a real, lasting wound that should persist and broadly diminish; a general EL drag that softens the target against *every* threat. Deliberate (usually Wagered).
- **Hindrance** - a specific, situational impediment that bites only when relevant and can't be healed away, only resolved in fiction; self-accelerates. Use when you want to erode a *particular* capability without broadly crippling - **Agnes's whole method** (drag resistance-to-her, invisibly).
- **Imposed Hook** - change what the target *wants or does*: an ongoing behavioral condition (fear, charm, compulsion, addiction), adoptable per the new page.
- **Down / Possession** - end the contest / seize control.
- Natural home: near `types-of-harm.md`, or a GM-craft page. Every bestiary creature's "method" is really a Harm-type choice - this page is the through-line for that.

### Court Booklets
Separate add-on booklets: The Dead, The Demon Court, The Fae Courts, etc.
Each standalone; each with seven systems; each with its own arc.

### Gear/Weapons Booklet (Proposed)
Averond has near-zero steel (sacred, Carterion-controlled). Bronze is primary. This significantly changes:
- Weapon types and fighting styles (spears favored; bronze dulls/doesn't hold edge; maces better vs. bronze armor)
- Armor types (leather, bronze scale/plate)
- Shield importance (greater than in steel-age settings)
- Economic power structures (who has steel = who has power)
- In-system expression: L1 Traits/Conceits; 5e material property tags; Boundless Position modifiers
