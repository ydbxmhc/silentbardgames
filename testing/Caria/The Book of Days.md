In the Beginning, the Abode of the gods bore all that they needed, and they strode among the heavens for days untold \- but they were not content.

Subtle Susheil shared her waters with them, and brought refreshing wind, but she yearned for the waves of the darkening deeps.

Beautiful Rana guided their home amid the stars, showing them the glory of her skyfire gems, but she yearned for the rains of a thundering sky.

Patient Grolikarnigan brought them grains and meat, but he yearned for the silence in the deep places of stone and wood.

Clever Carterion bade them wait for the fullness of time, and he clothed them with armor and weapons worthy of their glory.

But Rikitak came and asked, why need we weapons if we make no war? Why armor if we have no fear of each other?   
Why does Rana who guides our way yet yearn to fly?  
Why does Susheil who makes sweet our air and water seek darkness in it?  
Why does Grolikarnigan who spawns the leaf and lamb crave distance and quiet?

The arts of each of us we bring to perfection, but there is none to honor us in our gifts.

Grolikarnigan stood and said, There is one, but the Abode of the gods is not the place for Man.

Each agreed, and Rana guided the Abode through the stars, causing them to light her way to the World.

Here, she said, shall be the place of Man.  
And the gods looked upon the world and saw that it was good.  
The gods set the Arch of Heaven above the world, and established the Abode in it.  
They built the Pillars to hold it up.

And Susheil breathed freshness into the airs and the waters of the World.  
Grolikarnigan loosed upon the world beast, fish and fowl, and set trees and grasses and every flower and moss.

Susheil moved upon the waters, and the deeps became her own.

Rana lifted her face and arms into the skies, and the airs became her own.

Grolikarnigan wandered in the wild places, and delved into the stone and darkness, and the land became his own.

And Carterion gathered the bounties of Rana's skies and Susheil's waters and the deep stone of Grolikarnigan, and made for the gods new wonders such as the Abode had never seen, and they rejoiced.

And they brought forth Man and placed him in the World and said to him, This is the world we have made for you. Go forth and multiply in the laws and sight of the gods, that you may honor us in our gifts.

But Man said in himself, Why have the gods the Arch of Heaven and all the glories and comforts therein, while I must toil for shelter and bread? And he desired to be one with the gods, and railed against them that they should withhold any thing from his desire.

Then came again Rikitak among the gods and said, here is the war.   
Man will not rest while Rana has free, clear sky in which to fly.  
Man will not rest while Susheil has cold, clean deeps in which to swim.  
Man will not rest while Grolikarnigan has dark, quiet glades in which to sit.  
Man will not rest until every wonder of Carterion is laid at his feet, and yet will he clamor for more. Give us more\!

And Grolikarnigan said, Man is a predator. He must claim his place among the beasts, or be consumed.

And Rana said, Man is a beast of burden. He must be reined and stabled, and if he will not bear it, whipped and chained, or swept away in the storm.

And Susheil said, Man is a sickness. If he cannot be borne, he must be washed away.

But Carterion said, Man is a child, willful but ignorant. He must be taught, with gentle words when possible.

And Rikitak said, Carterion speaks half the truth. If gentle words do not avail, a child requires a rod until he can understand better the reasons for the gentle words. The campfire does not seek out the hand of the child, but teaches swiftly when respect is lacking. Yet why should Man respect the gods? We have given for no reason but generosity. What more should he expect?

The gods thus took the counsel each of the others, and sent their messengers to Man that he should no longer expect generosity unearned.

And Man said, the gods have abandoned me. I shall do as I please.

In his pride, the strong took the wife of the weak, and the rich the child of the poor, and Man lived in bitterness and strife, and the gods were angered.

Susheil said, I will raise a flood.  
Carterion said, No, spare him.  
Rikitak said, Leave him to learn from his pain.  
Grolikarnigan said, My beasts do not deserve to suffer a flood because of Man.

Rana said nothing.

Grolikarnigan said, I will send the beasts of the wild to ravage his villages and cast the bodies into the sea.  
Carterion said, No, spare him.  
Rikitak said, Leave him to learn from his pain.  
Susheil said, Do not poison my shores with his rotting blood and bones.

Rana said nothing, but sent spirits of air and breath to gather the wails of the downtrodden.

Carterion said, let us go down and build temples in which to teach.  
Susheil said, If he on river and ocean honors me, I will hear his prayers, but I will not lightly go and be seen. Let him fear the storm and the flood.  
Grolikarnigan said, If he will honor bird and beast, flower and tree, and keep his place in the order of all things, then I will bless the work of his hands, but I will not lightly go and be seen. Let him fear blight and famine.  
Rikitak asked of none but himself, Go among Man?  
And he said, Yes, I will go, and hear his prayers, and teach him Wisdom and Folly, that he may choose and reap; but I will not always make my face known, for Man must know that it is he that chooses. Let him fear the power and the consequence.

But Rana, having taken the names of the wicked among the strong and the rich, sent her anger in chariots of wind, and smote the wicked whose names she had learned with lighting and searing ice. Then she returned to her skies to be troubled no more.
