Session 5 - Tears Are Water

Level One solo playtest, Averond - Caria Greenhaven, Initiate of the Monastery of Strife. GM: Lilita. Played 2026-09-30/10-01. Counted as two sessions for advancement (split at sleep, Mira's hearth).

At a glance

A day and a night on the coast. Caria meets Mira Thousand-Voices with open hands and learns that the thing Mira burned on the beach had been a girl - a small one - which puts a name from the Monastery kitchens in her mouth and fear in her heart. She swears an oath to a starveling goblin, sits up all night with a fisherwife's croupy son, pays an undine with the one secret she is most ashamed of, and helps an old forge-fire touch the sea.

By morning, Saltwind is starting to talk about the Monastery girl who wouldn't take a copper.

Ends at Mira's hearth as the noon bell begins to toll for Brother Aubric.

Scene 1 - The Basin and the Wall

At Susheil's basin Caria finds herself crying again, and laughing, hands raised to the sea: tears are water too. Thousand-Voices arrives with Nell, an undine riding in her waterskin. Caria shows Shimmer open, empty hands - the message from the first night, when Shimmer expected oil and a torch - and the water grudges her a truce.

She prostrates herself to Mira as she did to Karinna and offers restitution for her Order's wayward hands. She can't say Aubric's name. Mira sits down on the cobbles to be level with her and doesn't let go of her hand.

"Aubric was my friend. How can I help?"

Mira offers her hearth. Caria accepts guest-right, and her friends in the fire-pot are made welcome: "a hearth is for fires."

Scene 2 - Mira's Hearth

A turf-roofed cottage tucked into the cliff west of the village. Caria falls asleep on the hearthstone mid-sentence. Asher goes down the anchor line to give the Weeping Court her love, and the House sends back a warm leaf.

At dusk, Ivro's letter is delivered into the right hand; its watching spark winks out, content. Over fish-and-barley stew Mira tells what Calder said he was hunting - the Almoner's "property," a sick servant, "it" one breath and "him" the next - and Caria tells her what walked: Hospitaller Aubric, on a leash, toward Mira's door.

Then Mira says it was two. The one they burned on the beach, with e'Di and Shimmer and every drop of lamp oil in Saltwind, had been small. A girl, once. Caria hears the kitchen maids whispering that no one has seen the girl in weeks, and sorts what she knows from what she has heard and what she fears: Wenna Tarro, twelve, surety for her father's grain, in the west wing.

"I trust Kade with my life and my loved ones - if I have any left."
Scene 3 - Min

Mira's household has another member: Min, a starveling juvenile goblin, one-eared, cataract-eyed and club-footed, who keeps her fire under a formal Accord. He smells Hobbe-fire on the guest. Caria kneels to his height and asks what she owes him. His terms: never tell any Hobbe where he is, and the sneaky-spark keeps his fire by day. Asher is thrilled.

Caria makes it larger: Min is her sacred trust, and she will defend the household that named her guest even if he no longer lives there. Min is horrified to be over-paid and offers his whole catch. She accepts one mussel, shared - steamed open by Asher on his new fire - and thanks him in the sky-tongue. He answers, flawlessly, before he can stop himself: Received and witnessed. So it is bound.

Scene 4 - Holes Tied Together With String

Mira teaches her to mend nets. Caria gives Mira herbs, and Mags's honey, carried from the Monastery kitchens "for the shrine-keeper." Old Bellows, through Min, admits peat is cow-fire and asks for applewood - and when is the sea? Mira knows: a smith's fire knows the quench. Dawn, at the cove, when the sea breathes out.

Scene 5 - The Croup

Past midnight, Bett Pellow comes up the cliff path with her youngest, Daw, whooping and dusky-lipped. She came for Thousand-Voices and finds the Monastery woman instead. Caria's Sight finds the swelling and the fright feeding it; she treats both, counting breaths in a cheerful voice and talking to the mother strictly in the relieved past tense. It works.

Then the lesson: never ask a priest of Strife for help - bring the problem, as Bett did. Fire is a teacher. And: if you want to save your boy, you keep calm. In the black hour the croup comes back, and Bett is already counting before Caria is awake.

"You're not that." - Bett, of the man whose cudgel broke her Jory's collarbone.
Scene 6 - The Quench

At the cove at dawn Shimmer names her price for letting a fire into her water: a secret Caria has told no one. Caria gives her the true one, and weeps. The undine takes her tears too, and keeps them. "Water is the one thing it cannot burn."

Old Bellows meets the sea through Mira's borrowed voice - "Six hundred years I've been quenching nails in a bucket!" - and comes up tempered: black as jet, his heart a live ember sealed inside. He asks to be carried home to his forge someday, with a story no fire in Averond can match.

Climbing back, Caria tells Mira about Jem and the lonely House that wants a tenant who won't fade. Mira sees the shape of it, and every reason for caution. "But..."

Scene 7 - Morning in Saltwind

Bett's back door is open to her, "and that's not a payment." The net-woman tests her knots: "They'll hold." At the well-house Caria borrows Karinna's grandmother's brass mortar, reads the pain in the old keeper's hands with the Sight, and teaches Nell the brine, the willow, the oil - and a different story every night. Karinna closes her fist, all the way, three times.

At the churchyard gate Brother Kade has kept vigil all night and washed the Hospitaller himself. She weeps into his coat; he ties her a proper black band over the charcoal one. "Keep that one under it. You made it with the boy." Soup, sleep, and the bell.

Rulings and canon from this session

  • Hook alterations are always free - a player-driven channel apart from improvements.
  • Spell endings: the world tends to put things back. Shaped stone resumes its shape where it sits; what can't, crumbles.
