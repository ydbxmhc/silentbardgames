# CHARACTER - Caria Greenhaven, Initiate of the Monastery of Strife (after S01 advancement, 2026-09-27)

@@* = not yet confirmed by Lee

- Name: Caria Greenhaven. Peasant stock, from Green Haven (~120 souls; family holdings, shrine duty rotated among families).
- Grandmother said to have been a "hedge witch"; nothing more known.
- Showed talent young; scolded and told to hide it.
- Taken at 9 by an old healer-priest of the monastery as the price of his service; no one argued.
- Raised in the Monastery of Strife (Rikitak's house): floors, vegetables, chamberpots, messages - and the run of the place under the old priest's watchful eye. No one dared abuse her.
- Does not know what marked her bloodline. Priests have debated it; she has heard some names.
- Age 19; ten years at the monastery.
- Temperament: serious-minded; retreated into duty and diligence as her defense against unscrupulous eyes and hands. Keeps her "demons" in check. A tendency to hyperbole when she talks.

- Name: **A Hint of Brimstone** (1). Visible tell: a single white lock of hair.

## Tricks (Lee kept all three, 2026-09-27)
- **Salt in the Hem** - a ring of salt, cold-iron shavings, silver and herbs sewn into the hem of a loose skirt (no break at the open front). Inert until she invokes it. When she does - tucking into it for a moment, or holding it actively as a Ward while moving - it grants a free-action Conjuror Ward Boost, because the circle is already on her and she focuses into it. It is the Lore of salt that protects, not the salt. (So no, it does not keep Asher out unless she means it to.)
- **Nothing to See, Sister** - when her Hook draws eyes at the worst moment, Release the bound Phantasmal Veil as a free action: guaranteed 1, aimed as she likes (attention Hindrance, a phantom arrow, a moment's glamour as a Boost to anything a perception could help). Costs the binding.
- **Query the Candle** - before a ritual or a question about fire, spirits, or the "manascape," she asks Asher; his answer is a Boost. The favor goes on the debt ledger.
