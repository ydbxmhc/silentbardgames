@@ UNCOMMITTED [Setup] Repertoire answers (Lee, 2026-09-26): (a) A bound Role rank is unavailable for casting or countering anything but its own spell, but still counts as skill for knowledge rolls and anything that isn't manipulating forces - so a full ritual of a known-but-unbound spell at Spellweaver 1 (rank bound elsewhere) rolls at bare Level. (b) A known spell adds NO EL - "a sword, not the skill to use it." It is known intimately; no book is needed to bind it. (c) A spell learned as a Loremaster Maneuver: if bound, its Maneuver rank counts toward casting EL. If unbound, the Maneuver rank (not the Loremaster Role rank) counts for countering that spell or full-ritual casting it, via synergy only. A non-Spellweaver might use it to counter, never to cast. (d) Swapping always means releasing the bound rank first (fast-cast it, or let it dissipate), then a fresh full-ritual binding - not possible mid-fight. -> spellcraft.md needs the known/bound distinction and these four points.

@@ UNCOMMITTED [Setup] Token-pinning trick (Lee): cast a spell and pin its effect with a Luck Token; the Token holds the duration without concentration and stays tied up (not recovered at the next scene) until released. @@ UNCOMMITTED [Setup] Token-pin answers (Lee): a pinned Token is *allocated* - still in your pocket, so it counts against the Level cap and blocks the per-scene refill, but can't be used for anything else until released. A pinned Ward resists at its rolled value. A caster who is present may retroactively roll a Boost on the standing spell, adding presence and aura - but that exposes them: whatever tears at the Ward is now opposition that can affect the caster, not just the spell. Left alone, the spell stands or falls on its own and is worn down like a door under a barbarian's boot. Default is resistance-only; with the existing **Reactive** Trait (traits.md: "Only acts when triggered" - wards named explicitly) it opposes anything trying to break it, at its rating. (Lilita wrongly flagged a name collision here; Lee corrected - same Trait.) Small wording note: Reactive is an *enhancement* on a Ward (passive thing gains the ability to act) but a *limitation* on an attack or a creature (active thing restricted to triggers), so under spells.md it adds 1 Difficulty in one case and pays 1 back in the other. The "useful vs limiting" rule already handles it by context; one example line would make that obvious. -> Parallels the Conjuror's Soulburn exposure in shaping-effects.md; worth writing the two side by side.

@@ UNCOMMITTED [Design] Improvising a Release (fast-cast) - RULED by Lee 2026-09-27: adopt "fast or shaped, not both." An Improvised Release costs the action (no longer free), keeps its guaranteed value, needs the hold roll vs. added Difficulty, and a failed hold wastes it (effect 0). Plain aiming (target, Manifestation) stays free. Original discussion follows: Canon (spellcraft.md): Release is a free action, guaranteed value = bound ranks, no new roll, so no Improvisation. Lee asks whether allowing a normal Improvise roll on a Release unbalances it. Lilita's analysis: (1) Aiming is not Improvising - target and Manifestation (Veil as a distraction vs. a phantom arrow; Light as a flash vs. a lamp) are description and should always be free on a Release. (2) The two things that normally pay for Traits both vanish on a Release: the spell is consumed anyway, so "lose the spell" costs nothing; and it's a free action, so Traits would ride on zero action cost. That combination (free-action, guaranteed, Area/Inexorable) scales badly at high rank. (3) Suggested balance: an Improvised Release costs the action (no longer free), keeps its guaranteed value, requires the hold roll against the added Difficulty, and a failed hold means the stored power bleeds away (effect 0). "Fast or shaped - not both." Variant: allow Traits on a free-action Release up to the caster's Improvisation Maneuver rank. -> Lee to decide.
