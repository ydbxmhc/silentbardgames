Session 6 (Part 1) - Every Prayer Has Weight

Level One solo playtest, Averond - Caria Greenhaven, Initiate of the Monastery of Strife. GM: Lilita. Played 2026-10-01, in progress. Saved mid-scene for a fresh chat.

At a glance

Saltwind buries Brother Aubric. Caria walks to the grave barefoot and ashed, says the words, sings him down in the sky-tongue, and then her body quits on her past the churchyard wall. On the shingle she gives Jem his mother's last message, nearly lets her own fury out through Jory Pellow's eyes, and begs him to help her not become it. He counts her breaths.

At the burial meal a frightened clerk does his sums out loud. The sums give a name to the girl Mira burned on the beach, and say that Wenna Tarro was still alive a month ago. In Mirna's back kitchen, a scorched brass button gives up a mother's voice, a father's voice and a child's name. Under all of that is something that should have stayed asleep.

Paused in the kitchen, the sea air blowing through the open yard door, with four people around a lamp and a folded cloth on the table.

Scene 1 - The Burial (Saltwind churchyard, noon)

Caria comes up the lane in the Healer's orange with no sash, hearth-ash on her face and hair, barefoot, unarmed. The village falls in behind her in silence. Some at the gate look away, ashamed of what they said about the Monastery girl.

The eulogy comes in three parts. First she owns the name they give her, "Monastery Girl," and names Olwin's cudgel and Jory's broken collarbone; her mark smokes in the cold air while she says it. Then she tells how Aubric carried a too-heavy basket for a nine-year-old bought from a sick mother. Then she lets go of the whys: we are here to honor the man.

"He will answer."

Others speak. Karinna, of thirty-one springs of jars for her hands. Hal Pellow, of three nights and two fingers and no copper asked. Mira, of a broken arm at nine and "You'll mend." Then, in a voice not quite hers: "Go on, then. Eat something." Last comes Kade, soldier-plain, before forty witnesses: murdered, and used. He says he will write it.

Caria sings the Celestial dirge Aubric used to sing on his circuit. The old voices of the village hum under it, Hollis keens on the long notes, and to the Sight the churchyard glitters with rising motes edged in ember-red. She crumbles the earth into the grave instead of throwing it, wipes it down her cheeks, and walks toward the sea. She doesn't reach it. Kade carries her.

Scene 2 - The Shingle (early afternoon)

She wakes with her head in Mira's lap and her bare feet in the surf, wrapped in Korrin's oilskin for the third time.

She calls Jem over, holds him, and delivers what his mother asked: not cross about the barley; keep your feet dry; sing your little ones all the verses. Jem laughs, breaks, and sings the middle verses of "Little Brown Wren." The debt to Tessa is paid.

Jory Pellow has followed her all the way down from the grave. "You meant it. About Olwin." Her answer comes out with fire behind her eyes: Olwin isn't the problem, but he has earned a share of the cure. He leans in, hungry for it. That frightens her back to herself. She seizes his hand and begs him to help her not become what he just saw.

"I serve the god of Strife, because I strive."

The demon in her whispers about Olwin's guts. Jory feels her shudder and starts to count her breaths, the way she taught his mother the night before. She asked for help and got it, so she owes him. His price is plain: fix his arm before the herring's gone.

Scene 3 - Mira's Cottage (mid-afternoon)

On the cliff path Kade tells her the wagons turn north in three days. He'll report the truth to the Almoner, and her name will sit beside his. Calder will read it, and so will Brask.

Shimmer pulls the sea out of her orange robe in one shining sheet. Caria thanks her and declines anything more today. She changes by the fire, past caring (Kade suddenly finds the door-latch fascinating), and sighs for the last of the brandy. Kade passes a tin flask back over his shoulder without turning round: in my trade you always carry black, and this. She kisses his temple the way she kisses his dog. He grins for the first time.

Scene 4 - The Burial Meal (Saltwind lodge)

Jory has saved her Mirna's good bowl and warmed the beer, exactly as promised. Then Caria sits down across from Clerk Fennick, who hasn't touched his soup.

She tells him he is a better man than the small, fussy clerk she knew on the mountain, and makes him eat. He's a layman, paid at quarter-day, with no vows to protect him. He works out the cost of each choice aloud, like a ledger, and decides the coin is his own hand: he'll go back and write it true, and make copies, because a record in one place can be burned. Caria names the people who could hold a copy, ending with Olef Tarro.

At that name the clerk goes still. The Almoner's books carry two surety lines in the same hand. TARRO (grain) opened four weeks ago and is still open. CORVANE (salt) opened in late summer and was "closed" seven weeks ago, with no release. Fennick initialed that audit himself.

"Provisions drawn aren't provisions eaten."

The girl Mira burned was the Corvane child. Wenna Tarro was alive four weeks ago. Caria sends Fennick to hear it from Mira himself.

Dasha Korrin offers the wagon-one seat north on the same terms, and wants an answer by the second evening. Caria tells Kade everything. He goes cold: they sent me to fetch it, and never said it was a child. Then the finder wakes in him: there's still something to find.

She tells him what Aubric taught her: when you know better, you do better, and she won't have to do it alone. She shows him the alembic scar. Kade pays his debt from the road, the barracks verse of "Little Brown Wren," under his breath and perfectly straight-faced. Caria laughs so hard she pours soup into her own boot, and Hollis cleans it.

Scene 5 - The Lamps

Caria offers to help old Mirna, who is fighting the lamp wicks, with obvious mischief. Asher would love to show off, but Shimmer's terms said "keeps to the hearth." So Caria asks for the terms to be renegotiated. Asher, taking "it doesn't hurt to ask" literally, puts the question to the rain-barrel himself and isn't put out.

"For that house's fires. When the widow asks. Not the girl. Not the spark."

Mirna turns to the sea, not to Caria, and asks: "You've had enough of mine. You can give me this." A spark comes down off the bluff and every lamp in the hall lights. Mirna pats Caria's cheek hard enough to count as a slap, and sits down.

Scene 6 - The Back Kitchen (dusk, in progress)

Caria, Mira, Kade and Fennick sit round a lamp in Mirna's back kitchen, with Hollis under the table. Caria asks what was left after the burning. Mira answers. There were small, black bones, which she buried under a cairn above the tideline. And there was a brass button, which she has kept in her pocket to pray on, and shown no one.

Caria opens the Sight on the button. Under weeks of Mira's prayers she finds a bright room that smells of brine, salt heaped in pans, a mother sewing the button onto blue wool, and two voices calling across the yard: "Bee!" and "Bryony! Come and see the new pans!" The mark on the button is three crystals over a pan. Something spoiled in the brass knows her mark, and clings.

She won't calm down. She makes the thing show her, in the sky-tongue and on a breath of sulphur. It is a splinter of something vast, chained and asleep under the Mountain, and Calder's hand is on the chain. The splinter is awake now. The vast thing turned over once in its sleep. Her own shaking was appetite: her brimstone wants to eat it.

Mira opens the yard door and the sea takes the sulphur away. Kade says that binding a thing that size takes its True Name, so either Calder has it or someone gave it to him. Fennick says that "somehow" is usually paid for, and that he enters those lines in a fair hand.

Rulings and table notes (S6 so far)
  • Hook budget spent on presence: Lee spent Hook dice to let the brimstone show, without taking a trigger, and to add flat successes. This happened twice, then a third time to make the splinter talk.
  • Hook trigger taken on the button reading: the taint clings, bad dreams tonight, +1 Karma.
  • Accords: renegotiating a spirit's terms by restating the spirit's own intent is a clarification, not a new favor, and costs nothing. An oracle "sign" result gave the condition.
  • Accords: asking for help ("help me") creates a debt, Caria's own Order's lesson turned on her.
  • Rules check: a True Name gets a spirit's attention almost always. Calling an uncontracted spirit to you is still its own choice.
  • Fennick is a layman, paid at quarter-day, with no vows.
  • Retcon: Mirna is twice widowed (Lee canon). Her line becomes "two husbands and two sons."

Resume at

Mirna's back kitchen, at dusk, with the yard door open to the sea. Caria has just laid it out for them: the sliver that festers into gangrene, the great dark lode under the Mountain, Calder's leash. Kade and Fennick have answered. Mira stands in the doorway looking toward the far end of the beach, where the cairn is, and hasn't spoken. Caria's thumb is circling the rim of her cup.
