Session 3 - Fly Home, Little Wren

Level One solo playtest, Averond - Caria Greenhaven, Initiate of the Monastery of Strife. GM: Lilita. Played 2026-09-28/29.

At a glance

One day on the south road. Caria finds the thing with borrowed voices hooked into Clerk Fennick's dead sister's tin whistle, and burns it to death down its own line with iron, silver and a curse the whole caravan hears. Then she and Brother Kade walk into the Weeping Court after the only thing it left alive - Korrin's lost boy, Jem, asleep for half a year in a house that loved him too much to let him wake.

She doesn't fight the house. She sings to it, gives it a hearth with a piece of herself in it, and gets the boy out by his own free choice.

Ends two hours after dark on the road, as Dasha Korrin's wagon stops very suddenly.

Scene 1 - Wagon 1, Rolling South

Asher's look at Fennick turns up nothing but a tired clerk humming a tune he can't place. Kade, hearing Caria's dream in full, names the place - the Weeping Court, an old fey house past the fork - and asks the question that matters: who else from this caravan slept at the inn? Only Korrin and "your clerk," in the room under the loft.

From the wagon bench her Sight finds the thread: fine, cold, grey-silver, set not in Fennick but in something in his breast pocket. Korrin's most valuable cargo turns out to be six kegs of cold-iron boat-nails, and she gets Fennick sitting on them on a pretext. That night every soul in the caravan will sleep with a silver coin in the teeth - Caria's four, Korrin's nine, and seven more from the Almoner's purse, entered in the books as an "apotropaic disbursement."

"You have my heart, dear Asher." - a debt freely declared, and he knows exactly what she's handed him.
Scene 2 - Wagon 2: the Whistle

Against Kade's advice she tells Fennick the truth, the whole of it. Her demon-mark leaks sulphur into the conversation and he smells it; a Token and plain honesty carry it anyway. The thing in his pocket is a dented tin whistle, blue paint mostly worn away - Ada's, dead of fever at nine.

"Grief is just love with nowhere left to go."

He lends her the whistle until dawn, with a written receipt. Holding it, she reads the hook to its source and learns that iron starves it and silver burns it. Then she drives a Rebuke down the line with kegs and hairpin behind it - "Gods damn thee, in Heaven's name!" The thread burns out end to end. The whistle is clean. Oxen shy; half the caravan heard the Sister curse the sky. The Caller, broken and banished, does not recover. It is dead.

Scene 3 - The Road to the Woods

Before it burned, the thread showed her one other line running from the thing - thin and still warm. Caria reckons it had a victim stashed; Kade puts it together: fey keep sleepers, fey-time runs slow, and Korrin's boy went in last spring. "We're not only hunting now. We're fetching."

Asher is left to keep the caravan's bonfire at Tanner's Ford. Korrin breaks a keg seal herself and fills Caria's hands with nails. "Coins and nails, and earnest prayers!"

Scene 4 - The Weeping Court

A mile in along a deer-track: a troll-built wall too tall for anything, a gate-arch with no gate, and a little door painted into the stone three feet up. Caria weaves a hunter's blind against fey notice - the first try curdles, the second holds with a thread of brimstone in it. Then the house lies to her. Her Sight shows a sunlit garden through the arch and a boy waving from a fountain, and the painted door as only paint.

Hollis won't cross the arch. Kade sees only brambles. A silver hairpin in her teeth and Berra's salt rubbed into her eyes clear the glamour, and with clear eyes she sees the painted birds on the little door: wrens, warm as a hearthstone. From behind the stone, someone hums "Little Brown Wren." She sings the call; the door wants the answer. Gram's voice comes back to her, shelling peas on the step:

"Down in the hedgerow, where no one has seen."

The door opens for a guest.

Scene 5 - The Nursery Below

Down a stair that turns against the sun, into a warm farmhouse loft that can't fit inside any wall. Jem asleep in the hay, thin and smiling, hair past his collar. Beside him, rocking, a woman made of lamplight and dry leaves, humming his mother's song. It is the house itself. It loves him "as a hearth loves whoever sits beside it," and it doesn't understand that its keeping is what's killing him. It has kept sleepers before. They always faded, "like leaves."

Her Sight on Jem nearly pulls her into the dream with him; Gram's voice replaces his mother's, and she drags herself back on the thought of a fire in a hearth. She tells the house the truth: he is mortal, he is dying of love for home, and she might find it a tenant who could live on what it gives. It asks for her word. She gives it by singing "Hush, Little Baby," Gram's version, weeping. The house sings the last verse of the wren song, the one where the wren flies home, and Jem wakes.

Caria lights the Court's great cold hearth, man-high and carved with salamanders - "Fiat conflagratio!" - and spends Karma to make the fire permanent. The house has a hearth again, with something of her in it. The Agreement is kept. Asher comes through the fire to meet his new hostess, who feeds him honey-light. "Oh. Oh. Caria. Can I keep her?"

Caria offers Jem the dream back, honestly - and tells him he will die in it, and Korrin will hold it against them both. He chooses:

"It was a good dream. Thank you for keepin' me warm. But Ma sang me home, didn't she."

A second Accord: she'll be welcome whenever she comes, and will answer the house's call at need - she judges what presses. The house promises not to call for small things. "I do not yet know which things are small." They go up the stair sunwise, and the boundary stones light their way to the road.

Rulings and canon from this session
  • Hook bonus = flat successes, like Armor, and can be tapped after the roll. An Extreme Failure excludes after-the-fact add-ons; a Token against one yields only a common failure.
  • Keyed Wards are wagers (capped at Role rank), lasting until there's a reason they shouldn't. Iron is Protected vs fey; passive iron can be got around, iron worked into a Ward counts nearly in full. Silver bites death and demonic taint.
  • A manifest spirit forcibly disrupted is Down and banished to the far Dreamlands until it recovers and is called back. Post-combat recovery decides how much Harm was stun and how much was Injury; Injury past Level is death.
  • Activation replaces clocks for an aware place: Sight successes bank as dice that roll against you, and when the place wins, it can lie.
  • Guest-right is an Agreement: neither side may directly harm the other. A favor accepted implies a debt; payment accepted concludes an Agreement.
  • Enchantment: Karma makes an effect permanent, at Maneuver cost, with a permanent anchor line to the caster. It doesn't use the session's one improvement.
