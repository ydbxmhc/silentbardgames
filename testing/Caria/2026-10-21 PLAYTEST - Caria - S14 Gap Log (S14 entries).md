## Opposition Extremes on small pools (S14, Day 14 night)
- Lee: "Again the gods hinder the opposition." The Cold's lean (3d), Cuthred's cancel (3d, 0 0 0) and others: small opposition pools roll Extremes often. On 3d, all-zero and all-one are each 1 in 8, so an Extreme of SOME kind turns up 1 time in 4.
- @@* Worth a line in extreme-results.md or the GM crib: a 3-die opposition will Extreme-fail about once in eight rolls and hand the player a side effect; GMs who want quieter opposition can roll 2d (no Extremes) or 4d+ (rarer, 1 in 16 each way). Not a problem as such -- it's honest dice -- but GMs should know the rate when choosing a rating.

## Spirit recovery (S14, Lee 2026-10-29)
- Lee: a Down spirit "still gets recovery rolls same as anyone": it rolls against its CURRENT Harm (recovery.md), so one hit hard may need an EXTREME to recover at all; it creeps back sooner or later, not always 1/day. (GM had first proposed an automatic 1/dawn -- retired.)
- Played: the Cold's local presence, Level 4 vs Harm 9 -> 4d vs 9d each dawn; only an Extreme (4 of 4, 1 in 16) is a likely way back. -> conjuror.md, near "merely incapacitated until they can recover": one line pointing to recovery.md.

## Searches are attrition (S14, Lee 2026-10-29)
- A spirit hunting one man through a city was first run as a one-shot roll. Lee: make it full ATTRITION -- progress accumulates against the opposition's Level; a lost exchange Harms the searcher by the margin (a Complication at -1 EL), and he can keep searching. Same model as the S12 damaged-text reading. -> GM guide: "a search is a fight with the haystack."

## Dawn: an Extreme on the AGE dice (S14 Day 17)
- The Bend Fax (8) vs its age dice 4d rolled 0 0 0 0 -- an Extreme failure for the opposition. The Dawn rule covers Extremes on the CASTING's own roll (success resets age; failure snuffs) but not on the age dice. Played: no effect at first. RULED [Lee]: an Extreme failure on the AGE dice works like an Extreme success on the casting's own roll -- the casting's AGE RESETS to 1. -> spellcraft.md beside the Dawn rule.

## Extreme side effects: the confidence Boost (S14, Lee 2026-10-29) -- STANDARD EXAMPLE
- GM first gave an opposition's Extreme failure the side effect "time for another roll" (an extra copy). Lee: that isn't a boon when rolls go badly -- more rolls, more chances to fail. His practice: give the good roll a SECOND effect -- it counts for its usual purpose AND as a CONFIDENCE BOOST on the next roll (dice scaled to the roll: 3 dice -> Boost 3). It helps or it doesn't; it raises the odds of the next roll going well and, with a bigger pool, lowers the odds of another crit. -> extreme-results.md: list it as a standard example side effect.
