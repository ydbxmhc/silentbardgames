# CAST - Voices of Saltwind and the Road North (through S08)

How the Caria playtest NPCs talk, carry themselves, and what they want. Companion to the Saltwind Gazetteer, which covers who they are, where they live and what happened. Player-safe: no secrets here. Most voices began as GM inventions in play (@@*); Lee trues them up as he likes. Quoted lines are from play.

Each entry: **voice** (how they sound) / **manner** (what they do with their hands and eyes) / **wants** / **lines**.

Updated 2026-10-09 with S08: new lines for the Saltwind folk Cari said goodbye to, Thimble the brownie, Tibby at Tanner's Fords, and a much fuller Jem.

---

## Saltwind

**Mira Thousand-Voices**
- Voice: soft, unhurried, plain words. She gets quieter rather than louder when something matters.
- Manner: sits at your level, barefoot. She lets you come to the telling in your own time, and her net-mender's hands are always busy with something.
- Wants: every prayer delivered, and every debt kept honest. She wants Min safe, and she wants to see a thing with her own eyes before trusting it.
- Lines: "Thousand-Voices is what they call me when they're being polite. Mira will do." / "And the tide bring you home."

**Min**
- Voice: goblin pidgin, all nouns and verbs ("Min give, Min get soup?"). His deep, perfect Celestial comes out only when he quotes, or when he forgets to hide it.
- Manner: wary of doors, eyes and big houses ("Many eyes"). Haggles everything under the Accords and lines his spoon up square when he's done. Waits to eat until the cook has.
- Wants: fair trades, a full belly, a place that stays his.
- Lines: "That is not porridge." / "Pot keep the count. Pot never cheat." / "Goblin-place need pot." / "Min say go Mira. Jory say no wake Mira. Min say SHUT MOUTH." / "Min not on list. Min here. Min get love direct." / "Min eat soup for Sister. Every night."
- Also: about three feet tall (an adolescent goblin) [Lee]. Greets under the Accords with "met as paid," two fingers laid on an open palm. Takes any promise as an Agreement, gravely, with both hands.

**Shimmer** (undine)
- Voice: rarely words at all. A chill, a drip, prismatic spray. When she does speak, it's brief and a little sly.
- Manner: shy; lives in the rain-barrel; trades in secrets and gossip, never gold.
- Wants: secrets, romance, gossip. A soft spot for Mirna's house.
- Lines: "When the widow asks. Not the girl. Not the spark."

**Karinna**
- Voice: an old coast woman: dry, fair and unafraid. Short sentences with the bark left on.
- Manner: knows you by your step and smell; won't admit her knees hurt; lets folk think Nell's drawing water is "training."
- Wants: true water, and neighbors who behave like neighbors.
- Lines: "Get up off my cobbles. I'm too old to watch young folk kneel in fish-scale." / "You're no spy of that mountain. You're something it made and couldn't keep." / "Round here we call that being neighbors." / "Who's been slapping you, girl? ...Ah. Then you've been blessed." / "When you're not sure what you are, wash your face in it. She'll tell you."
- Also: "looks" at you by touch, fingers up your arm to your face. Wakes before the gulls and draws her own first bucket.

**Nell** (about ten)
- Voice: chatter, breathless news, scandalized gasps.
- Manner: stares at Cari's white lock; falls asleep at feasts; gives eggs nobody needs.
- Lines: "EVERYONE is talking about her."

**Mirna Hale**
- Voice: gruff, few words. "Hm." means yes, and probably means good.
- Manner: pinches cheeks, pats them hard enough to count as a slap, guards her shelf ("Not even the cat"), scours a dead man's pot every spring for twenty years.
- Wants: nobody kept waiting, the lamps lit, and nobody's roof afire. She wants Garth's pot full.
- Lines: "Too thin, the lot of you from that mountain." / "Bowls at full dark. Anybody can come. Nobody asks who put in what." / (to the sea) "You've had enough of mine. You can give me this." / "Pot's scraped. Folk come back to a scraped pot." / "Sand's never lain that tidy for me in forty years." / "She wakes before the gulls. Always has."
- Also: will not look impressed, and will not stop working to watch. Says goodbye with deeds (an oatcake thrown at your back), never words. The cook eats when the pot's scraped.

**Harl Pennock**
- Voice: slow, salted, sparing, with a dry kick at the end. Calls Cari "lass."
- Manner: combs his whiskers; keeps the key on its nail; offers "air" with a tilt of the head. Says the hard true thing, and then the kind one.
- Wants: the ambergris quiet and the village fed. An apology he never got to make.
- Lines: "She had it coming, mind. Doesn't make it yours to give." / "Forty-one years I've climbed that hill. Never once seen her take a thing inland." / "Go on, then, lass."
- Farewell gesture: two fingers touched to his brow, from the watch post.

**Davey**
- Voice: young, eager, a little out of breath.
- Manner: runs the news uphill before anyone asks.

**Old Tobin**
- Voice: never stops talking, even when he's digging a grave.
- Manner: kindly and practical; offers the speaker the first handful of earth.

**Bett Pellow**
- Voice: warm and blunt, a fishwife's practical courage.
- Manner: braces for the bill; learns fast; her word carries at the lodge.
- Wants: her boys whole; debts paid by feeding people.
- Lines: "He can't breathe." / "You're not that." / "Don't be away long, or I'll send Jory after you, sling and all."

**Hal Pellow**
- Voice: easy, loud "aye," a teller of stories.
- Manner: a two-fingered hand he doesn't hide; small beer for guests.
- Lines: "Hal's all anyone's ever called me. Even the gulls." / "Bread's nothing. The boy's arm is something."

**Jory Pellow** (16)
- Voice: proud, quick, trying to sound older than he is.
- Manner: hides a hitched breath; goes still when touched kindly; counts breaths for other people.
- Wants: to haul before the herring's gone. A share in the giving. Olwin hurt (banked, not gone).
- Lines: "Fix my arm so I can haul before the herring's gone, and we're square." / "Yes, Sister." (caught out, low) / "I promise. I'll be hauling before the herring's thin. And I'll tell 'em. Every one."
- Also: when he's broken her rules, shame burns hotter in him than the pain.

**Annet Fallow**
- Voice: tired, plain, surprised into laughing.
- Manner: wraps her own thumb in oilcloth; answers Hesta in front of the gutting tables.
- Lines: "Like telling them it won't hurt." / "Folk who leave something growing always come back to see if it took."

**Lissa and Wat Fallow**
- Lissa (about four): hoarse, bossy once she's better. "I'll tell her to eat her greens."
- Wat (about six): solemn, asks the frightening question out loud. "Is Lissa going to die like Da?"

**Cobb**
- Voice: very little. Denial first, then a name will open him. Once he's open: plain, few words, a little old-fashioned ("ye") @@*. His laugh is a rusty bark he hasn't used in years.
- Manner: tall and stooped, in his late wife Maud's knitted cap, which he takes off a moment when something is grave. Mends nets in the warehouse lee by day and by lamplight past midnight. Learns rules like knots, by saying them back. Seals a bargain wrist-to-wrist once he's shown how.
- Wants: Maud's bees kept after he's gone. Never to shame the people who stood up for him.
- Lines: "That was Lissa Fallow's anyway. Midwinter's come early." / "Generations. There's only me." / "So it pays itself. From the bees." / "Can't lie. Can talk round, though. Can't they?" / "Take a thing, you owe. Pay proper, it's square. Break it, and the other's loose." / "I'll not shame ye. Nor her." / "Ye'll sleep now." / "Go careful north, Sister."

**Thimble** (a brownie, Cobb's)
- Voice: low, rustic, a little formal ("Tied my hands with his, ye have"). Understands Celestial and answers in its own country speech. Cannot lie, and says so.
- Manner: knee-high, with shaggy brown hair to the ankles, a face like a winter apple, long clever hands, and no clothes. Shows its eyes and hands from the shadow under the oak's roots, and never the rest. Wary of robes. A slow, wide grin at leave to frighten thieves.
- Wants: a hearth that's kept warm ("a cold one's no home"), honest wages, work to do, and manners from the fire-folk.
- Lines: "Thimble'll do. It's not my name. It'll answer." / "Treat, then. What's the house, and what's the wage?" / "Soup's honest." / "I'll mind my manners if he minds his."

**Hesta Rook**
- Voice: sharp, carrying, sure of herself, the gossip's "I'm only saying."
- Manner: grey braids, rigid back; goes house to house; would rather go hungry than eat from a trough.
- Wants: to be right. The world to answer for what it took.
- Lines: "My man's never ate from a trough." / "He's not here for any pot of yours--"

**Abel Rook**
- Voice: big and quiet. Says one thing, and it lands.
- Lines: "I am, though, Hes." / "Sit. Have a bowl. It's good."

**Sal Doughty** - Hesta's ears: watchful, eager, sees the ember.
**Ellen Brannock and the twins** - Ellen young and proud. Nan and Tod shriek "It's chicken!" and make Tam tell the goat story again.

## The road north

**Dasha Korrin**
- Voice: hard bargainer, fair, short sentences, no ornament.
- Manner: grey braid, whip on her shoulder; never spends a nail on a wagon board.
- Wants: cargo delivered, terms kept, her people fed.
- Lines: "Light at night, eyes on the dark." / "Half my drovers are already up there." / "You break him, you've bought him." / "That's mine. ...Eat it, then. The both of you." / "Language, Initiate." / "Mine balance." / "Very pretty. Sit down." / "Next time you mean to light a forge, warn me first."
- Also: talks from the wagon box without turning round. Laughs seldom, and it's worth something when she does. Jem's self-appointed guardian [Lee]: she sets the terms for his lessons and asked Cari to help with his nightmares since the Court. Notices more than she asks; when she does ask, it's sharp ("You never ask. What did you see up there?").

**Jem** (14, Cari's apprentice since S08)
- Voice: drover-plain, hungry for everything; asks straight out. Calls Cari "Mes." He's learning her first rule (ask when you want to know; she'll say if you must wait) and sometimes applies it to himself.
- Manner: hair hacked short; a drover's coat two sizes too big; saw the sea for the first time and won't stop looking at it. Braids like he does the mules' manes for the Ganth fair. Seals deals by spitting in his palm. Counts her fingers, pointedly. Can't sit empty, so he fills a silence with work. Reads being watched in silence as trouble coming. Keeps the oak leaf from the Court and the river's quartz-ringed stone inside his shirt. Knows his name "to look at" and tally marks, little else (carved his name in the Crossroads inn's loft rafters [Lee]). His fingertips go warm near fire magic, and nobody knows why yet.
- Wants: to learn the river before he goes in. To hang the lights himself some nights so Cari can sleep. To tell the House he isn't angry.
- Lines: "Ma always said she'd take me." / "She didn't mean it. ...That's the bit that's dangerous. Isn't it." / "Has it been? Worth it. For you." / "So you learn the river first. Before you go in." / "...Like before. Mes, it's like before." / "I'm still here." / "That's just me too. Isn't it." / "Then I'll not choose. Not yet. Not till I know what I'm choosing."

**Brother Anselm Kade and Hollis**
- Voice: dry, level, with almost no change of expression. His jokes arrive deadpan.
- Manner: watches from walls; says the professional thing first and the human thing second.
- Lines: "Gossip you fight, you feed." / "Leave them so they don't need you." / "Then the vapors are going around." / "Six. Two days of one Sister and I'm not sure I have the constitution for more." / "Since when?" / "I heard that. ...So did he. He hasn't moved." / "He snores."
- Also: NO dialect, ever; dry and level. Keeps late watch with a spare cup by the coals. Opens his blanket without a word, lays his cloak over a woman and a dog without comment, and makes no promises about his report.
- Hollis: a spirit-hound with serene loyalty. Salt stings his tongue; he likes bread; he stops to *show* you things. Through a hag-stone, a great pale hound of quiet light, older than the dog, looking back. He growled once at Cari's flare, then chose her and leaned on her.

**Clerk Fennick**
- Voice: precise, careful, faintly anxious. Speaks in records and copies.
- Manner: hands flat on the table; writes everything; enters hard truths "in a fair hand." Ink-fingered at his writing box by the fire; returns a greeting with a small, real smile: "Sister."

**Tam** (wagon two)
- Voice: big and easy, unbothered.
- Manner: offers an arm on the steep part (his mam taught him); smells of goat.
- Lines: "Seen stranger on the road. He's not got a goat, has he?" / "Hungry I know. Road teaches you that, or it buries you." / "Smells better than my goat!" / "Smelled your lamp from my wagon. Thought it was my goat." / "You all right, Sister?"
- Also: picks out the best seasoned oak without asking what it's for, and carries it for you. Doesn't wait for more than a nod.

**Asher** (ember sprite, Cari's)
- Voice: crackle and spark; proud, touchy, devoted.
- Manner: sulks when forgotten, forgives fast ("I keep the fire"); evens a peat bed perfectly for a widow who invited him.
- Wants: Cari's attention, offerings, a hearth that's welcome.
- Also: calls her "Child of the Contract." Needs a flame, or metal that is hers, to reach through, and is nervous beside running water. Very proud of his rounds (Mirna's fire, Cobb's hearth, Mira's coals, the Court). Tasted the sea for the first time in Harl's ambergris.
- Lines: "Civil as a candle." / "Is this the one? He is very thin. Does he have offerings?" / "A HEARTBEAT? I am a *hearth*." / "Received. I am yours, as I was. More." / "Caria. I saw. You are still Caria. I keep the fire -- I know what is yours and what is not." / "He would make a good one."

## Tanner's Fords

**Tibby** (about six, a thorpe child)
- Voice: brave and serious, then giggles behind both hands at a rude word.
- Manner: bare feet; creeps down the bank to watch; appoints herself foreman of any job going; holds stock-still with her eyes screwed shut while her face is washed.
- Lines: "...What's it say?" / "Mam says don't go out past the black posts. That's where it's deep." / "I'm Tibby." / "Thank you, Mes Cari!" (to Jem, grudgingly: "...you too")

**The river**
- Voice: none. It answers in stillness, a little less cold, and a stone rolled to your hand. Busy being a river.
