Session 2 - The Empty Bowl

Level One solo playtest, Averond - Caria Greenhaven, Initiate of the Monastery of Strife. GM: Lilita. Played 2026-09-27/28.

At a glance

One day on the road and one night in Crossroads. Caria leaves the Monastery a robe everyone distrusts, and rolls out of Crossroads next morning as "one of Aubric's" - having fed five neglected gods, kept a widow's oven with a fire-sprite, been hunted in her dreams by something wearing a dead friend's voice, and sworn a public oath over an empty bowl.

Ends as Korrin's wagons roll south, and Clerk Fennick takes out his pen.

Scene 1 - The Cell, Before Dawn

Morning prayers, with Asher as the altar light. She recasts Fax Iacta onto her staff as a lasting, steerable light - the hold wobbles, and she spends a Token and insists. Asher accepts her invitation to ride in the flame: "Both. So neither." The blue-green light carries a gold heart from then on.

Scene 2 - The North Road

Dasha Korrin - grey braid, whip on her shoulder, no love for the Monastery's road tax - takes her on: a free seat on wagon 1 and meals with the drovers, in exchange for her light at night camps and a spirit-watch past the old fey ruin beyond the fork. Looking at wagon 4, Caria promises to set any thief among the goods aflame; Fennick hears it and writes it down.

She relays Kade's warning. Korrin tells her about Jem, fourteen, a drover's boy who heard his dead mother calling from the trees near the ruin last spring and was never seen again. Kade says the timing is wrong for his quarry - something else on this road calls with borrowed voices. At the ruin, he advises, don't open the Eye.

Scene 3 - Crossroads: the Forge and the Shrines

Garrow Hask, the smith, devotee of Carterion, sells her amber, brimstone and applewood charcoal. His forge holds an old, grumpy fire spirit - Old Bellows - to whom Caria offers a favor with no debt attached. His wish: a live coal of his fire carried to the sea, so he can say he has touched it. She carries it now in a lidded fire-pot.

The shrine cluster is neglected, the circuit priest two months gone, Rikitak's horns scratched away - and to her spirit-sense the crossroads runs thin and fast, like a river too low. She cleans every bowl and feeds every god, and sets a fuelless blue flame burning in Rikitak's (with a second Token, when the hold fails).

Scene 4 - The Inn Kitchen

Berra - Mags's sister-in-law - lets her in at "Mags sent me," and quietly keeps Fennick from finding her. Caria sheds the robes, scrubs pots, and earns Berra's leave (a third Token, earned trust rather than glamour) for Asher to keep her fires. He holds the bread oven at a perfect heat all night.

They talk about Brother Aubric - the circuit healer who set Berra's burned hand twenty years ago, whose only price was "feed whoever comes to your back door hungry." Caria knew him at the Monastery: he was the late Hospitaller. She teaches the pot-boy Dunny why you never ask a priest of the Order for help: rules become habits, and habits hold when fear comes. Dunny tells her Jem slept in this loft the night before the fork - and dreamed of his mother singing.

Scene 5 - The Loft

No ward - only her silvered hairpin, and a prayer. In the night something reaches into her dream in Aubric's voice - "Hush. Don't ask. Just let me." - and the dream catches blue-white fire. For a heartbeat she sees it: long, grey, hollow-faced, too many finger-joints, crouched on a dry fountain in a roofless hall with trees growing through the floor. It recoils, burned, and is gone.

Scene 6 - Dawn in the Square

Berra slept through the night for the first time in twenty years. She gives oil, herbs and bread, and agrees to tell the lane the truth: a pilgrim-priest's blessing, not a message. At the well, Rook - who scratched the horns in anger at the tithe cart - demands to know what the Monastery wants with their gods.

"I damned well intend to find out."

She re-tends every bowl with a prayer, then puts out Rikitak's flame and leaves his bowl empty: an oath to find out why his shrine went untended and his healers extort. Rook confesses and swears to keep the shrines until a proper priest comes. On the way out she presses a silver into Berra's hand - "Hush." - to be ground and scattered in the loft.

"I have much work to do!"

Rulings and canon from this session
  • Luck Tokens work after the dice fall, as long as the result isn't resolved. A Token-bought top result is provisionally not an Extreme Success (uncommitted).
  • Persistent lasts until there's a reason it shouldn't; brightness is adjustable.
  • Etiquette of the Order of Rikitak: never ask a priest for help - tell him someone is hurt or sick. He comes on his own, and never asks a price you cannot pay. Aid requested carries a price; fire teaches.
  • Apology Karma: no Hook trigger in a session pays one Karma (already in karma.md).
  • A Hook used by choice for bonus dice isn't a trigger; if its bill comes due later and derails plans, that pays then (proposed).
