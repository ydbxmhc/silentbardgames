# GM Technique: The Bare Oracle (from S06 play)

Status: candidate for gm-rules.md. Distilled from how the oracle was actually used in the Caria playtest, S06. No spoilers.

## The roll

Bare 2d, no Level, no modifiers.

- **2 = Yes.**
- **1 = A sign.** Yes-but, partly, half-done, or a clue instead of an answer.
- **0 = No.**

Odds: 25% yes, 50% sign, 25% no. The middle result is the most common, and that's a feature. Most of the time the oracle says "sort of," and the GM has to interpret. That interpretation is where the story comes from.

## When to use it

Use it when the fiction touches a fact the GM hasn't decided and **nobody is opposing anything**. Opposed actions roll pools. The oracle is for the world's state, not for anyone's effort.

## The question families (all used in S06)

| Family | Shape | S06 example |
|---|---|---|
| NPC initiative | "Does X notice / offer / act?" | Does Tam notice her leaving and offer to walk her up? |
| World state | "Has the condition changed?" | Has Lissa's stridor at rest come back tonight? |
| Quality | "Is it actually good?" | Is Min's barley good? (1: scorched, but rescued) |
| Rumor / knowledge | "Has X heard?" | Has Tam heard Hesta's tale? |
| Offscreen past | "Did X happen while we weren't looking?" | Did Jory get honey from Cobb? |
| Resources | "Does X have it to spare?" | Does Mira have a softening salve? |
| Witnesses | "Did anyone see?" | Did anyone outside see the red through Harl's window? |
| Follow-through | "Did someone else already handle it?" | Had anyone moved the livers somewhere cold? |
| Hidden truth | (rolled privately, revealed by play) | Is Harl's lump real ambergris? |

## The disciplines

1. **Ask before you decide.** If you already know the answer, don't roll for it. Rolling after deciding is theater.
2. **Ask neutral questions, and only ones where both answers are playable.** If "no" kills the scene, it isn't an oracle question. Set the stakes another way.
3. **Read the 1 as the most interesting partial truth that fits what's established.** For example, scorched and over-salted, but with stolen sea fennel in it that makes it good.
4. **The oracle sets a fact. The players decide what the fact means.** Hesta seeing the red was one die result, and it became a rumor arc. The roll didn't decide that. The PCs' response to it did.
5. **Never oracle a PC's own choices or feelings.** Those belong to the player.
6. **Follow-through rule.** The "did someone already?" question is asked only when a PC actually goes to check. The roll decides what she finds; her choice to look is what saves it. If nobody checks, time decides. This rewards attention without punishing players for not micromanaging chores, and it lets NPCs be busy, tired, and human.
7. **Log every answer as canon.** Show the dice to the player unless the truth is meant to be found in play. Hidden rolls get logged too, privately.

## Why it fits L1

It's the same d2 economy as every other roll, with nothing new to learn. It respects "everything rolls": the world gets a vote too, instead of the GM silently picking. It also keeps the GM honest. Surprises the GM didn't plan are the best evidence that nobody's railroading.
