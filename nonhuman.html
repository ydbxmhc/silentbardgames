Nonhuman Characters

Fantasy settings are rife with the promise of monsters great and small, but not everything that isn't human is a monster. Defining these things in Level One is just a matter of differentiating them from humans, and we'll assume the baseline of your game is human.

Conceits

A Conceit is an unrated capital-T Truth about a character or something in it. When these things are really important, the only accurate way to say "this is true" is to say it is. A demon might be Immune to Fire. A ghost might be Incorporeal. A powerful warrior might be Feared Throughout the Kingdom.

Some things cannot be measured. When those things are really important, the only accurate way to say "this is true" is to say it is. A demon might be Immune to Fire, and that means exactly what it says: fire is simply not relevant to them as a threat or damage source. The only accurate response to "I breathe fire on the demon" is "it doesn't affect it at all."

Conceits are incredibly powerful. Don't abuse them. Still, games are about fun.

Hodges' Rule of Thumb

Do not assume reasonable behavior from anything with an opposable thumb.

To make a nonhuman, all you really need is a description. Make it a goblin warrior? Discuss with the GM and the group and give it a short description for goblin culture, and run with it. That might be all that's needed.

Nonhuman As A Hook

This goblin character is going to be adventuring in human lands and will likely suffer significant prejudice. That alone is worth noting as a Hook of at least rank 1, maybe rank 2. It might be that protecting the neighborhood kids earns some good will, or maybe it earns contempt for that kind of social presumption. Guarda will deny them access to taverns; town guard will watch the group carefully. The upside is the PC can add the value of the Hook to any resistance when dealing with attempts to exploit the Hook itself — and the stronger the prejudice, the more the character can use it!

A common trope in fantasy is that some peoples have an easier or harder time with certain skills than others. This is built into the Hook mechanic already. If you want to give them that sort of bonus, let the character develop a bloodline- or culture-relevant Maneuver, but make them pay Maneuver slots for it. Just not their very first free one.

Another common trope is the inborn magical effect. Treat this as a Role or Maneuver that's widely common among their people. The only thing they get for free is at least one rank of Adept (Pixie Magic) or Spellweaver which they'd also have to buy. The only thing they get for free is one rank of the relevant Role. They still pay for Maneuvers.

A Pixie with an Entangle spell (a rank of Adept/Pixie Magic) is perfectly reasonable, but they still pay for it and for having a spell that they'd also have to pay for for a rank of Spellweaver. The only thing they get for free is their Hook. If you want to give them something more interesting than just the Race Hook bonus, let the character develop a bloodline- or culture-relevant Maneuver, but they pay Maneuver slots for it, never for free (except the one they'd have with a Hook anyway).
